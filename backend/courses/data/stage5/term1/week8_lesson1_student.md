# Week 8 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Present a 5-minute structured formal presentation to a panel of 2 (you + invited assessor) following the required structure: Problem -> Solution -> Live Demo -> Evidence -> Reflection
- Demonstrate a working Arduino prototype live during the presentation: the prototype must respond to input and produce correct output without you assistance
- Read a written introduction and conclusion from prepared notes (demonstrating formal written-to-spoken English)
- Answer at least 2 questions from the panel with reasoned, evidence-based responses

## Big idea
A project is not finished until it has been explained to someone else. Presentation is the final act of engineering.

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Formal oral presentation with structured argument: you read a written introduction from notes (full sentences, formal register). The 5-part structure – Problem, Solution, Live Demo, Evidence, Reflection – mirrors the structure of formal reports and scientific papers. Panel Q&A develops the ability to respond to unexpected questions with reasoned, evidence-based language. This is the most advanced spoken English task of the programme and maps directly to secondary school presentation and debate skills.
- **Maths:** Quantifying project outcomes: every learner must present at least one number – a measurement, a calculation, or a data point – from their testing phase. The number must appear in a graph or table displayed during the presentation. You and assessor independently score the presentation; if scores differ by more than 10%, they moderate. This models the statistical concept of inter-rater reliability used in formal assessment.

## Let's begin

### PREPARATION – 30 Minutes Before Presentations Begin  (30 min)
1. Display at least one measurement, calculation, or data point from testing (graph or table shown during presentation)
2. Complete a peer feedback rubric for a classmate's presentation using the 5-question structured form
3. Submit a final written design brief (all 7 sections) and annotated sketch for you and assessor review
4. All learner project prototypes (pre-tested by you before session starts)
5. All learner written design briefs (you returns with written comments from Week 7)
6. Printed Demo Day assessment rubric (A4 for you) – 5 criteria, 4-point scale (from DDD Part 8.6)
7. Printed peer feedback rubric (A4 for you) – 5 questions, space for written comments
8. Printed panel score sheet (A4, one per assessor) – same 5 criteria as learner rubric
9. AI Engineer Certificate templates (pre-prepared, unsigned)

### PRESENTATIONS – 5 Minutes Per Learner + 3 Minutes Q&A  (90 min)
10. Participation Acknowledgement certificates (for any learner not yet at pass threshold)
11. Score calculation worksheet (A4 per you) – for real-time weighted score calculation during the session
12. Camera / phone for session photos (parental consent already obtained at enrolment)
13. Refreshments if appropriate (parent/guardian attendance encouraged)
14. You sets up the presentation space: one table at the front for the panel (you + invited assessor), chairs facing forward, a clear space for the presenter to stand and demonstrate.
15. You sets up their prototype at their workspace and does a final test: upload the sketch, confirm it works, leave it running. If any project does not work at this point, you provides a 5-minute targeted intervention. If still not working, learner presents the design brief and code only – this is noted on the rubric but does not disqualify.
16. You briefs the panel assessor: explain the 5 rubric criteria and the 4-point scale. Confirm: scores are independent, moderation applies if scores differ by >10%.
17. Remind you of the presentation order (announced in advance). Remind them: panel questions come after the 5-minute presentation, not during.
