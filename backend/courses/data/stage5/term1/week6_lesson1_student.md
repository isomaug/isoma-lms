# Week 6 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Define artificial intelligence, pattern recognition, training data, classification, and prediction in your own words
- Sort 20 picture cards into categories by applying a set of classification rules and compare the process to what an AI does
- Read a provided article about one real-world AI application and write a summary of at least 150 words including: what the AI does, how it was trained, one benefit, and one concern

## Big idea
AI does not think. It finds patterns in data and follows rules – the same patterns and rules you are already writing in Arduino. The difference is scale.

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Reading comprehension of a non-fiction AI article: you read independently, then write a structured summary of minimum 150 words. The summary must contain all 4 required elements. This mirrors the non-fiction reading comprehension skills developed in P5–P6 English. Oral contribution: you is expected to make at least one reasoned spoken contribution to yourself discussion. "I think AI is fair/unfair because…" contributions are logged by the you as participation evidence.
- **Maths:** Classification activity mathematics: you sort 20 picture cards into 5 categories. After sorting, calculate: how many cards are in Category A? Probability that a randomly chosen card belongs to Category A = number in Category A ÷ 20. Express as a fraction (e.g. 6/20 = 3/10) and as a percentage (30%). Extend: if 3 rules apply simultaneously, how many cards satisfy all 3 rules? This introduces compound classification – the mathematical foundation of AI filtering.

## Let's begin

### HOOK – You Are Already Doing AI  (20 min)
1. Connect their LDR sensor project from Week 5 to AI concepts: explain how the sensor data could train a simple rule-based AI model
2. Participate meaningfully in a class discussion about AI fairness and make a reasoned contribution documented by the you
3. Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen picture card belongs to Category A, expressing it as a fraction and a percentage
4. 4 laptops (for research reference if internet available, or printed article if not)
5. Printed AI article (A4, one for you) – you selects an age-appropriate article about AI in daily life (e.g. phone face unlock, spam filter, autocomplete); Isoma Edutech provides this
6. 20 picture sort cards for you (printed A5, cut) – mix of 5 categories: animals, vehicles, food, tools, buildings
7. Printed AI summary template (A4 for you) – 4 labelled sections: What it does | How it was trained | One benefit | One concern
8. Classification rule card (A5 for you) – 3 sorting rules to apply simultaneously to the picture cards

### GUIDED ACTIVITY – Read, Classify, Connect  (60 min)
9. Printed discussion log sheet (A4 per you) – for recording meaningful contributions for you
10. A computer screen to follow along
11. USB flash drives
12. No introduction – open with an action. Give each pair the 20 picture cards face-down. "Turn them over. Sort them into groups. You have 90 seconds. Go."
13. After sorting, ask: "What rule did you use? Could someone else follow your rule and get the same result?" Take 3 different responses.
14. Reveal: "What you just did – classifying items by finding patterns and applying rules – is exactly what a machine learning AI does. The difference is the AI does it millions of times per second, on millions of items."
15. Show examples on projected screen: phone face unlock ("it learned your face from 1000 photos"), spam filter ("it learned what spam looks like from millions of emails"), autocomplete ("it learned what words follow other words from billions of sentences"). Ask: "What is the pattern in all of these?" (They all learned from examples.)
16. Get ready to use the AI article. You read silently for 5 minutes before discussion begins.
