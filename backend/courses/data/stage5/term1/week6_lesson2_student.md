# Week 6 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Define artificial intelligence, pattern recognition, training data, classification, and prediction in your own words
- Sort 20 picture cards into categories by applying a set of classification rules and compare the process to what an AI does
- Read a provided article about one real-world AI application and write a summary of at least 150 words including: what the AI does, how it was trained, one benefit, and one concern

## Continue

### INDEPENDENT WORK – Summary Writing and Discussion Preparation  (50 min)
1. Article reading and annotation (15 min): you read the provided AI article. As they read, they underline: one sentence about what the AI does (blue), one sentence about training data (red), one sentence about a benefit or concern (green). You discuss: "Did you find all three?"
2. Classification rule challenge (15 min): give each pair yourselfification rule card with 3 simultaneous rules (e.g. Rule 1: must be living; Rule 2: must be able to move independently; Rule 3: must be found in Uganda). You re-sort all 20 cards applying all 3 rules at once. Count: how many cards satisfy all 3 rules? Calculate the probability as a fraction and percentage.
3. Connect to Week 5 sensor project (15 min): you poses the question: "Your LDR sensor from last week collected light data. Imagine you recorded 1000 readings under different light conditions and labelled each one: 'daytime', 'evening', or 'night'. What would an AI do with that data?" Pair discussion. Share responses.
4. Introduce the 4 vocabulary terms formally: pattern recognition, training data, classification, prediction. You write your own definition for each in the margin of the article – not copied, in your own words.
5. Rotate your own pacing. Begin AI summary writing on the summary template.
6. AI article summary: you write independently on the summary template. Minimum 150 words total. Must include all 4 sections: what the AI does, how it was trained, one benefit, one concern. No copying from the article – paraphrase and explain.
7. Pair self-check: swap templates. Partner checks: are all 4 sections present? Is the word count approximately met? Is there any copied sentence (if so, circle it and ask them to rewrite it in your own words).
8. Discussion preparation: you write two discussion contribution sentences on a sticky note – one they agree with, one they are unsure about: "I think AI is fair because…" and "One thing that worries me about AI is…"
9. Optional Arduino link: if the LDR is available, write a 10-line sketch that classifies the light reading: if lightValue > 600 print "Bright – daytime"; else if lightValue > 300 print "Medium – evening"; else print "Low – night". This is a rule-based AI classifier.

### SHOWCASE & DEBRIEF – Class Discussion  (30 min)
10. Check your work carefully, checks summary writing, notes discussion preparation quality on participation log.
11. Structured class discussion (15 min): you poses each question and gives every learner the opportunity to contribute. "Where do you see AI in Uganda today?" "Is AI fair? Can it make mistakes?" "Where could AI help in Uganda that it does not yet?" You logs meaningful contributions on the discussion log sheet.
12. 2–3 you share their summary conclusions aloud (read from their template). Class gives 1 Star (something strong in the summary) + 1 Wish (one improvement).
13. Remember the AI–Arduino connection: "The LDR classifier you wrote – if light > 600, print Bright – is a rule-based AI system. Real AI uses the same logic, but it learns the numbers (like 600) from data instead of us writing them by hand."
14. Remember English link: "Reading a non-fiction article and writing a structured summary is a skill you will use in every subject. Today we practised it with a computing article."
15. Remember Math link: "Probability, fractions, and percentages are how we measure how likely an AI is to make a correct classification. The maths you already know is inside every AI system."
16. Exit question (written sticky note): "Write one sentence: what is the difference between AI and a regular programme?" Collect. Target answer: AI learns from data; a regular programme follows rules written by a human.
17. Submit AI summary template via USB flash drive.

## Stay safe
Safeguarding Note: The AI article and discussion may raise questions about facial recognition, surveillance, or social media algorithms. Facilitate discussion in an age-appropriate, balanced way. Do not allow you to share personal opinions about named companies or individuals. Remind you: AI systems have been shown to make mistakes, particularly with people from underrepresented groups – this is a real ethical issue, not a conspiracy theory.
