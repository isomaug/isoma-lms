# Lesson 8: Demo Day – AI Engineer Showcase

**Duration:** 3 hours (Saturday) / Demo Day format – no holiday split (split across Week 8 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 1 · Week 8
**Age Range:** 10–12 years
**Group Size:** 8 learners presenting individually; partner supports during demo
**Hardware:** All project hardware from each learner's Week 7 prototype (Arduino Uno, USB cable, laptop, component kit). Facilitator to confirm all projects are charged and tested before the session begins.
**Bloom's:** L3–L6 (Applying through Creating – all levels demonstrated in presentation and live demo)
**DDD ILO:** ILO 3.4 – Design, build, test, iterate, and present an original Arduino project with written design brief, live demonstration, and 5-minute formal presentation to a panel
**NCDC:** Uganda NCDC P5–P6 – English Strand: Formal oral presentation, structured argument, reflection; Mathematics Strand: Quantifying outcomes with measurements, data, and calculations; ICT Strand: Demonstrating a working digital project
**Cambridge:** Cambridge Primary Computing 0845 – Stage 5: Presenting and explaining computing work; evaluating outcomes; reflecting on the design process

**Big Idea:** A project is not finished until it has been explained to someone else. Presentation is the final act of engineering.

## Learning Objectives

- Present a 5-minute structured formal presentation to a panel of 2 (facilitator + invited assessor) following the required structure: Problem → Solution → Live Demo → Evidence → Reflection
- Demonstrate a working Arduino prototype live during the presentation: the prototype must respond to input and produce correct output without facilitator assistance
- Read a written introduction and conclusion from prepared notes (demonstrating formal written-to-spoken English)
- Answer at least 2 questions from the panel with reasoned, evidence-based responses

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** Formal oral presentation with structured argument: each learner reads a written introduction from notes (full sentences, formal register). The 5-part structure – Problem, Solution, Live Demo, Evidence, Reflection – mirrors the structure of formal reports and scientific papers. Panel Q&A develops the ability to respond to unexpected questions with reasoned, evidence-based language. This is the most advanced spoken English task of the programme and maps directly to secondary school presentation and debate skills.

**Maths:** Quantifying project outcomes: every learner must present at least one number – a measurement, a calculation, or a data point – from their testing phase. The number must appear in a graph or table displayed during the presentation. Facilitator and assessor independently score the presentation; if scores differ by more than 10%, they moderate. This models the statistical concept of inter-rater reliability used in formal assessment.

## Lesson Structure

### PREPARATION – 30 Minutes Before Presentations Begin  (30 min)
1. Display at least one measurement, calculation, or data point from testing (graph or table shown during presentation)
2. Complete a peer feedback rubric for a classmate's presentation using the 5-question structured form
3. Submit a final written design brief (all 7 sections) and annotated sketch for facilitator and assessor review
4. All learner project prototypes (pre-tested by facilitator before session starts)
5. All learner written design briefs (facilitator returns with written comments from Week 7)
6. Printed Demo Day assessment rubric (A4 per learner) – 5 criteria, 4-point scale (from DDD Part 8.6)
7. Printed peer feedback rubric (A4 per learner) – 5 questions, space for written comments
8. Printed panel score sheet (A4, one per assessor) – same 5 criteria as learner rubric
9. AI Engineer Certificate templates (pre-prepared, unsigned)

### PRESENTATIONS – 5 Minutes Per Learner + 3 Minutes Q&A  (90 min)
10. Participation Acknowledgement certificates (for any learner not yet at pass threshold)
11. Score calculation worksheet (A4 per facilitator) – for real-time weighted score calculation during the session
12. Camera / phone for session photos (parental consent already obtained at enrolment)
13. Refreshments if appropriate (parent/guardian attendance encouraged)
14. Facilitator sets up the presentation space: one table at the front for the panel (facilitator + invited assessor), chairs facing forward, a clear space for the presenter to stand and demonstrate.
15. Each learner sets up their prototype at their workspace and does a final test: upload the sketch, confirm it works, leave it running. If any project does not work at this point, facilitator provides a 5-minute targeted intervention. If still not working, learner presents the design brief and code only – this is noted on the rubric but does not disqualify.
16. Facilitator briefs the panel assessor: explain the 5 rubric criteria and the 4-point scale. Confirm: scores are independent, moderation applies if scores differ by >10%.
17. Remind learners of the presentation order (announced in advance). Remind them: panel questions come after the 5-minute presentation, not during.
18. Learners review their written introduction and conclusion one final time.

### SCORING AND CERTIFICATES  (30 min)
19. Each learner presents in order. Facilitator times strictly: 5 minutes for presentation, 3 minutes for panel questions. Signal at 4 minutes and again at 5 minutes.
20. Presentation structure each learner must follow: (1) Problem – read written introduction from notes; name the real-world problem. (2) Solution – explain what the project does; describe the key coding concept used. (3) Live Demo – run the prototype live; explain what is happening as it runs. (4) Evidence – show the graph or data table; state the mean, range, or key calculation; explain what it shows. (5) Reflection – name one challenge faced and one thing that would be changed or improved next time.
21. Panel questions: facilitator asks at least 1 question; invited assessor asks at least 1 question. Learner answers both. Example questions: "Why did you choose this sensor?" "What would happen if the light level was much higher?" "How does the mathematics in your project connect to what you learn in class?"
22. Audience (other learners): during each presentation, audience members complete the peer feedback rubric for the presenter. Rubric must be completed with specific written comments, not just scores.
23. Facilitator and assessor independently complete the panel score sheet for each presenter immediately after their presentation. Do not wait until all presentations are finished.
24. After all presentations: facilitator and assessor compare scores. For any learner where scores differ by >10%, moderate immediately using the evidence (design brief, sketch, rubric).
25. Facilitator calculates each learner's weighted total score using the DDD Part 9 formula. Weights: Participation 10%, Weekly Artifacts 25%, Prototype Functionality 10%, Code Quality 10%, Integration 10%, Presentation 20%, Written Design Brief 10%, Peer Review 5%.
26. Grade boundaries (DDD Part 9.1): Distinction ≥85%; Merit ≥70%; Pass ≥55%; Near Pass 45–54% (resubmission offered); Not Yet <45% (Participation Acknowledgement issued).
27. Check all 3 minimum pass requirements (DDD Part 9.2): overall score ≥55%, Demo Day rubric score ≥2/4 on all 5 criteria (no zero criteria), at least 5 of 7 weekly artifacts submitted, minimum 6 of 8 sessions attended.

### CELEBRATION & CLOSE  (30 min)
28. Facilitators sign certificates for qualifying learners. Invited assessor co-signs if present. Programme Director signs if attending. Certificates are not awarded for attendance alone.
29. Any learner in the Near Pass band (45–54%): facilitator schedules a private conversation with the learner and parent/guardian. One resubmission of the Demo Day project is offered within 2 weeks.
30. Certificate ceremony: call each learner's name, announce their grade (privately to them, not publicly), and present their certificate. Whole class applauds each certificate. Do not read out scores publicly.
31. Facilitator gives whole-programme closing address: celebrate specific achievements visible across the 8 weeks. Name specific skills each learner demonstrated (prepared in advance from participation log).
32. Group photo opportunity (parental consent obtained at enrolment – confirm before taking photos). Learners may hold their prototypes and certificates.
33. Feedback forms for parents/guardians if attending: 3-question form on programme quality, facilitator communication, and likelihood to recommend.
34. Collect all completed peer feedback rubrics, panel score sheets, and score calculation worksheets. Archive with Assessment Records.
35. Remind learners: Stage 6 (if continuing) builds on everything learned in Stage 5. The AI Engineer Certificate is the entry requirement for Stage 6 or equivalent demonstrated competency.
