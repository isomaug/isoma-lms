# Week 8 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Present a 5-minute structured formal presentation to a panel of 2 (you + invited assessor) following the required structure: Problem -> Solution -> Live Demo -> Evidence -> Reflection
- Demonstrate a working Arduino prototype live during the presentation: the prototype must respond to input and produce correct output without you assistance
- Read a written introduction and conclusion from prepared notes (demonstrating formal written-to-spoken English)
- Answer at least 2 questions from the panel with reasoned, evidence-based responses

## Continue

### SCORING AND CERTIFICATES  (30 min)
1. You review their written introduction and conclusion one final time.
2. You presents in order. You times strictly: 5 minutes for presentation, 3 minutes for panel questions. Signal at 4 minutes and again at 5 minutes.
3. Presentation structure you must follow: (1) Problem – read written introduction from notes; name the real-world problem. (2) Solution – explain what the project does; describe the key coding concept used. (3) Live Demo – run the prototype live; explain what is happening as it runs. (4) Evidence – show the graph or data table; state the mean, range, or key calculation; explain what it shows. (5) Reflection – name one challenge faced and one thing that would be changed or improved next time.
4. Panel questions: you ask at least 1 question; invited assessor asks at least 1 question. Learner answers both. Example questions: "Why did you choose this sensor?" "What would happen if the light level was much higher?" "How does the mathematics in your project connect to what you learn in class?"
5. Audience (other you): during each presentation, audience members complete the peer feedback rubric for the presenter. Rubric must be completed with specific written comments, not just scores.
6. You and assessor independently complete the panel score sheet for each presenter immediately after their presentation. Do not wait until all presentations are finished.
7. After all presentations: you and assessor compare scores. For any learner where scores differ by >10%, moderate immediately using the evidence (design brief, sketch, rubric).
8. You calculates you's weighted total score using the DDD Part 9 formula. Weights: Participation 10%, Weekly Artifacts 25%, Prototype Functionality 10%, Code Quality 10%, Integration 10%, Presentation 20%, Written Design Brief 10%, Peer Review 5%.
9. Grade boundaries (DDD Part 9.1): Distinction ≥85%; Merit ≥70%; Pass ≥55%; Near Pass 45–54% (resubmission offered); Not Yet <45% (Participation Acknowledgement issued).

### CELEBRATION & CLOSE  (30 min)
10. Check all 3 minimum pass requirements (DDD Part 9.2): overall score ≥55%, Demo Day rubric score ≥2/4 on all 5 criteria (no zero criteria), at least 5 of 7 weekly artifacts submitted, minimum 6 of 8 sessions attended.
11. Yous sign certificates for qualifying you. Invited assessor co-signs if present. Programme Director signs if attending. Certificates are not awarded for attendance alone.
12. Any learner in the Near Pass band (45–54%): you schedules a private conversation with the learner and parent/guardian. One resubmission of the Demo Day project is offered within 2 weeks.
13. Certificate ceremony: call you's name, announce their grade (privately to them, not publicly), and present their certificate. Whole class applauds each certificate. Do not read out scores publicly.
14. You gives whole-programme closing address: celebrate specific achievements visible across the 8 weeks. Name specific skills you demonstrated (prepared in advance from participation log).
15. Group photo opportunity (parental consent obtained at enrolment – confirm before taking photos). You may hold their prototypes and certificates.
16. Feedback forms for parents/guardians if attending: 3-question form on programme quality, you communication, and likelihood to recommend.
17. Collect all completed peer feedback rubrics, panel score sheets, and score calculation worksheets. Archive with Assessment Records.
18. Remind you: Stage 6 (if continuing) builds on everything learned in Stage 5. The AI Engineer Certificate is the entry requirement for Stage 6 or equivalent demonstrated competency.
