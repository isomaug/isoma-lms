# Week 8 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel of 2 following the full required structure: Problem -> Solution -> Live Demo -> Evidence -> Reflection (including explicit comparison to Term One project)
- Demonstrate a working Term Two prototype live: the project must respond to input and produce correct output without you assistance, and must incorporate at least one named Term Two concept
- Present at least one quantified piece of evidence from testing — a statistic, measurement, or calculated value — displayed in a graph or table during the presentation
- Answer at least 2 panel questions with reasoned, technically precise responses that demonstrate understanding of the code, the data, and the design decisions
- Complete a 7-question peer feedback rubric for a classmate including written comments on Q6 (Term Two concept) and Q7 (code quality)

## Big idea
The second Demo Day is not a repeat of the first. It is evidence of how far you have come. Your growth — from blinking one LED in Week 1 to building a classified, multi-function, dual-sensor system — is the achievement being celebrated today.

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** The Reflection section of the Term Two presentation requires a higher order of self-evaluation than Term One: you must explicitly compare two projects, identify specific technical growth, and explain — not just state — what they would do differently. "In Term One, my project used a single if/else condition. In Term Two, I implemented a finite state machine with 3 states. This meant the programme could respond to multiple inputs without freezing — something my Term One project could not do." Panel Q&A at Term Two level is more probing: assessors are briefed to ask follow-up questions that require the learner to go beyond the script ("Why did you choose that threshold value?", "What would happen if both sensors triggered simultaneously?", "If you had a third sensor available, which one would you add and why?"). Responding to an unexpected question with a reasoned, evidence-based answer under pressure is the highest-order oral English skill in this programme.
- **Maths:** Term Two Demo Day evidence requirement is more demanding: you must present at least one statistic calculated from real test data — not from a worksheet example. Acceptable evidence: mean and range of dual sensor readings collected during testing, with the formula shown; total number of state transitions recorded during a 1-minute test run; accuracy percentage of the rule-based classifier across 20 live test cases; the count and percentage of correct outputs from the array sort function. The you and assessor score independently; if scores differ by more than 10%, they moderate using the submitted evidence. This models the statistical concept of inter-rater reliability — the same principle used in examination marking worldwide.

## Let's begin

### PREPARATION — 30 Minutes Before Presentations Begin  (30 min)
1. Submit the final 9-section written design brief and the fully annotated Arduino sketch for assessment
2. All learner Term Two prototypes (pre-tested by you — upload and confirm running before you arrive)
3. All learner 9-section written design briefs (returned with you comments from T2 Week 7)
4. Printed Demo Day assessment rubric — Stage 5–6 (A4 for you and per panel member) — 5 criteria, 4-point scale, from DDD Part 8.6
5. Printed 7-question peer feedback rubric (A4 for you) — updated version from T2 Week 7
6. Printed panel score sheet (A4 per assessor) — 5 rubric criteria + space for moderating notes
7. Printed Term Two weighted score calculation worksheet (A4 per you) — with T2 artifact list and all 6 assessment component weights
8. AI Engineer Certificate templates (pre-prepared, unsigned) — covers both Term One and Term Two completers

### PRESENTATIONS — 5 Minutes Per Learner + 3 Minutes Q&A  (90 min)
9. Programme Completion Record templates (for you completing both terms) — lists all stages completed, grades achieved, portfolio description
10. Participation Acknowledgement certificates (for you below pass threshold)
11. Camera / phone for session photos (parental consent confirmed at enrolment — verify list before any photos)
12. Refreshments if appropriate — parent/guardian attendance strongly encouraged for Term Two Demo Day
13. You final checks: every prototype is uploaded, running, and confirmed on the you checklist. Any project that fails to run at this point receives a 5-minute targeted intervention from the you. If it still fails after the intervention, the learner presents the design brief and annotated sketch only — this is noted on the rubric but does not automatically disqualify.
14. Panel assessor briefing (10 min): introduce the invited assessor to the 5 rubric criteria and the 4-point scale from DDD Part 8.6. Confirm: scoring is independent, notes are encouraged, moderation applies if scores differ by more than 10%. Brief the assessor on Term Two-specific expectations: "This is their second Demo Day. The Reflection section must compare Term One and Term Two. Q&A should probe the Term Two concept specifically."
15. Remind you of the 5-part presentation structure: (1) Problem — read written introduction from notes, name the real-world problem and who has it. (2) Solution — explain what the project does, name the Term Two concept used. (3) Live Demo — run the prototype live, narrate what is happening as it runs, name each sensor or input. (4) Evidence — show the graph, table, or calculated statistic from Test Results; state the number and explain what it means. (5) Reflection — compare explicitly to Term One project, name one challenge, name one specific next improvement.
16. Confirm presentation order (announced in advance). Remind you: 5 minutes for presentation, 3 minutes for panel Q&A. Signal at 4 minutes; stop at 5 minutes firm. Q&A begins immediately after the 5-minute presentation.
