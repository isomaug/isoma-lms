# Lesson T2–8: Demo Day 2 — AI Engineer Showcase & Programme Completion

**Duration:** 3 hours (Saturday) — Demo Day format; no holiday split (split across Week 8 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 2 · Week 8
**Age Range:** 10–12 years
**Group Size:** 8 learners presenting individually; partner supports during live demo
**Hardware:** Each learner's Term Two prototype (Arduino Uno, USB cable, laptop, component kit). Facilitator confirms all projects are uploaded, tested, and running before the session begins — minimum 30 minutes before learners arrive.
**Bloom's:** L3–L6 — all Bloom's levels demonstrated across presentation, demonstration, Q&A, and written documentation
**DDD ILO:** ILO 3.4 – Design, build, test, iterate, and present an original Arduino project with written design brief, live demonstration, and 5-minute formal presentation to a panel; Term Two: the project must demonstrate growth from Term One through incorporation of a named Term Two concept and a critical reflection on the iterative process
**NCDC:** Uganda NCDC P5–P6 – English Strand: Formal oral presentation, structured argument, self-reflection, and response to questions; Mathematics Strand: Quantifying outcomes with statistics, measurements, and calculations presented to an audience; ICT Strand: Demonstrating a working original digital project, evaluating its effectiveness
**Cambridge:** Cambridge Primary Computing 0845 – Stage 5: Presenting and explaining computing work; evaluating and reflecting on design decisions; demonstrating understanding of AI and data concepts

**Big Idea:** The second Demo Day is not a repeat of the first. It is evidence of how far you have come. Your growth — from blinking one LED in Week 1 to building a classified, multi-function, dual-sensor system — is the achievement being celebrated today.

**Context:** Term Two Context: Term One Demo Day established the format and the standard: 5-minute presentation, live prototype, panel of 2, written design brief, peer feedback rubric. Term Two Demo Day holds to the same DDD assessment framework (Parts 8.6, 9.1, 9.2) and the same certificate criteria — but the bar for what constitutes excellence is higher. Learners are expected to use more precise technical vocabulary, demonstrate more complex code, present more sophisticated data evidence, and reflect more critically on their own design decisions. The Reflection section of the presentation now explicitly requires learners to compare their Term Two project to their Term One project and identify specific technical growth.

## Learning Objectives

- Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel of 2 following the full required structure: Problem → Solution → Live Demo → Evidence → Reflection (including explicit comparison to Term One project)
- Demonstrate a working Term Two prototype live: the project must respond to input and produce correct output without facilitator assistance, and must incorporate at least one named Term Two concept
- Present at least one quantified piece of evidence from testing — a statistic, measurement, or calculated value — displayed in a graph or table during the presentation
- Answer at least 2 panel questions with reasoned, technically precise responses that demonstrate understanding of the code, the data, and the design decisions
- Complete a 7-question peer feedback rubric for a classmate including written comments on Q6 (Term Two concept) and Q7 (code quality)

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** The Reflection section of the Term Two presentation requires a higher order of self-evaluation than Term One: learners must explicitly compare two projects, identify specific technical growth, and explain — not just state — what they would do differently. "In Term One, my project used a single if/else condition. In Term Two, I implemented a finite state machine with 3 states. This meant the programme could respond to multiple inputs without freezing — something my Term One project could not do." Panel Q&A at Term Two level is more probing: assessors are briefed to ask follow-up questions that require the learner to go beyond the script ("Why did you choose that threshold value?", "What would happen if both sensors triggered simultaneously?", "If you had a third sensor available, which one would you add and why?"). Responding to an unexpected question with a reasoned, evidence-based answer under pressure is the highest-order oral English skill in this programme.

**Maths:** Term Two Demo Day evidence requirement is more demanding: learners must present at least one statistic calculated from real test data — not from a worksheet example. Acceptable evidence: mean and range of dual sensor readings collected during testing, with the formula shown; total number of state transitions recorded during a 1-minute test run; accuracy percentage of the rule-based classifier across 20 live test cases; the count and percentage of correct outputs from the array sort function. The facilitator and assessor score independently; if scores differ by more than 10%, they moderate using the submitted evidence. This models the statistical concept of inter-rater reliability — the same principle used in examination marking worldwide.

## Lesson Structure

### PREPARATION — 30 Minutes Before Presentations Begin  (30 min)
1. Submit the final 9-section written design brief and the fully annotated Arduino sketch for assessment
2. All learner Term Two prototypes (pre-tested by facilitator — upload and confirm running before learners arrive)
3. All learner 9-section written design briefs (returned with facilitator comments from T2 Week 7)
4. Printed Demo Day assessment rubric — Stage 5–6 (A4 per learner and per panel member) — 5 criteria, 4-point scale, from DDD Part 8.6
5. Printed 7-question peer feedback rubric (A4 per learner) — updated version from T2 Week 7
6. Printed panel score sheet (A4 per assessor) — 5 rubric criteria + space for moderating notes
7. Printed Term Two weighted score calculation worksheet (A4 per facilitator) — with T2 artifact list and all 6 assessment component weights
8. AI Engineer Certificate templates (pre-prepared, unsigned) — covers both Term One and Term Two completers
9. Programme Completion Record templates (for learners completing both terms) — lists all stages completed, grades achieved, portfolio description

### PRESENTATIONS — 5 Minutes Per Learner + 3 Minutes Q&A  (90 min)
10. Participation Acknowledgement certificates (for learners below pass threshold)
11. Camera / phone for session photos (parental consent confirmed at enrolment — verify list before any photos)
12. Refreshments if appropriate — parent/guardian attendance strongly encouraged for Term Two Demo Day
13. Facilitator final checks: every prototype is uploaded, running, and confirmed on the facilitator checklist. Any project that fails to run at this point receives a 5-minute targeted intervention from the facilitator. If it still fails after the intervention, the learner presents the design brief and annotated sketch only — this is noted on the rubric but does not automatically disqualify.
14. Panel assessor briefing (10 min): introduce the invited assessor to the 5 rubric criteria and the 4-point scale from DDD Part 8.6. Confirm: scoring is independent, notes are encouraged, moderation applies if scores differ by more than 10%. Brief the assessor on Term Two-specific expectations: "This is their second Demo Day. The Reflection section must compare Term One and Term Two. Q&A should probe the Term Two concept specifically."
15. Remind learners of the 5-part presentation structure: (1) Problem — read written introduction from notes, name the real-world problem and who has it. (2) Solution — explain what the project does, name the Term Two concept used. (3) Live Demo — run the prototype live, narrate what is happening as it runs, name each sensor or input. (4) Evidence — show the graph, table, or calculated statistic from Test Results; state the number and explain what it means. (5) Reflection — compare explicitly to Term One project, name one challenge, name one specific next improvement.
16. Confirm presentation order (announced in advance). Remind learners: 5 minutes for presentation, 3 minutes for panel Q&A. Signal at 4 minutes; stop at 5 minutes firm. Q&A begins immediately after the 5-minute presentation.
17. Each learner presents in order. Facilitator times with a visible timer. Partner sits adjacent to operate the prototype during the Demo section if needed — the presenter must narrate what the partner is doing: "My partner is now pressing button 1 to trigger the GREEN state."

### SCORING, MODERATION AND CERTIFICATES  (30 min)
18. Panel Q&A (3 minutes per learner): facilitator asks at least 1 question; assessor asks at least 1 question. Term Two-specific panel questions the assessor is briefed to choose from: "Explain why you chose that specific threshold value in your classifier." "What would happen if the temperature sensor read an extreme value of 50°C — would your FSM handle it correctly?" "You used an array — what is stored at index 0 and at the last index?" "How does your project relate to the AI concepts you studied in Week 6?" "If you were to add a third sensor, which one and why?" "Your Reflection mentions Term One — what is the single most important technical skill you learned between Term One and Term Two?"
19. Audience (other learners): complete the 7-question peer feedback rubric during each presentation. Q6 and Q7 require careful attention — Q6 asks learners to identify and describe the Term Two concept; Q7 asks them to evaluate code quality from the information given in the presentation.
20. Panel scoring: facilitator and assessor complete the score sheet independently for each presenter immediately after their Q&A. Do not wait for all presentations to finish. Moderate after all presentations are complete if scores differ by more than 10%.
21. After all 8 presentations: facilitator and assessor confer. Moderate any score with a discrepancy greater than 10% using the design brief, sketch, and test results log as evidence. Finalise all panel scores before the certificate calculation phase.
22. Calculate each learner's Term Two weighted total score using the full DDD Part 9 formula: Participation 10% (8-week average on 3-point scale); Weekly Artifacts 25% (7 artifacts across T2 Weeks 1–7, assessed on 5-point rubric, weighted average); Prototype Functionality 10%; Code Quality 10%; Integration English + Math 10%; Presentation 20%; Written Design Brief 10%; Peer Review 5%.
23. Apply the minimum pass requirements from DDD Part 9.2: (1) Overall weighted score ≥55%. (2) Demo Day rubric score ≥2/4 on all 5 criteria — no zero criteria allowed. (3) At least 5 of 7 Term Two weekly artifacts submitted. (4) Minimum 6 of 8 Term Two sessions attended.
24. Apply the grading scale from DDD Part 9.1: Distinction ≥85%; Merit ≥70%; Pass ≥55%; Near Pass 45–54% (resubmission offered within 2 weeks); Not Yet <45% (Participation Acknowledgement issued, repeat stage at 50% fee reduction, parent/guardian meeting required).
25. For learners who completed both Term One and Term Two: issue the Programme Completion Record listing both terms, both grades, and a portfolio description. This record is the basis for Stage 6 entry or demonstration of competency.

### CELEBRATION, CERTIFICATES AND CLOSE  (30 min)
26. Sign and prepare certificates: AI Engineer Certificate signed by Lead Facilitator and Programme Director. For Distinction achievers: note on Isoma Edutech Honour Roll, prepare written personal commendation. For Near Pass learners: prepare written feedback identifying exactly which rubric criteria were not met and what resubmission evidence is required.
27. Certificate ceremony: call each learner's name individually. Announce their grade descriptor only — not the numerical score — to maintain dignity. "Awarded with Distinction", "Awarded with Merit", "Certificate Awarded", "Certificate pending resubmission." Whole class applauds each award. Scores are communicated privately after the ceremony.
28. Programme Completion Records issued to all learners who completed both Term One and Term Two — regardless of grade. This record acknowledges the full 16-week journey.
29. Facilitator closing address (prepared in advance from 8-week participation log): name a specific achievement for each learner that is visible and genuine. "Over these 8 weeks, I have watched you move from blinking one LED to building a dual-sensor classifier that categorises the environment. That is real engineering growth." Address the group as a whole: specific skills they now have that they did not have in Week 1 of Term One.
30. Group photograph (parental consent verified). Learners may hold prototypes and certificates.
31. Parent/guardian feedback forms if attending — 4-question form: programme quality, facilitator communication, learner growth observed at home, likelihood to recommend or continue to Stage 6.
32. Collect all peer feedback rubrics, panel score sheets, weighted score calculation worksheets, and Test Results logs. File with Assessment Records. All records retained for programme moderation and auditing purposes.
33. Inform learners of Stage 6 pathway: Stage 6 builds on the AI Engineer Certificate. Key concepts in Stage 6 include: more complex sensor networks, introduction to machine learning with real datasets, and a final capstone project. The AI Engineer Certificate (or demonstrated equivalent competency) is the entry requirement.
