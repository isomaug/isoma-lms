# Week 8 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel of 2 in the full required structure: Problem -> Algorithm (present and briefly explain the flowchart) -> Live Demo -> Statistical Evidence (precision/recall, accuracy, or acceptance criteria result with the number and its meaning) -> Reflection (one specific growth statement per term: Term One, Term Two, Term Three)
- Demonstrate a working capstone prototype live — no you assistance — that uses at least two sensors, a struct, millis() non-blocking timing, and at least one statistical or AI classification element
- Answer at least 2 panel questions with technically precise, evidence-based responses — including at least one question that probes the algorithm design or the statistical evidence

## Big idea
Three terms. Twenty-four sessions. You arrived not knowing what a variable was. You leave having built a data logger, a rule-based AI classifier, and a capstone system that your you could not have built without you. Today you show what that journey made possible.

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** The Term Three Demo Day Reflection section is the most demanding oral component of the programme. You must produce three specific growth statements — one per term — in formal spoken English, in sequence, without reading from notes: "In Term One, I could not decompose a problem into functions — I wrote everything in loop(). By Term Three, I designed a capstone programme with eight named functions, each with a single clearly defined job." "In Term Two, I built a decision tree classifier but chose thresholds by intuition. In Term Three, I chose thresholds by inspecting 30 labelled training readings and calculating the median-based optimal boundary." "In Term Three, I measured my classifier with precision and recall — I know that my classifier achieves 70% accuracy and that its lowest recall is 50% for the DIM class, and I know what that means for a deployed system." Producing three such statements under panel pressure, in sequence, in formal English, demonstrates oral language mastery at a level well above the primary school standard — and directly previews secondary school assessed oral presentations.
- **Maths:** The Statistical Evidence section requires you to present at least one number from the capstone test cycles with its full meaning. Acceptable evidence: (1) "Our running mean ldrNorm after 20 readings was 0.612 ± 0.031 — the range of 0.031 tells us the light level was relatively stable during our test period." (2) "Our classifier achieved 70% accuracy on 10 held-out test inputs — meaning 7 correct classifications and 3 errors. The Precision for COMFORTABLE was 1.0 — every reading we classified as COMFORTABLE was genuinely COMFORTABLE. The Recall for DIM was 0.5 — we missed half the DIM readings, classifying them as COMFORTABLE instead." (3) "The deviation alert fired 3 times during a 5-minute logging session — a rate of 0.6 alerts per minute, or one alert every 100 seconds on average." The panel assessor is briefed to ask: "What would need to change in your system to increase the Recall for your lowest class?"

11. AI Engineer Certificate templates (pre-prepared, unsigned) — one per qualifying learner
12. Isoma Edutech Stage 5 Programme Completion Record templates — for three-term completers listing all 24 sessions, all grades, and a portfolio description

The Statistical Evidence section must include a named metric — precision, recall, accuracy, or a calculated rate — with a specific value. The panel Q&A should probe the algorithm design and the statistical evidence specifically." Get ready to use the Panel Q&A Probing Question Bank.
