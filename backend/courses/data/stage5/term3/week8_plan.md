# Lesson T3–8: Final Demo Day — Programme Culmination, Full Assessment & Stage Certification

**Duration:** 3 hours (Saturday) — Full Demo Day format; no holiday split (split across Week 8 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 3 · Week 8
**Age Range:** 10–12 years
**Group Size:** 8 learners presenting individually; pair partner supports live demo
**Hardware:** Each learner's Term Three capstone prototype — pre-tested and confirmed running by the facilitator a minimum of 60 minutes before learners arrive. All USB cables, laptops, and breadboard circuits must be assembled and verified before the session opens.
**Bloom's:** L3–L6 — all Bloom's levels demonstrated across the presentation, live demonstration, evidence, Q&A, and written documentation
**DDD ILO:** ILO 3.4 – Present an original Arduino capstone project integrating coding, English literacy, and Mathematics; ILO 3.3 – Demonstrate AI literacy at the analytical and evaluative level by including precision, recall, or accuracy data as evidence in the presentation; all four Stage 5–6 ILOs are assessed holistically on Term Three Demo Day.
**NCDC:** Uganda NCDC P5–P6 – English Strand: Formal oral presentation at highest programme standard; structured argument; reflective and evaluative discourse; responding to probing questions; Mathematics Strand: Quantifying project outcomes with statistical evidence and presenting to a panel; ICT Strand: Demonstrating mastery of a fully integrated original digital project
**Cambridge:** Cambridge Primary Computing 0845 – Stage 5: Presenting and explaining computing work; evaluating design decisions; demonstrating AI literacy; reflecting on the full learning journey; portfolio-level evidence of computing mastery

**Big Idea:** Three terms. Twenty-four sessions. You arrived not knowing what a variable was. You leave having built a data logger, a rule-based AI classifier, and a capstone system that your facilitator could not have built without you. Today you show what that journey made possible.

**Context:** Term Two Context: Term One Demo Day assessed a first prototype and a first 7-section design brief. Term Two Demo Day required a second project with a Term One reflection and a 9-section brief. Term Three Demo Day is the programme culmination: the capstone project is assessed against the highest standard of the programme, the presentation must include Algorithm Documentation (the flowchart), statistical or AI evidence (precision/recall or acceptance criteria quantitative results), and a Reflection section that spans all three terms explicitly. The panel for Term Three Demo Day should include, where possible, a technical assessor — a computing teacher, university student, software engineer, or parent with a technology background — in addition to the facilitator. The DDD assessment framework from Parts 8.6, 9.1, and 9.2 applies in full. For learners completing all three terms, the Programme Completion Record is issued covering the full Stage 5 journey.

## Learning Objectives

- Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel of 2 in the full required structure: Problem → Algorithm (present and briefly explain the flowchart) → Live Demo → Statistical Evidence (precision/recall, accuracy, or acceptance criteria result with the number and its meaning) → Reflection (one specific growth statement per term: Term One, Term Two, Term Three)
- Demonstrate a working capstone prototype live — no facilitator assistance — that uses at least two sensors, a struct, millis() non-blocking timing, and at least one statistical or AI classification element
- Answer at least 2 panel questions with technically precise, evidence-based responses — including at least one question that probes the algorithm design or the statistical evidence

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** The Term Three Demo Day Reflection section is the most demanding oral component of the programme. Learners must produce three specific growth statements — one per term — in formal spoken English, in sequence, without reading from notes: "In Term One, I could not decompose a problem into functions — I wrote everything in loop(). By Term Three, I designed a capstone programme with eight named functions, each with a single clearly defined job." "In Term Two, I built a decision tree classifier but chose thresholds by intuition. In Term Three, I chose thresholds by inspecting 30 labelled training readings and calculating the median-based optimal boundary." "In Term Three, I measured my classifier with precision and recall — I know that my classifier achieves 70% accuracy and that its lowest recall is 50% for the DIM class, and I know what that means for a deployed system." Producing three such statements under panel pressure, in sequence, in formal English, demonstrates oral language mastery at a level well above the primary school standard — and directly previews secondary school assessed oral presentations.

**Maths:** The Statistical Evidence section requires learners to present at least one number from the capstone test cycles with its full meaning. Acceptable evidence: (1) "Our running mean ldrNorm after 20 readings was 0.612 ± 0.031 — the range of 0.031 tells us the light level was relatively stable during our test period." (2) "Our classifier achieved 70% accuracy on 10 held-out test inputs — meaning 7 correct classifications and 3 errors. The Precision for COMFORTABLE was 1.0 — every reading we classified as COMFORTABLE was genuinely COMFORTABLE. The Recall for DIM was 0.5 — we missed half the DIM readings, classifying them as COMFORTABLE instead." (3) "The deviation alert fired 3 times during a 5-minute logging session — a rate of 0.6 alerts per minute, or one alert every 100 seconds on average." The panel assessor is briefed to ask: "What would need to change in your system to increase the Recall for your lowest class?"

## Lesson Structure

### PREPARATION — 60 Minutes Before Presentations Begin  (30 min)
1. Submit all required documentation before the presentation begins: 10-section design brief, pseudocode and flowchart, 3-cycle acceptance criteria log, code review self-assessment, annotated sketch — all assembled in order
2. Complete an 8-question peer feedback rubric for a classmate's presentation with specific written comments on every question
3. If the overall weighted score qualifies: receive the AI Engineer Certificate for Stage 5, Term Three — and, for three-term completers, the Isoma Edutech Stage 5 Programme Completion Record
4. All learner capstone prototypes — pre-tested by facilitator; any failing project receives targeted intervention before learners arrive
5. All learner 10-section written design briefs — returned with facilitator comments from T3 Week 7
6. Printed Demo Day assessment rubric — Stage 5–6 (A4 per learner and panel member) — 5 criteria, 4-point scale, from DDD Part 8.6, with a Term Three annotation for each criterion
7. Printed 8-question peer feedback rubric (A4 per learner) — Term Three version: Q1–Q5 standard; Q6: Was the flowchart presented and explained?; Q7: Was a specific statistical metric cited with its value and meaning?; Q8: Did the Reflection span all three terms with a specific growth statement per term?
8. Printed panel score sheet (A4 per assessor) — 5 rubric criteria with space for independent scores and moderation notes
9. Printed Term Three weighted score calculation worksheet (A4 per facilitator) — with the full 8-week T3 artifact list and all 6 DDD assessment component weights

### PRESENTATIONS — 5 Minutes + 3 Minutes Q&A Per Learner  (90 min)
10. Printed "Panel Q&A Probing Question Bank" (A4 per facilitator/assessor) — 12 Term Three-specific questions the assessor may choose from
11. AI Engineer Certificate templates (pre-prepared, unsigned) — one per qualifying learner
12. Isoma Edutech Stage 5 Programme Completion Record templates — for three-term completers listing all 24 sessions, all grades, and a portfolio description
13. Participation Acknowledgement certificates — for learners below the pass threshold
14. Camera / phone — parental photo consent confirmed from enrolment register before any recording
15. Programme feedback forms for parents and guardians — 5-question form on programme quality, learning growth, and Stage 6 interest
16. Facilitator pre-session checklist (30 min before learners arrive): every prototype is uploaded, running, and confirmed. Every USB cable is connected and every Serial Monitor is open and showing correct output. Any project that fails at this point receives a targeted intervention from the facilitator. If the project still fails after the intervention, the learner presents the design brief, algorithm, and code walkthrough only — this is noted on the rubric but does not automatically disqualify from the certificate.
17. Documentation collection: collect all required documents from each learner before the session formally begins. Any learner missing the pseudocode/flowchart or the acceptance criteria log is directed to complete the missing section in the 30-minute preparation window — this is the last opportunity before assessment begins.
18. Panel assessor briefing (10 min): introduce the invited assessor to the 5 rubric criteria and the 4-point scale from DDD Part 8.6. Brief on Term Three-specific expectations: "This is the capstone presentation. The Reflection section must cover all three terms with a specific growth statement per term. The Statistical Evidence section must include a named metric — precision, recall, accuracy, or a calculated rate — with a specific value. The panel Q&A should probe the algorithm design and the statistical evidence specifically." Distribute the Panel Q&A Probing Question Bank.

### SCORING, MODERATION, AND CERTIFICATE CALCULATION  (30 min)
19. Learner preparation (10 min): remind learners of the 5-part structure. Remind them of the timing: 5 minutes exactly for the presentation; the facilitator signals at 4 minutes and stops at 5. Q&A is 3 minutes. Remind them: the flowchart should be held up or placed on the table during the Algorithm section — the panel will look at it.
20. Each learner presents in announced order. The pair partner sits adjacent and operates the prototype during the Demo section if needed — narrated by the presenter. Timer is visible. Signal at 4 minutes. Hard stop at 5 minutes.
21. Presentation structure each learner must follow: (1) Problem — written introduction read from notes; 2–3 sentences on the real-world problem and the named target user. (2) Algorithm — hold up the flowchart; explain in 2–3 sentences what the main decision path is: "The programme starts by reading both sensors. If the temperature exceeds the threshold, it enters the WARM branch. Otherwise it checks the light level." (3) Live Demo — run the prototype live; narrate what is happening and which functions are executing. Type a Serial command and explain the output. (4) Statistical Evidence — state the specific metric, the value, and its meaning. (5) Reflection — three specific growth statements, one per term, delivered in sequence without reading from notes.
22. Panel Q&A: facilitator asks at least 1 question from the Probing Question Bank; assessor asks at least 1. Term Three-specific probing questions include: "Walk me through what happens in your algorithm when both sensors trigger simultaneously — which branch takes priority and why?" "Your recall for the DIM class is 50%. What specific change to your training data or your threshold value would you make to improve that?" "If you were to hand this programme to a junior engineer to maintain, which section of your design brief would you ask them to read first and why?" "How does the struct you used in the capstone relate to the individual variables you used in Term One? What does the struct make possible that the variables could not?"
23. Audience completes the 8-question peer feedback rubric during each presentation. Q6, Q7, and Q8 are Term Three-specific — learners must attend carefully to the flowchart, the statistical metric, and all three Reflection statements to complete these questions accurately.
24. Panel scoring: facilitator and assessor complete the score sheet independently for each presenter immediately after their Q&A. Scores are finalised after all presentations. Moderate any criterion where scores differ by more than 10% using the submitted documentation as evidence.
25. Apply the full DDD Part 9 weighted score formula for each learner. Weights: Participation 10% (T3 8-week average on 3-point scale); Weekly Artifacts 25% (7 artifacts from T3 Weeks 1–7, 5-point rubric weighted average); Prototype Functionality 10%; Code Quality 10%; Integration English + Math 10%; Presentation 20%; Written Design Brief 10%; Peer Review 5%. The 10-section design brief is assessed for Written Design Brief; the code review self-assessment contributes to Code Quality.
26. Apply DDD Part 9.2 minimum pass requirements for Stage 5–6: overall weighted score ≥55%; Demo Day rubric score ≥2/4 on all 5 criteria (no zero criteria allowed); at least 5 of 7 Term Three weekly artifacts submitted; minimum 6 of 8 Term Three sessions attended.
27. Apply DDD Part 9.1 grading scale: Distinction ≥85% — name on Honour Roll, written personal commendation, encouraged to mentor next Stage 5 cohort; Merit ≥70% — written personal commendation; Pass ≥55% — certificate awarded with written feedback identifying 2 strengths and 1 area for Stage 6 focus; Near Pass 45–54% — resubmission offered within 2 weeks; Not Yet <45% — Participation Acknowledgement issued, stage repeat at 50% fee reduction, parent/guardian meeting required.

### CELEBRATION, CERTIFICATES AND PROGRAMME CLOSE  (30 min)
28. For learners completing all three terms (Terms 1, 2, and 3): issue the Isoma Edutech Stage 5 Programme Completion Record. This document lists all three terms, all three Demo Day grades, all major projects, and a portfolio description. It is the basis for Stage 6 entry. Sign and seal by the Lead Facilitator and Programme Director.
29. For Distinction achievers: record on the Isoma Edutech Honour Roll. Prepare the personal commendation — naming 3 specific technical achievements from the capstone that exceeded the programme standard. For Near Pass learners: prepare the resubmission brief — specifying exactly which rubric criteria were below 2/4 and what evidence is needed.
30. Certificate ceremony: call each learner's name. State the grade descriptor only ("with Distinction", "with Merit", "awarded") — never the score — publicly. The pair partner stands alongside during the certificate presentation. Whole class applauds each award. Near Pass and Not Yet outcomes are communicated privately after the ceremony.
31. Programme Completion Records: issued to all three-term completers. The facilitator reads a personalised statement for each three-term completer — prepared from the 24-session participation log — naming specific technical milestones: "From the first Blink sketch to a multi-sensor AI data logger in 24 sessions. That is the journey this record documents."
32. Facilitator closing address for the group: name the collective journey explicitly. "In Term One Week 1, you installed Arduino IDE and blinked an LED. In Term Three Week 4, you built a machine learning pipeline, collected labelled training data, and calculated the precision and recall of your own classifier. In Term Three Week 6, you wrote a formal position paper addressed to a Ugandan government decision-maker arguing for AI governance policy. That is the arc of this programme — and you completed it."
33. Stage 6 pathway: briefly describe what Stage 6 builds on. Learners who completed Stage 5 (any number of terms) hold the AI Engineer Certificate as the entry requirement for Stage 6. Key Stage 6 concepts that build directly on Term Three: object-oriented programming (builds on structs from T3 W2), machine learning with real Python libraries (builds on the AI pipeline from T3 W4), and networked data systems (builds on the data logger from T3 W5).
34. Group photograph — parental consent verified against the enrolment register before any photo is taken. Learners hold their prototypes and certificates.
35. Collect all peer feedback rubrics, panel score sheets, weighted score worksheets, and documentation portfolios. Archive in the Isoma Edutech Assessment Records. All records retained for programme moderation, audit, and Stage 6 transition planning.
36. Distribute parent/guardian programme feedback forms. Request completion before departure or via the contact provided. Feedback informs the next cohort's delivery.

## Safeguarding

Safeguarding Note: Photo and video recording requires written parental consent from the enrolment register — verify the consent list before any device is used. Photos used for marketing or social media require a separate explicit consent form. Scores and grades are communicated privately — never announced publicly, never posted on whiteboards or displays visible to others. Near Pass and Not Yet outcomes are shared in private one-to-one meetings only — never in front of other learners. Any learner showing signs of distress during the session (performance anxiety, an unexpected result, or any other reason) is supported quietly by a designated adult away from the main group. Certificates for Near Pass learners are withheld until resubmission — a certificate that has not been earned must not be issued. All Programme Completion Records are stored securely and disclosed only to the learner, their parent/guardian, or an authorised educational institution upon written request.
