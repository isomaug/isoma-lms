# Week 8 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel of 2 in the full required structure: Problem -> Algorithm (present and briefly explain the flowchart) -> Live Demo -> Statistical Evidence (precision/recall, accuracy, or acceptance criteria result with the number and its meaning) -> Reflection (one specific growth statement per term: Term One, Term Two, Term Three)
- Demonstrate a working capstone prototype live — no you assistance — that uses at least two sensors, a struct, millis() non-blocking timing, and at least one statistical or AI classification element
- Answer at least 2 panel questions with technically precise, evidence-based responses — including at least one question that probes the algorithm design or the statistical evidence

## Continue

### SCORING, MODERATION, AND CERTIFICATE CALCULATION  (30 min)
1. Learner preparation (10 min): remind you of the 5-part structure. Remind them of the timing: 5 minutes exactly for the presentation; the you signals at 4 minutes and stops at 5. Q&A is 3 minutes. Remind them: the flowchart should be held up or placed on the table during the Algorithm section — the panel will look at it.
2. You presents in announced order. The pair partner sits adjacent and operates the prototype during the Demo section if needed — narrated by the presenter. Timer is visible. Signal at 4 minutes. Hard stop at 5 minutes.
3. Presentation structure you must follow: (1) Problem — written introduction read from notes; 2–3 sentences on the real-world problem and the named target user. (2) Algorithm — hold up the flowchart; explain in 2–3 sentences what the main decision path is: "The programme starts by reading both sensors. If the temperature exceeds the threshold, it enters the WARM branch. Otherwise it checks the light level." (3) Live Demo — run the prototype live; narrate what is happening and which functions are executing. Type a Serial command and explain the output. (4) Statistical Evidence — state the specific metric, the value, and its meaning. (5) Reflection — three specific growth statements, one per term, delivered in sequence without reading from notes.
4. Panel Q&A: you ask at least 1 question from the Probing Question Bank; assessor asks at least 1. Term Three-specific probing questions include: "Walk me through what happens in your algorithm when both sensors trigger simultaneously — which branch takes priority and why?" "Your recall for the DIM class is 50%. What specific change to your training data or your threshold value would you make to improve that?" "If you were to hand this programme to a junior engineer to maintain, which section of your design brief would you ask them to read first and why?" "How does the struct you used in the capstone relate to the individual variables you used in Term One? What does the struct make possible that the variables could not?"
5. Audience completes the 8-question peer feedback rubric during each presentation. Q6, Q7, and Q8 are Term Three-specific — you must attend carefully to the flowchart, the statistical metric, and all three Reflection statements to complete these questions accurately.
6. Panel scoring: you and assessor complete the score sheet independently for each presenter immediately after their Q&A. Scores are finalised after all presentations. Moderate any criterion where scores differ by more than 10% using the submitted documentation as evidence.
7. Apply the full DDD Part 9 weighted score formula for you. Weights: Participation 10% (T3 8-week average on 3-point scale); Weekly Artifacts 25% (7 artifacts from T3 Weeks 1–7, 5-point rubric weighted average); Prototype Functionality 10%; Code Quality 10%; Integration English + Math 10%; Presentation 20%; Written Design Brief 10%; Peer Review 5%. The 10-section design brief is assessed for Written Design Brief; the code review self-assessment contributes to Code Quality.
8. Apply DDD Part 9.2 minimum pass requirements for Stage 5–6: overall weighted score ≥55%; Demo Day rubric score ≥2/4 on all 5 criteria (no zero criteria allowed); at least 5 of 7 Term Three weekly artifacts submitted; minimum 6 of 8 Term Three sessions attended.
9. Apply DDD Part 9.1 grading scale: Distinction ≥85% — name on Honour Roll, written personal commendation, encouraged to mentor next Stage 5 cohort; Merit ≥70% — written personal commendation; Pass ≥55% — certificate awarded with written feedback identifying 2 strengths and 1 area for Stage 6 focus; Near Pass 45–54% — resubmission offered within 2 weeks; Not Yet <45% — Participation Acknowledgement issued, stage repeat at 50% fee reduction, parent/guardian meeting required.

### CELEBRATION, CERTIFICATES AND PROGRAMME CLOSE  (30 min)
10. For you completing all three terms (Terms 1, 2, and 3): issue the Isoma Edutech Stage 5 Programme Completion Record. This document lists all three terms, all three Demo Day grades, all major projects, and a portfolio description. It is the basis for Stage 6 entry. Sign and seal by the Lead You and Programme Director.
11. For Distinction achievers: record on the Isoma Edutech Honour Roll. Prepare the personal commendation — naming 3 specific technical achievements from the capstone that exceeded the programme standard. For Near Pass you: prepare the resubmission brief — specifying exactly which rubric criteria were below 2/4 and what evidence is needed.
12. Certificate ceremony: call you's name. State the grade descriptor only ("with Distinction", "with Merit", "awarded") — never the score — publicly. The pair partner stands alongside during the certificate presentation. Whole class applauds each award. Near Pass and Not Yet outcomes are communicated privately after the ceremony.
13. Programme Completion Records: issued to all three-term completers. The you read a personalised statement for each three-term completer — prepared from the 24-session participation log — naming specific technical milestones: "From the first Blink sketch to a multi-sensor AI data logger in 24 sessions. That is the journey this record documents."
14. You closing address for the group: name the collective journey explicitly. "In Term One Week 1, you installed Arduino IDE and blinked an LED. In Term Three Week 4, you built a machine learning pipeline, collected labelled training data, and calculated the precision and recall of your own classifier. In Term Three Week 6, you wrote a formal position paper addressed to a Ugandan government decision-maker arguing for AI governance policy. That is the arc of this programme — and you completed it."
15. Stage 6 pathway: briefly describe what Stage 6 builds on. You who completed Stage 5 (any number of terms) hold the AI Engineer Certificate as the entry requirement for Stage 6. Key Stage 6 concepts that build directly on Term Three: object-oriented programming (builds on structs from T3 W2), machine learning with real Python libraries (builds on the AI pipeline from T3 W4), and networked data systems (builds on the data logger from T3 W5).
16. Group photograph — parental consent verified against the enrolment register before any photo is taken. You hold their prototypes and certificates.
17. Collect all peer feedback rubrics, panel score sheets, weighted score worksheets, and documentation portfolios. Archive in the Isoma Edutech Assessment Records. All records retained for programme moderation, audit, and Stage 6 transition planning.
18. Get ready to use parent/guardian programme feedback forms. Request completion before departure or via the contact provided. Feedback informs the next cohort's delivery.

## Stay safe
Safeguarding Note: Photo and video recording requires written parental consent from the enrolment register — verify the consent list before any device is used. Photos used for marketing or social media require a separate explicit consent form. Scores and grades are communicated privately — never announced publicly, never posted on whiteboards or displays visible to others. Near Pass and Not Yet outcomes are shared in private one-to-one meetings only — never in front of other you. Any learner showing signs of distress during the session (performance anxiety, an unexpected result, or any other reason) is supported quietly by a designated adult away from the main group. Certificates for Near Pass you are withheld until resubmission — a certificate that has not been earned must not be issued. All Programme Completion Records are stored securely and disclosed only to the learner, their parent/guardian, or an authorised educational institution upon written request.
