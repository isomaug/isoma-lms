Term Three Focus
The DDD Part 3.2 hardware profile explicitly lists 'Bluetooth' as a Micro:bit v2 capability. Term Three Week 5 introduces Bluetooth via MakeCode's Bluetooth extension — allowing the Micro:bit to communicate with a paired phone or tablet. This completes full hardware coverage of the DDD's declared platform and provides a direct comparison with the radio module used in T2 Week 1.
Key ILO(s)
ILO 2.1 (hardware completion) – Use the MakeCode Bluetooth extension to send sensor data (temperature or accelerometer) from the Micro:bit to a paired device; compare Bluetooth and radio as two wireless communication methods; explain in writing one advantage and one limitation of each.
Big Idea
Radio and Bluetooth are both wireless — they both send data through the air without cables. But they work differently and are useful in different situations. Understanding the difference between communication technologies is part of being a computational thinker, not just a coder.
Prior Learning Link
T2 Week 1 introduced the radio block for device-to-device Micro:bit communication. This week introduces Bluetooth — a different wireless standard — using the same conceptual framework (send / receive / data). Learners compare both directly, deepening understanding of both by seeing them side-by-side.
LEARNING OBJECTIVES
#
Objective
1
Enable the MakeCode Bluetooth extension and explain why enabling Bluetooth disables the radio module (they share the same antenna hardware on the Micro:bit v2)
2
Use the Bluetooth extension to display the Micro:bit's name and UUID on the LED matrix so a paired device can identify it
3
Programme the Micro:bit to broadcast a live temperature reading over Bluetooth using the 'bluetooth temperature service' block
4
Pair a phone or tablet with the Micro:bit using the official Micro:bit app (iOS/Android) and confirm the temperature reading appears on the paired device screen
5
Create a comparison table: Radio vs. Bluetooth — 4 rows comparing range, number of connected devices, setup complexity, and real-world use case
6
Write a 4-sentence technical explanation: what Bluetooth is, how it differs from radio, what the Micro:bit uses it for, and one real-world application where Bluetooth would be preferred over radio

Cross-Curricular Links
English Literacy
Technical comparative writing — learners complete the 4-row comparison table and then write the 4-sentence technical explanation. This is the DDD's Stage 3–4 English strand applied to a new technology context: reading comprehension of a technical concept, followed by written explanation in the learner's own words. Sentence frame: ① 'Bluetooth is a type of wireless communication that…' ② 'Unlike radio, Bluetooth…' ③ 'The Micro:bit uses Bluetooth to…' ④ 'One real-world situation where Bluetooth is better than radio is… because…'. Vocabulary in use: learners must use the words 'paired', 'wireless', 'range', and 'protocol' in their 4 sentences.
Mathematics
Comparison and measurement: the Bluetooth vs. Radio comparison table includes range as one of the 4 rows. Bluetooth typical range: 10–30 metres. Micro:bit radio typical range: up to 70 metres outdoors (configurable). Calculate: how many times further can radio communicate than Bluetooth? (70 ÷ 10 = 7×, at minimum.) If a classroom is 12 metres long, is Bluetooth sufficient for classroom communication? (Yes.) What is the maximum number of Micro:bits that can be in the same radio group? (Unlimited, but discussed as practical group sizes.) Bluetooth supports 1-to-1 pairing in MakeCode — what does this mean for a class of 8? (4 pairs of devices at most.) Connects to DDD Part 2.4 mathematics: 'Number patterns; sorting and comparison operators.'
LESSON STRUCTURE
Step
Activity
HOOK – Two Ways to Go Wireless (10 min)
1
Hold up the Micro:bit and ask: 'We already know one way this device sends data without wires — what is it?' (Radio.) 'Today we learn the second way. Who has heard of Bluetooth?' Take 3 responses. Ask: 'What devices in your home use Bluetooth?' (Headphones, phone, speakers.)
2
Introduce the technical hook: 'On the Micro:bit v2, Bluetooth and radio share the SAME antenna. You cannot use both at the same time. When you enable Bluetooth, the radio turns off. Why do you think the engineers designed it that way?' Take 2 speculative answers — acknowledge all as reasonable engineering hypotheses.
3
Distribute the Bluetooth vs. Radio comparison worksheet. Learners fill in what they ALREADY KNOW for the radio column — from T2 Week 1. This pre-fills their prior knowledge before the new content begins.
4
Bridge: 'By the end of today, you will fill in the Bluetooth column too — and write a technical explanation comparing both. Then you will pair this device with a phone and read a temperature reading on the phone screen. Let's go.'
DESIGN – Enable Bluetooth and Plan the Programme (10 min)
5
Facilitator demonstrates on the projected screen: in MakeCode, go to Extensions → search 'Bluetooth' → add the extension. Show the warning: 'Adding Bluetooth will disable the radio blocks.' Ask: 'Why do you think this warning appears? What does this tell us about the hardware?' Guide to: shared antenna.
6
Show the Bluetooth extension block categories: Bluetooth → 'on bluetooth connected' event, 'on bluetooth disconnected' event, 'bluetooth temperature service'. Learners open MakeCode on laptops and add the extension themselves. Navigator verifies the Bluetooth category now appears.
7
Pairs plan the programme on a sticky note before coding: on start → show Micro:bit name (so the phone can identify it) → bluetooth temperature service start. On bluetooth connected → show icon tick. On bluetooth disconnected → show icon cross. Facilitator checks: does every pair's plan include the connection event handlers?
DELIVER – Code, Pair, and Broadcast (30 min)
8
Distribute Micro:bits and laptops. Assign Driver and Navigator.
9
Step 1 – Build the programme (10 min): Driver codes from the sticky-note plan. on start → show string (Micro:bit name) → bluetooth temperature service start. on bluetooth connected → show icon tick → clear screen. on bluetooth disconnected → show icon cross → clear screen. Download to Micro:bit.
10
Step 2 – Pair the device (8 min): facilitator distributes phones/tablets (1 per pair). Learners open the Micro:bit app → click 'Pair' → hold Buttons A and B simultaneously on the Micro:bit while pressing reset. Follow the pairing steps on screen. Confirm: does the tick icon appear on the Micro:bit when paired successfully?
11
Step 3 – Read the temperature (7 min): in the Micro:bit app, navigate to the temperature graph. Confirm a live reading is displayed. SWAP Driver and Navigator. New Driver holds the Micro:bit in different locations (near a window, near a computer fan) — does the reading change? Record 3 readings.
12
Step 4 – Compare and complete the table (5 min): pairs complete the Bluetooth column of the comparison table using what they observed today. Compare to the radio column filled in at the start of the session.
DISCOVER – Write, Compare, and Reflect (8 min)
13
Each learner writes the 4-sentence technical explanation independently. Facilitator circulates: 'Sentence 4 asks about a real-world situation — think about something specific. Not just "headphones" but "wireless headphones on a bus where many people have devices" — why is that a better example?'
14
Two pairs share their comparison tables. Class checks: 'Do the radio and Bluetooth columns agree with what we know from T2 Week 1? Is there anything in the Bluetooth column that surprises you?'
15
Maths check: 'If Bluetooth has a range of 10 metres and radio has a range of 70 metres, how many times further can radio communicate? If our classroom is 12 metres long, which technology works for us today? What if we needed to communicate between two classrooms 25 metres apart?' Pairs calculate and respond.
16
Bridge to next session: 'You now know every sensor and every communication method on the Micro:bit v2 hardware. Next week we review everything you have built across all three terms before the final project and Demo Day.'
WRAP-UP – Save and Collect (2 min)
17
Rename project 'T3W5_[Name]_Bluetooth'. Disconnect Micro:bit. Return phones/tablets to the facilitator — check all have been unpaired and the Micro:bit app closed.
18
Collect comparison tables and technical explanation writing frames — both are formally assessed artifacts. Facilitator checks: all 4 rows of the comparison table completed for both columns; 4-sentence explanation written with required vocabulary words.
Assessment
Formative (During)
Observation: Does the Bluetooth programme show the tick icon when paired and the cross icon when disconnected? (connection event handlers check)
Does a live temperature reading appear on the paired phone/tablet via the Micro:bit app? (Bluetooth broadcast functionality check)
Comparison table completed with all 4 rows filled for both Radio and Bluetooth columns? (worksheet check)
4-sentence technical explanation written with the words 'paired', 'wireless', 'range', and 'protocol' all present? (vocabulary-in-use check)
Exit Assessment
Facilitator asks each learner: ① 'Why does enabling Bluetooth disable the radio on the Micro:bit?' ② 'What was the temperature reading on the paired device — was it higher or lower than expected? Why?' ③ 'Name one situation where you would choose radio over Bluetooth, and one where you would choose Bluetooth over radio.' 3 points per learner — recorded on assessment log as the week's formally weighted artifact.
Differentiation
Support: Pre-pair the Micro:bit with the phone before the session so learner only needs to build the programme and confirm the temperature reading appears. Reduce the comparison table to 2 rows (range and use case only). Provide sentence completion frames for all 4 technical explanation sentences. Extension: Programme the Micro:bit to broadcast BOTH temperature AND accelerometer strength alternately over Bluetooth using a forever loop that switches between bluetooth temperature service and bluetooth accelerometer service every 5 seconds. Write a paragraph comparing what the app displays for each service and explain what each reading represents physically.
Key Vocabulary
Bluetooth · radio · wireless · paired · UUID · antenna · range · service · broadcast · protocol · connection event · disconnect · extension · communication
Safeguarding Note: Bluetooth pairing requires the Micro:bit to be in pairing mode — this involves holding both buttons simultaneously, which must be modelled by the facilitator before learners attempt it. Ensure no learner pairs their Micro:bit to a personal device without facilitator supervision. All phones and tablets used are facilitator-owned programme devices. Learners must not download or access any apps beyond the official Micro:bit app. Bluetooth communications in this session broadcast sensor data only — no personal data, names, or identifying information is transmitted.
