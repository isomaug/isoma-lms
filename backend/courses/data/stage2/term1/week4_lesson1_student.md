# Week 4 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Add a second character to the Stack KIDI stage and give it a different name, colour, and starting position
- Write a separate 4-block sequence for each of the two characters
- Describe how both characters' programmes run at the same time when the Green Flag is pressed
- Evaluate whether the two characters' movements look like they are "interacting" and suggest one improvement

## Big idea
In Stack KIDI, each character has its OWN sequence of blocks - they run at the same time. Designing two characters to interact requires planning BOTH programmes before coding either.

## What you need
- 4 tablets with Stack KIDI (you' own projects from previous lessons)

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Narrative with two characters: every story needs at least two characters to create INTERACTION. You write a one-sentence description of the relationship between their two characters: "Character 1 is ___ and Character 2 is ___. They meet when ___." Vocabulary: character, interact, stage, simultaneous, sequence, independent, programme.
- **Maths:** Coordinates / positions: you mark starting positions of both characters on the stage diagram using a simple 3×3 grid (left/centre/right × top/middle/bottom). This introduces the concept that each character has an independent position (x,y). Count: "How many blocks does Character 1 have? Character 2? Total = ___."

## Let's begin

### HOOK - The Two-Character Drama (8 min)
1. Two-Character Story Planner (A4 for you) - split-page: left = Character 1 programme plan, right = Character 2 programme plan, with a stage diagram in the centre to show starting positions
2. Character design mini-cards (blank A6 cards) - each pair designs both characters with name, colour, and role in the story
3. Block sequence planning strips (blank horizontal strips, 4 squares each) - you draw block icons for each character before coding
4. Interaction evaluation card (A5) - "Does it look like they interact? YES / PARTLY / NOT YET. What would make it better?"
5. Projector/TV for you to model the two-character setup
6. Two yous (or two learner volunteers) stand at opposite sides of the room.
7. You 1 calls out: "I am going to walk forward, stop, then bow." Does it. You 2 calls out: "At the SAME TIME - I am going to run to the centre, spin, then wave."
8. Both perform simultaneously. Ask yourself: "Did they interact? Did they bump into each other? Did their movements feel connected?"

### DESIGN - Plan Both Characters (15 min)
9. Bridge: "Stack KIDI works the same way. Each character runs its own programme at the same time. Our job today is to make two programmes that feel like a STORY - not just two random movements."
10. Show a finished 2-character Stack KIDI project on the projector. Ask: "What does Character 1 do? What does Character 2 do? Do they look like they are in the same story?"
11. Get ready to use Two-Character Story Planners and character design mini-cards.
12. Step 1 (3 min): Each pair decides on a story (e.g. a cat chases a mouse; a robot delivers a gift to a friend; a child and their dog play in a park).
13. Step 2 (4 min): Design both characters on the mini-cards - name, colour, role. Agree: which is Character 1, which is Character 2?
14. Step 3 (4 min): On the stage diagram in the centre of the planner, mark where each character starts using initials (C1 and C2) on the 3×3 grid. They should NOT start at the same position.
15. Step 4 (4 min): On the block planning strips, draw 4 block icons for each character.
16. At least one block must be different between the two characters.
