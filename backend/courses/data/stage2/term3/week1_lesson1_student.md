# Week 1 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Work in a group of 4 to produce a collaborative project brief with clearly assigned responsibilities for each pair
- Design two sub-systems (one for you) that are independently buildable AND can be integrated
- Define the interface between the two sub-systems: what message, page, or shared variable connects them
- Document the collaboration process: who decided what, how disagreements were resolved, and what the final agreement is

## Big idea
Real software is built by teams. Each person or team builds one part. Then the parts are connected. The most important skill in team programming is designing the INTERFACE between parts - so that they connect cleanly.

## What you need
- 4 tablets - NOT used today (design session)

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Collaborative language and negotiation: "I suggest ___ because ___." "I agree with ___ but I think ___." "What if we ___?" You must use at least one of these structures during the design session. The Negotiation Record requires writing: "We disagreed about ___ and resolved it by ___." Vocabulary: collaborate, negotiate, interface, sub-system, integrate, assign, responsibility, agree, resolve.
- **Maths:** Workload distribution: the total programme will have 4 sub-problems. If each pair builds 2 sub-problems, each pair has a 50% workload share. But if Sub-system A is more complex (6 blocks) than Sub-system B (3 blocks), is the workload still equal? You calculate total estimated build time and check whether the split is fair.

## Let's begin

### HOOK - Term 3 Welcome and The Integration Challenge (8 min)
1. Collaborative Project Brief (A3 per group of 4): Title / Concept (2 sentences) / Sub-system A design (by Pair 1) / Sub-system B design (by Pair 2) / Interface specification (how A and B connect) / Integrated success criteria (4 items for the whole project) / Workload split (who builds what)
2. Interface design card (A5 per group): "Sub-system A sends ___ to Sub-system B when ___. Sub-system B responds by ___." (You fill in the blanks - this is the contract between the two you)
3. Negotiation record (A5 per group): 3 rows - Decision made / Who agreed / How disagreement was resolved
4. Sub-system design strip (A5 for you, 2 per group): each pair's independent mini-plan - blocks, states, sounds - that they will build in the build week
5. Term 3 Complexity poster (A3 on wall): shows the progression of programme complexity from Week 1 Term 1 (1 character, 2 blocks) to Week 1 Term 3 (team project, 4 sub-systems)
6. Welcome you to Term 3. Display the Term 3 Complexity poster. "Look at where you started. Look at where you are now. This term - you build as a TEAM."

### DESIGN - Collaborative Project Brief (42 min)
7. Show two Stack KIDI projects on two separate tablets. "These are two separate projects. Can they be one programme?" Show the challenge: they need to connect - Sub-system A sends a message that triggers Sub-system B on a different page.
8. "This is what software teams do every day. Each team member builds their part independently. Then the parts are connected. The hardest part is not the building - it is making sure the parts FIT together."
9. Groups of 4 form: Pair 1 and Pair 2 from adjacent desks combine. Each group introduces themselves formally: "We are the ___ development team."
10. CONCEPT (5 min): Group of 4 agrees on a concept in 5 minutes. One sentence each: "I think the project should be ___." Vote. Record the winning concept on the brief. This is non-negotiable once decided - no changing after the 5-minute window.
11. SUB-SYSTEM ASSIGNMENT (5 min): Decide which pair builds Sub-system A and which builds Sub-system B. Write the assignment on the brief. Sign next to your assignment.
