# Lesson 20  ·  Term 3 · Week 4

**Duration:** 60 minutes (split across Week 4 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 3 · Week 4
**Age Range:** 7–8 years
**Hardware:** Tablets with Stack KIDI App
**Bloom's:** L4 Analyse · L5 Evaluate · L6 Create

**Big Idea:** True mastery is not what you can build with help — it is what you can demonstrate, explain, and modify alone. Today each learner proves what they personally know, regardless of what their pair built together.

**Source plan:** `Stage2_Lesson20_Term3_Week4.docx`

## Learning Objectives

- Independently demonstrate 6 computing concepts by showing and explaining them in a programme (from any term) without assistance
- Make a live programme modification under timed conditions: a specific change announced by the facilitator 2 minutes before the assessment begins
- Select and present a "best work" portfolio piece from any term and explain why it represents their strongest computational thinking
- Complete a year-long self-assessment locating personal progress across all 20+ skills taught from Term 1 Week 1 to Term 3 Week 4

## Materials and Preparation

- 4 tablets with Stack KIDI — all three terms' project files accessible

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** The most demanding oral assessment of the year: each learner must explain 6 technical concepts in their own words — without reading from notes. "An algorithm is ___. Here is an example in my programme: ___." This is the computing equivalent of a comprehension test — can you explain what you know in clear, accurate English? Vocabulary: demonstrate, explain, independent, evidence, justify, select, reflect, mastery.

**Maths:** Year-long self-assessment calculation: 22 skills × 3 maximum = 66 total possible points. Sum the self-assessment scores. Calculate percentage: (total ÷ 66) × 100 = ___%. Compare to the facilitator's assessment scores on the Mastery Assessment Card. Are they close? If significantly different, what does that tell us about calibration of self-assessment over the year?

## Lesson Structure

### HOOK — Individual Work Day (5 min)
1. Individual Mastery Assessment Card (A4, 1 per learner): 6 Computing Concept rows — Algorithm / Loop / Debug / Variable / Message / Decomposition — each with: "Show me an example in your programme" + "Explain it in one sentence" + Facilitator score (0/1/2)
2. Live Modification Envelope (1 per learner, sealed): opened 2 minutes before the 1-to-1 assessment. Contains a modification task: e.g. "Add a loop to any existing programme that did not have one" or "Change a variable to make the game harder"
3. Portfolio Selection Card (A5 per learner): "My best work from this year is ___ (project name and term). I chose it because ___. The most complex computational thinking in it is ___."
4. Year-Long Skills Self-Assessment (A4 per learner): all 22 skills listed from Terms 1–3 in order. Learner scores each: 3 = can explain and build independently / 2 = can build with some support / 1 = still developing / 0 = not yet started
5. Portfolio folder (per learner): all design documents, evaluation grids, passports, and rubrics from Terms 1–3 collected here for review
6. Facilitator announces: "Today is individual. No pairs, no groups. Everything today is YOUR work, YOUR understanding, YOUR explanation."

### DELIVER — 1-to-1 Mastery Assessments (40 min)
7. Distribute all materials. Learners have 5 minutes to review their project files on the tablet — any project from any term may be used in the assessment.
8. Facilitator: "I will call each of you one at a time for a 10-minute 1-to-1 assessment. While waiting, complete the Year-Long Skills Self-Assessment and write the Portfolio Selection Card."
9. Learners who are not in the 1-to-1 assessment work silently on their self-assessment and portfolio selection for the duration.
10. Facilitator calls learners one at a time (approximately 8 minutes each, 5 learners in 40 minutes — remaining 3 learners assessed in a follow-up slot if needed).
11. ENVELOPE OPEN (2 min): learner opens their Live Modification Envelope. Reads the modification task. Has 2 minutes to plan the change before demonstrating it.
12. LIVE MODIFICATION (3 min): learner makes the modification live on the tablet. Facilitator observes — does not help.

### DISCOVER — Whole-Class Reflection (10 min)
13. CONCEPT DEMONSTRATION (4 min): Facilitator selects 4 of the 6 concepts from the Mastery Assessment Card to ask about today. For each: "Show me an example in a programme. Explain it in one sentence." Learner demonstrates and explains. Facilitator scores each.
14. PORTFOLIO PIECE (1 min): learner shares their Portfolio Selection Card and runs the selected project. 30 seconds max.
15. While 1-to-1 assessments continue, other learners complete the Year-Long Skills Self-Assessment independently.
16. After all assessments: class sits together. Facilitator asks three questions — hands up for responses:
17. "Which skill from Term 1 do you STILL use almost every lesson?" (e.g. algorithm, sequence)
18. "Which skill from Term 3 still feels most challenging?"

### WRAP-UP (5 min)
19. "If you could teach ONE thing you learned this year to a learner who is just starting — what would it be? Why?"
20. Each learner reads their Portfolio Selection Card reason aloud in one sentence.
21. Year-Long Skills Self-Assessment collected. Percentage calculated and recorded on the front cover of the Portfolio Folder.
22. Facilitator announces: "Lessons 21–23 are the final polish weeks. Lesson 24 is the End-of-Year Showcase — the largest Demo Day of the programme."
23. Each learner writes one sentence about what they want to achieve in the End-of-Year Showcase: "In the final showcase, I want the audience to understand ___."

## Assessment

- Formative (During)
- Mastery Assessment Card: facilitator scores for 4 of 6 concepts demonstrated live (target: minimum 5/8 = 62%)
- Live Modification: completed independently within time limit (YES/NO)
- Portfolio Selection Card: specific justification for choice (not "because it was fun")
- Year-Long Skills Self-Assessment: completed for all 22 skills — scores reviewed by facilitator
- Exit / Summative
- Facilitator compares learner self-assessment percentage to own Mastery Assessment Card scores. If self-assessment is significantly higher than facilitator score — learner is over-estimating. If significantly lower — learner is under-estimating. Both are discussed briefly and honestly with each learner in the 1-to-1. Target: realistic self-assessment that matches evidence.
- Differentiation
- Support: Reduce Mastery Assessment to 3 concepts (algorithm, loop, debug). Simplify the Live Modification to a pre-agreed change that the learner has practised. Extension: Learner produces a 1-minute "Computing Concept Video" by recording their screen and voice (if device supports it) explaining their best project. This becomes a showcase artefact for parents.
