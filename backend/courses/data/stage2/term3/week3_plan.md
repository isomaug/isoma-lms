# Lesson 19  ·  Term 3 · Week 3

**Duration:** 60 minutes (split across Week 3 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 3 · Week 3
**Age Range:** 7–8 years
**Hardware:** Tablets with Stack KIDI App
**Bloom's:** L4 Analyse · L5 Evaluate · L6 Create

**Big Idea:** Polishing a team project is different from polishing a solo project. Every decision must be agreed. Every change must be tested. The most powerful polish is the one that the whole team agrees makes the project better.

**Source plan:** `Stage2_Lesson19_Term3_Week3.docx`

## Learning Objectives

- Apply a 7-item team polish checklist to the integrated project, achieving a minimum of 6/7 items ticked
- Assign a specific speaking role to each of the four team members for the Demo Day presentation
- Rehearse a 2-minute team presentation where each member speaks for approximately 30 seconds about their part
- Score the integrated project against all 4 integrated success criteria and produce a final self-evaluation score

## Materials and Preparation

- 2 tablets per group of 4 - integrated project files from Lesson 18
- Team Polish Checklist (A5 per group): 7 items –
- ☐ Both sub-systems run correctly
- ☐ Interface triggers cleanly
- ☐ No sound clashes between sub-systems
- ☐ Backgrounds visually distinct between pages
- ☐ Title screen present
- ☐ End sequence present
- ☐ Full programme runs without facilitator intervention

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** Team presentation structure: each of the 4 team members has a specific and non-overlapping speaking role. The group must ensure that all four parts connect smoothly - no repetition, no gaps. Pairs practise the handover: "I will now pass over to ___ who will explain ___." Vocabulary: handover, transition, integration, demonstrate, explain, evaluate, contribute, collaborate.

**Maths:** Final evaluation: 4 criteria × 2 points each = 8 maximum. Sum scores. Convert to percentage. Compare this term's collaborative project score to the individual project scores from Term 2. Is the collaborative score higher or lower? What factors explain the difference?

## Lesson Structure

### HOOK - The Premiere (6 min)
1. Presentation Role Assignment Card (A5 per group): 4 roles - Introducer (title + concept) / Sub-system A Explainer / Sub-system B Explainer / Integration Explainer + Self-Evaluator. Each learner writes their name next to their role
2. Team Rehearsal Feedback Card (A5, used by facilitator): "Person 1 - clear? YES/PARTLY/NO. Vocabulary word used? ___. One tip: ___." One row per team member
3. Final Evaluation Grid (A4 per group): 4 integrated success criteria × 3-point scale (0/1/2) + group-level reflection section
4. Facilitator: "Tonight is the premiere. Tomorrow the public sees your project. Today is the last chance to make it perfect."
5. Each group runs their integrated project from start to finish - silently. Facilitator watches. Notes anything that needs fixing on the Team Polish Checklist.
6. Groups receive the blank checklist. Together, they run the project again and tick each item themselves. Any unticked item = a fix that must happen in the build phase.

### DELIVER - Team Polish Sprint (25 min)
7. Rule: "Every fix today must be agreed by the whole group before it is made. No individual changes without discussion."
8. Groups work on their one tablet. Build polish items systematically from the checklist. Each fix agreed verbally before implementing.
9. Item 1 - Sub-system coherence (4 min): run each sub-system independently. Any issue remaining from Lesson 18 - fix now. Tick.
10. Item 2 - Interface clean run (3 min): run the full programme. Interface fires correctly? Tick.
11. Item 3 - Sound design pass (4 min): listen to all sounds in sequence. Any clash? Adjust volume positions. Tick.
12. Item 4 - Visual consistency (4 min): does the visual style feel coherent across both sub-systems? One small colour or background adjustment if needed. Tick.

### DISCOVER - Presentation Role Assignment and Rehearsal (20 min)
13. Item 5 - Title screen (4 min): does a title screen exist? If not, add a minimal one - background + game/story name as a scrolling character + tap to start. Tick.
14. Item 6 - End sequence (3 min): does the programme end with a clear completion signal (sound, animation, or visual)? If not, add one. Tick.
15. Item 7 - Independent run test (3 min): facilitator presses Green Flag on the untouched integrated tablet. Does the programme run start to finish without any help? If yes - tick. Final Polish Checklist score calculated: ___/7.
16. Distribute Presentation Role Assignment Cards. Group of 4 assigns roles: no one may refuse a speaking role. Negotiate assignments in 2 minutes.
17. Solo preparation (3 min): each learner writes 2–3 bullet points for their 30-second section. What will you say? What part of the programme will you point to?
18. Rehearsal Round 1 (4 min): full 2-minute team presentation. Facilitator times. After: each team member receives one tip from the facilitator on the Team Rehearsal Feedback Card.

### WRAP-UP (4 min)
19. Rehearsal Round 2 (4 min): implement the tip. Repeat the 2-minute presentation. Facilitator notes improvement.
20. Final Evaluation Grid completed (4 min): group agrees on a consensus score for each of the 4 integrated success criteria. Each person must agree before the score is written. Calculate total and percentage.
21. Save final polished project as "FINAL POLISHED - [Group Names]."
22. Each learner writes: "My speaking role is ___. The most important thing I will say is ___."
23. Preview: "Demo Day next lesson. Guests will hear your team present AND play your project. Every person speaks. Every person is prepared."

## Assessment

- Formative (During)
- Team Polish Checklist: minimum 6/7 items ticked for Demo Day clearance
- Presentation Role Assignment Card: all 4 roles assigned, all 4 learners have bullet points
- Rehearsal Round 2: improvement from Round 1 observed (facilitator Feedback Card)
- Final Evaluation Grid: consensus score recorded, total and percentage calculated
- Exit / Summative
- Facilitator runs the programme on the integration tablet once more - does it pass item 7 of the Polish Checklist (runs without help)? If YES: Demo Day ready. If NO: facilitator identifies the single highest-priority remaining issue and fixes it with the group after the session.
- Differentiation
- Support: Simplify the speaking role to one sentence only ("My name is ___ and I built the ___"). Facilitator scaffolds the sentence by asking questions the learner answers aloud. Extension: Prepare an additional "technical deep dive" card that can be offered to any guest who asks a follow-up question - covering one specific computing concept in detail (e.g. "Here is how our interface message works").

## Safeguarding

Safeguarding Note: Presenting in a group reduces individual anxiety but can create pressure for quieter learners. Remind all groups: "Every speaking role is equally important. The person who explains the integration is not more important than the person who introduces the title." Give shy learners the Introducer role - it is the shortest and most clearly scripted.
