# Lesson 24  ·  Term 3 · Week 8    END-OF-YEAR FINAL SHOWCASE

**Duration:** 90 minutes (extended session) (split across Week 8 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 3 · Week 8
**Age Range:** 7–8 years
**Hardware:** Tablets with Stack KIDI App
**Bloom's:** L4 Analyse · L5 Evaluate · L6 Create

**Big Idea:** Every line of code you wrote this year started as a thought in your head. You turned a thought into a plan, a plan into a programme, a programme into a story or a game, and today - a story or game into a public achievement. That journey from thought to audience is what every software engineer, game designer, and digital artist does for their career. You have done it. In Year 2. Completely.

**Source plan:** `Stage2_Lesson24_Term3_Week8.docx`

## Learning Objectives

- Deliver the full 2-minute team presentation to multiple rounds of community guests, maintaining quality and confidence across all rounds
- Handle a panel Q&A from community guests - including at least one question about the AI adaptive response - using subject vocabulary and programme evidence
- Facilitate a community guest playing the collaborative programme and narrate their experience in real time
- Complete the Stage 2 Year Completion Assessment and receive the Stage 2 Certificate

## Materials and Preparation

- 2 tablets per group - AI-Enhanced Final Project (locked from Lesson 21)
- Technical Portfolio Folders (per learner, sealed from Lesson 22)
- Personalised Showcase Briefing Cards from Lesson 23 (facilitator prepared)

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** The highest-level English performance task of the stage: a team public presentation with panel Q&A that demands technical vocabulary, logical explanation, narrative coherence, and interpersonal confidence - simultaneously. The community audience includes non-technical adults who require analogies and accessible language. The skill of "explaining computing to a non-specialist" is one of the most in-demand professional skills in Uganda's growing tech sector. Vocabulary: algorithm, state, decompose, integrate, adaptive, iterate, playtest, evidence, achievement, legacy.

**Maths:** Class-level statistics for the Community Impact Statement: facilitator calculates total lessons attended, total programmes built, total bugs debugged (estimated from Bug Reports and Debug Logs across all three terms), total playtests conducted, and total Demo Days. These are presented as a data-driven narrative of the year. Individual learners calculate their personal rubric average across all three terms.

## Lesson Structure

### SETUP - The Professional Exhibition (15 min)
1. Final Showcase Rubric (A4 per learner, scored jointly by facilitator and an invited technical assessor): 7 criteria - Programme Runs Completely / All 4 Members Speak / Algorithm Explained / Collaborative Design Described / AI Feature Explained / Q&A Handling / Confidence and Clarity - each 0–3 (max 21)
2. Community Guest Observation Forms (A5, 2 per guest): "The team that impressed me most was ___ because ___. One computing concept I now understand: ___. My question for the team: ___."
3. Stage 2 Year Completion Certificate (A5, pre-printed per learner): "This certifies that ___ has completed Stage 2 of the Isoma Edutech Design · Deliver · Discover Programme, demonstrating advanced computational thinking, collaborative programme design, software testing, and AI awareness across three full terms."
4. Year Reflection Scroll (A4, rolled and tied with a ribbon per learner): learner's own Year-Long Progress Narrative from Lesson 22, printed and prepared as a keepsake
5. Community Impact Statement (A3 poster, displayed): prepared by facilitator - "This class built ___ programmes, attended ___ lessons, fixed ___ bugs, playtested ___ times, and presented at ___ Demo Days. They are ready for Stage 3."
6. Room set up as a professional technology exhibition: 2 team stations with tablets, portfolio folders open, programme screenshots displayed, Community Impact Statement poster visible.
7. Learners arrive to find Personalised Showcase Briefing Cards at their stations. Each learner reads their card silently.

### DELIVER - The Final Showcase (45 min)
8. Final device checks: both tablets running. AI adaptive response tested. Facilitator confirms: "Both branches fire correctly? Title screen loads? Win state triggers?" Only YES answers proceed.
9. Facilitator walks the line: one personal encouragement per learner. Specific, true, personal.
10. Rule reminder: "If a question is about your partner pair's sub-system - you still answer it. You are ONE team. You are responsible for knowing the whole programme."
11. Guests welcomed. Facilitator address: "Stage 2 learners have spent this year building programmes that use algorithms, loops, variables, messages, and AI-style adaptive responses. They built alone and together. They tested with real users, iterated, and polished. Each team today will present their work and answer your questions. They have earned this."
12. Rotation 1 (10 min): guests divided between both team stations. Teams deliver 2-minute presentation. Invited technical assessor at one station, facilitator at the other.
13. Rotation 2 (10 min): guests swap stations.
14. Community Guest Play Phase (10 min): a community member is invited to PLAY the programme while the team narrates: "You are now in the ___ state. The score variable is currently at ___. When you reach ___, watch what the adaptive response does."

### DISCOVER - Ceremony, Reflection, and Legacy (25 min)
15. Rotation 3 and 4 (15 min): remaining guest groups cycle through. Facilitator and assessor complete Final Showcase Rubrics.
16. Guests seated. Community Impact Statement read aloud by the facilitator.
17. Facilitator invites 2 community guests to share their observation forms: "What computing concept do you now understand that you did not this morning?"
18. Year Reflection: each learner reads their Paragraph 5 sentence ("When I begin Stage 3, I am ready to ___ because ___") aloud.
19. Stage 2 Learning Contracts read back: each learner reads their two Stage 2 goals aloud. Then says: "I set these goals at the start of Term 3. By achieving ___/2, I know I am ready for what comes next."
20. Certificate ceremony: each learner called by name. Receives Year Completion Certificate and Year Reflection Scroll. Facilitator says one specific, evidence-based sentence about each learner's year.

### WRAP-UP (5 min)
21. Year Reflection Scrolls signed by a community guest as witnesses.
22. Class close: standing. Each learner says one sentence: "This year I proved that ___." Facilitator adds: "This year, YOU proved that." Full stop.
23. Thank and farewell guests. Collect all observation forms.
24. Return Technical Portfolio Folders and Year Reflection Scrolls to learners for families.
25. Save and back up all final project files.
26. Facilitator post-session: complete all Final Showcase Rubrics. Write Stage 2 individual progress notes for Stage 3 handover. Celebrate the year with the class.

## Assessment

- Formative (During)
- Final Showcase Rubric: 7 criteria × 3 = 21 max. Score per learner recorded by facilitator + assessor
- AI adaptive response explained to at least one guest using correct vocabulary
- Community Guest Play narrated in real time (all 4 members contributed)
- Stage 2 Learning Contract read back and progress against goals stated publicly
- Exit / Summative
- Stage 2 Year Completion Assessment: facilitator completes a final individual record per learner:
- ① Strongest computational thinking skill demonstrated
- ② Stage 3 readiness: Distinction (≥85% mean rubric score across all three terms) / Merit (≥70%) / Pass (≥55%)
- ③ One personal note for the Stage 3 facilitator handover. This document is the most important educational record produced this year.
- Differentiation
- Support: Learner presents only the Title and Story Summary section of the team presentation (20 seconds). Other team members cover the rest. Learner still participates in Q&A with one prepared answer. Extension: Learner writes and delivers a 90-second closing address to all guests at the end of the ceremony - summarising what the whole class achieved this year and why it matters for Uganda's future. This is an advanced public speaking task that requires synthesis of the entire year.

## Safeguarding

Safeguarding Note: As for all large public events: all guests registered, photography consent obtained, facilitator present throughout, no learner alone with an adult at any time. The Year Reflection Scrolls contain personal reflective writing - do not display or read these without explicit learner consent. Certificate documents should note that they are educational records, not legal qualifications. Store all assessment records securely and in line with the school's data protection policy.
