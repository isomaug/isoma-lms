# Lesson 23  ·  Term 3 · Week 7

**Duration:** 60 minutes (split across Week 7 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 3 · Week 7
**Age Range:** 7–8 years
**Hardware:** Tablets with Stack KIDI App
**Bloom's:** L4 Analyse · L5 Evaluate · L6 Create

**Big Idea:** A team presentation requires that every person is prepared, every handover is smooth, and every member is ready to answer any question — not just the questions about their own part. Real teams rehearse together until the whole is greater than the sum of its parts.

**Source plan:** `Stage2_Lesson23_Term3_Week7.docx`

## Learning Objectives

- Deliver the complete 2-minute team presentation with each of the four members speaking their assigned section without overlap or gaps
- Handle a panel Q&A where any team member can be asked any question about any part of the programme
- Evaluate the team presentation against a 6-criteria rubric and agree on a consensus team score
- Identify one specific improvement for each team member to implement before the Final Showcase

## Materials and Preparation

- 2 tablets per group — final polished collaborative project + AI-enhanced version from Lesson 21
- Team Presentation Structure Cards from Lesson 19 (updated with AI feature section)

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** Team presentation as a genre distinct from individual presentation: the handover is a genre convention — "I will now pass to ___ who will explain ___." Learners must practise handovers until they are seamless. The panel Q&A requires each member to listen carefully to questions addressed to other members, in case the question crosses sub-system boundaries. Vocabulary: panel, Q&A, handover, seamless, consensus, evidence, technical accuracy, confident, pacing.

**Maths:** Consensus scoring: each of 4 team members scores the presentation on 6 criteria (each 0–3). Calculate the individual totals and the team average. "Member 1 total: ___/18. Member 2: ___/18. Member 3: ___/18. Member 4: ___/18. Team average: ___/18." How close are the individual scores? Standard deviation concept introduced informally: "How much do your scores disagree?"

## Lesson Structure

### SETUP AND BRIEFING (7 min)
1. Dress Rehearsal Observation Sheet (A4, 1 per invited audience member): "Which team member spoke most clearly? What computing vocabulary did you hear? What question would you ask this team? How good was the programme?  (1–5)"
2. Panel Q&A Card Bank (A5, per facilitator): 15 questions ranging from ① straightforward ("How many pages does your programme have?") to ⑤ advanced ("Explain how the adaptive response works and what makes it similar to AI")
3. Team Consensus Rubric (A4 per group): 6 criteria — all 4 members score independently, then average for consensus. Criteria: Handover smoothness / All 4 voices / Technical accuracy / Vocabulary depth / Q&A handling / Confidence and pacing
4. Individual Improvement Note (A6 per learner): "Before the Final Showcase I will ___. This will make the presentation better because ___."
5. Room arranged: 2 team presentation stations (one per group of 4), with tablets, portfolio folders, and programme display materials.
6. Groups update Presentation Structure Cards: Section 3 (technical choice) must now include the AI adaptive response feature.
7. Invited audience arrives. Facilitator brief: "These teams will present their collaborative programme and answer your questions as a panel. Please ask any question — including difficult ones. They have prepared for this."

### DELIVER — Team Dress Rehearsal Presentations (24 min)
8. Final device check: both tablets in each group run the programme correctly. AI adaptive response tested one final time.
9. Group 1 presents (2 minutes + 3-minute Q&A = 5 minutes per group per rotation).
10. During presentation: invited audience completes Observation Sheets. Facilitator tracks: which members spoke, handover smoothness, vocabulary count, Q&A quality.
11. Q&A phase: facilitator selects 3 questions from the Panel Q&A Card Bank for Group 1. Directs each question to a DIFFERENT team member than the one who built that part — cross-team knowledge tested.
12. Group 2 presents (same format).
13. Rotation: audience moves. Both groups present again to a different audience subset.

### DISCOVER — Team Consensus Evaluation and Improvement (20 min)
14. Throughout: facilitator notes one specific improvement action per team member on a private note.
15. Invited audience leaves. Groups collect Observation Sheets.
16. Individual scoring (4 min): each team member scores the presentation independently on all 6 criteria.
17. Consensus discussion (5 min): share scores. Where do team members agree? Where do they disagree? Negotiate a consensus score for each criterion.
18. Calculate team average. Identify the lowest-scoring criterion. This is the team's single improvement priority.
19. Individual Improvement Notes (4 min): each team member writes their personal improvement for the Final Showcase.

### WRAP-UP (9 min)
20. Facilitator shares private notes (one per team member). Each learner reads theirs, signs it, and attaches it to their portfolio.
21. One improvement rehearsal (7 min): each group rehearses the specific handover or section identified as weakest. Facilitator confirms improvement observed.
22. Save final versions of both AI-enhanced tablets. Confirm both run from start to finish independently.
23. Collect all Observation Sheets, Consensus Rubrics, and Individual Improvement Notes for portfolios.
24. Facilitator to each group: one specific personal encouragement per team member.
25. Preview: "Next lesson is the End-of-Year Final Showcase. Your Technical Portfolios will be displayed. Your programme will run in front of the whole community. Every person on this team has something to be proud of."

## Assessment

- Formative (During)
- All 4 team members spoke during the rehearsal (facilitator observation)
- Panel Q&A: cross-team questions handled (at least one question answered about the other pair's sub-system)
- Team Consensus Rubric: individual and consensus scores recorded
- Individual Improvement Notes: specific, personal, and signed
- Exit / Summative
- Facilitator's Dress Rehearsal Log: one row per team member — overall readiness rating (Ready / Nearly Ready / Needs Support). Any learner rated "Needs Support" receives a private pre-Final Showcase preparation conversation, not in front of the group.
- Differentiation
- Support: Assign the learner the Introducer role — the shortest, most scripted section. Facilitator provides a complete written script for that section. Learner practises reading it naturally (not robotically). Extension: Learner prepares a supplementary "Deep Technical Explanation" card for the most complex feature (the AI adaptive response). This is offered only to audience members who ask follow-up questions about the AI element.
