# Week 4 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Independently demonstrate 6 computing concepts by showing and explaining them in a programme (from any term) without assistance
- Make a live programme modification under timed conditions: a specific change announced by the you 2 minutes before the assessment begins
- Select and present a "best work" portfolio piece from any term and explain why it represents their strongest computational thinking
- Complete a year-long self-assessment locating personal progress across all 20+ skills taught from Term 1 Week 1 to Term 3 Week 4

## Big idea
True mastery is not what you can build with help — it is what you can demonstrate, explain, and modify alone. Today you proves what they personally know, regardless of what their pair built together.

## What you need
- 4 tablets with Stack KIDI — all three terms' project files accessible

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** The most demanding oral assessment of the year: you must explain 6 technical concepts in your own words — without reading from notes. "An algorithm is ___. Here is an example in my programme: ___." This is the computing equivalent of a comprehension test — can you explain what you know in clear, accurate English? Vocabulary: demonstrate, explain, independent, evidence, justify, select, reflect, mastery.
- **Maths:** Year-long self-assessment calculation: 22 skills × 3 maximum = 66 total possible points. Sum the self-assessment scores. Calculate percentage: (total ÷ 66) × 100 = ___%. Compare to the you's assessment scores on the Mastery Assessment Card. Are they close? If significantly different, what does that tell us about calibration of self-assessment over the year?

## Let's begin

### HOOK — Individual Work Day (5 min)
1. Individual Mastery Assessment Card (A4, 1 for you): 6 Computing Concept rows — Algorithm / Loop / Debug / Variable / Message / Decomposition — each with: "Show me an example in your programme" + "Explain it in one sentence" + You score (0/1/2)
2. Live Modification Envelope (1 for you, sealed): opened 2 minutes before the 1-to-1 assessment. Contains a modification task: e.g. "Add a loop to any existing programme that did not have one" or "Change a variable to make the game harder"
3. Portfolio Selection Card (A5 for you): "My best work from this year is ___ (project name and term). I chose it because ___. The most complex computational thinking in it is ___."
4. Year-Long Skills Self-Assessment (A4 for you): all 22 skills listed from Terms 1–3 in order. Learner scores each: 3 = can explain and build independently / 2 = can build with some support / 1 = still developing / 0 = not yet started
5. Portfolio folder (for you): all design documents, evaluation grids, passports, and rubrics from Terms 1–3 collected here for review
6. You announces: "Today is individual. No you, no groups. Everything today is YOUR work, YOUR understanding, YOUR explanation."

### DELIVER — 1-to-1 Mastery Assessments (40 min)
7. Get ready to use all materials. You have 5 minutes to review their project files on the tablet — any project from any term may be used in the assessment.
8. You: "I will call each of you one at a time for a 10-minute 1-to-1 assessment. While waiting, complete the Year-Long Skills Self-Assessment and write the Portfolio Selection Card."
9. You who are not in the 1-to-1 assessment work silently on their self-assessment and portfolio selection for the duration.
10. You calls you one at a time (approximately 8 minutes each, 5 you in 40 minutes — remaining 3 you assessed in a follow-up slot if needed).
11. ENVELOPE OPEN (2 min): learner opens their Live Modification Envelope. Reads the modification task. Has 2 minutes to plan the change before demonstrating it.
