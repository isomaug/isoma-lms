# Week 7 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Design 4 structured playtest observation questions before the playtest session begins
- Facilitate a silent playtest observation — watch a player use the game without interfering and record specific behavioural observations (not opinions)
- Analyse playtest feedback and identify the single highest-priority improvement based on frequency of occurrence across multiple testers
- Implement the priority improvement and retest with the same observation question to verify the fix works

## Continue

### DELIVER — Playtest Round (30 min)
1. TESTER 1 (8 min): One member from the adjacent pair plays the game for 5 minutes. Designer pair observes SILENTLY, completing the tally sheet and observation sheet. No speaking unless the tester is completely stuck for 60 seconds. Record all observations factually.
2. After Tester 1 (2 min): Tester 1 verbally describes what they found easy and hard. Designer pair records responses. Tally sheet updated.
3. TESTER 2 (8 min): A learner from a DIFFERENT pair playtests. Same silent observation protocol.
4. After Tester 2 (2 min): Same debrief process.
5. FREQUENCY ANALYSIS (5 min): Review both sets of observations. Complete the Feedback Frequency Tally. Identify the top issue — the one that appears most often across both testers.

### DISCOVER — Fix and Retest (10 min)
6. PRIORITY FIX (5 min): Complete the Priority Improvement Card. Decide: "What is the single most impactful fix we can make in 10 minutes?"
7. Implement the priority fix (8 min). One change only — the highest-priority item from the Improvement Card.
8. Retest (2 min): Ask one of the original testers to replay ONLY the part that was problematic. Observe. Complete the Post-Fix Retest Record.
9. Class debrief: "How many you fixed their priority issue? How many found the fix created a new problem?" (This is normal — real software iterations often introduce regressions.)

### WRAP-UP (4 min)
10. Reflection: "Playtesting taught me that ___. Before I thought my game was ___ but after watching real players I know ___."
11. Save the updated game as "PLAYTESTED GAME — [Pair Names]."
12. File all playtest materials: observation sheets, tally, improvement card, retest record.
13. Preview: "Demo Day next week. Your game will be played by adults and guests who have never seen it before. You will present it just like you observed today — but now YOU are explaining it to THEM."

- Exit / Summative
