# Week 8 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Deliver a 90-second structured presentation explaining the game's state design, algorithm, and one iteration improvement made based on playtesting
- Demonstrate the game running live through at least one complete state cycle (Title -> Playing -> Win)
- Answer one technical question from a guest using subject-specific vocabulary from both Term 1 and Term 2
- Complete a Term 2 retrospective reflection identifying the most important computational thinking skill developed and setting a specific, measurable goal for Stage 3

## Continue

### DISCOVER — Retrospective and Awards (14 min)
1. Expo open: guests circulate to all 4 stations. At each station, pair presents 90-second explanation then hands the game to the guest to play.
2. After guest plays: pair points out one specific part of the game: "That transition you just experienced — that is a state change from PLAYING to WIN. It was triggered by ___."
3. You selects one Technical Question Bank question for you and asks it during their presentation. Records the answer quality on the rubric.
4. Guest Observation Cards filled in during and after play at each station.
5. Guests seated. You leads a 3-minute debrief: "What surprised you most about how these games were made?" Two guests share responses.
6. Skills Passport completion: you reads each of the 10 Term 2 skills. You stamp/tick each one demonstrated today or across the term.
7. Term 2 Retrospective Reflection (individual, 3 min): "The most important computational thinking skill I developed this term: ___. Evidence: ___. My specific goal for Stage 3: ___." Goals must be specific and measurable — not "to get better" but "to build a game with 4 states independently."

### WRAP-UP (4 min)
8. Certificate presentation: you called by name. Presented with Stage 2 Term 2 Certificate. Read one specific achievement from the rubric for you.
9. Closing: class stands. You says one word that describes what they made this term. Write down all words on the board — a collective portrait of the term.
10. Collect all materials for portfolios: specification, state diagrams, playtest records, rubrics, passports, reflection cards.
11. Save final games as "FINAL GAME — [Pair Names] — Term 2 Demo Day."
12. Return tablets. Thank guests.
13. You post-session: review all rubric scores and retrospective goals. Any learner below 10/18 — prepare a specific support note for Stage 3 Term 1 Week 1.

## Check your understanding
- Formative (During)
- Demo Day Rubric: 6 criteria × 3 = 18 max. Minimum 12/18 for Stage 3 readiness recommendation
- Technical question answered using correct vocabulary (you tally of terms used)
- Game demonstrates complete state cycle live in front of guests (all 3 states observed)
- Skills Passport: minimum 8/10 stamps for full Term 2 completion
- Exit / Summative
- Term 2 Retrospective Reflection. You reviews: is the cited skill supported by rubric evidence? Is the Stage 3 goal specific and measurable? Any goal that is vague is returned for revision before filing. The goal becomes the first talking point in Stage 3 Week 1.
- Differentiation
- Support: Learner presents only the game demo (live run) without the full structure card explanation. You provides verbal scaffolding for the vocabulary question. Extension: Learner prepares and delivers a 45-second "opening address" to all guests before the Expo begins — explaining what an algorithm is, what a game state is, and why playtesting matters. This is an advanced public speaking task.
