# Week 5 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About 30 minutes** (you will finish this week in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Connect the Micro:bit temperature sensor in MakeCode and display a live reading on the LED every 10 seconds
- Record 6 temperature readings over time in a paper data table with correct column headers (Time, Temperature °C)
- Plot the 6 readings on a hand-drawn line graph with labelled axes, title, and appropriate scale
- Calculate the mean temperature (sum ÷ 6) and the range (highest − lowest)
- Write a short scientific report: Aim / Method / Results (graph) / Conclusion – minimum 5 sentences
- Discuss one real-world application where a temperature sensor could be useful and explain why

## Big idea
Sensors let computers read the real world. When we record data over time and plot it on a graph, we can see patterns and trends that are invisible in a list of numbers.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits with USB cables (temperature sensor built-in)
- Data table recording sheet – columns: Reading #, Time (min), Temperature (°C)
- Blank graph paper with x-axis and y-axis for a line graph
- Scientific report writing frame: Aim / Method / Results / Conclusion
- Rulers and pencils
- Optional: warm cup or ice pack to vary readings (do not touch other people for heat readings)

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Scientific report writing: Aim / Method / Results (attach graph) / Conclusion. Minimum 5 full sentences. Starters: 'Our aim was to…', 'We found that…', 'The data showed that…'.
- **Maths:** Data table with units (°C). Line graph: time on x-axis, temperature on y-axis; label axes; choose scale. Mean = sum ÷ 6. Range = highest − lowest. Identify trend (rising, falling, stable).

## Let's begin

### HOOK – What Can Your Micro:bit Feel?
1. Can this device feel the temperature in the room right now? Watch a temperature display programme.
2. Where do you see temperature sensors in real life? (fridge, weather app, thermometer, car, air conditioner)
3. Learn the words: sensor, data, reading, mean, range, trend. Give one daily-life example for each.
4. Why do scientists record data in a table instead of only remembering numbers?

### DESIGN – Plan the Experiment
5. Write your Aim: measure the temperature in the room every 2 minutes for 12 minutes and look for a pattern.
6. Write the Method (2–3 sentences): what you will do, what you will record, how you will display results.
7. On graph paper, prepare axes: x = 0–12 minutes, y = about 20–35°C. Add title and labels.
8. Maths warm-up: for readings 23, 24, 24, 25, 25, 26 – what is the mean? What is the range?

### DELIVER – Programme and measure (start)
9. **Step 1:** In MakeCode Input, use temperature (°C). Build button A → show number temperature, or forever → show number → pause 10000 ms → clear. Download.
10. **Step 2:** Every 2 minutes, read the LED temperature and record Reading #, Time, Temperature. Collect 6 readings.
