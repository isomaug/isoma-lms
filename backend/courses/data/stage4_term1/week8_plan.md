# Lesson 8: Demo Day – RoboCreator Showcase

**Duration:** 60 minutes (split across Week 8 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 1 · Week 8
**Age Range:** 8–10 years
**Hardware:** BBC Micro:bit v2, USB cable, shared laptops
**Bloom's:** L3–L6 Applying / Analysing / Evaluating / Creating
**DDD Phase:** DISCOVER – Final fixes, structured 3-minute presentations, peer feedback, scoring, and certificate award

**Big Idea:** A great engineer does not just build something – they explain it, show it working, reflect on what they learned, and say what they would improve. Presenting your work clearly is a skill just as important as coding it.

## Learning Objectives

- Make final improvements to the Week 7 project based on peer review feedback
- Deliver a structured 3-minute oral presentation: Problem → Solution → Demo → What I Learned → What I Would Improve
- Demonstrate the live Micro:bit programme running without help
- State and explain at least one numerical or mathematical element of the project to the audience
- Complete a peer feedback rubric for at least one other presenter
- Receive the RoboCreator Certificate upon meeting the pass threshold (≥ 55% overall weighted score)

## Materials and Preparation

- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits with USB cables (projects pre-loaded from Week 7)
- Presentation structure card – 5 sections with sentence starters
- Facilitator assessment rubric (DDD Part 8.5)
- Peer feedback rubric cards
- RoboCreator Certificates
- Projected screen or TV

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** Structured oral presentation (about 3 minutes) with all 5 sections. Sentence starters: 'The problem I noticed was…', 'My solution works by…', 'As you can see from the demo…', 'The most important thing I learned was…', 'If I had more time, I would…'.

**Maths:** State and explain one numerical element connected to the code (for example trigger temperature, loop counts, or scene timing fractions).

## Lesson Structure

### FINAL FIXES – Prepare and Rehearse
1. Review your Week 7 design brief and peer feedback.
2. Make final improvements to your project based on that feedback.
3. Check the programme is downloaded and running on the Micro:bit.
4. Practise the 5-section presentation using sentence starters:
   - Problem: The problem I noticed was…
   - Solution: My solution works by…
   - Demo: As you can see from the demo…
   - Learned: The most important thing I learned was…
   - Improve: If I had more time, I would…
5. Practise your maths statement: One mathematical element in my project is…
6. Technical check: confirm your Micro:bit programme runs before presentations begin.

### PRESENTATIONS – RoboCreator Showcase
1. Present for up to 3 minutes (+ 1 minute for questions). Cover all 5 sections in order.
2. At Demo, run the programme live. Then explain one maths element with a number, calculation, or data point.
3. While others present, complete a written peer feedback rubric (at least one sentence, not only ticks).

### DEBRIEF – Reflect and Celebrate
4. Reflect as a group: What coding concept did many projects use? What maths concept appeared more than once?
5. Read your peer feedback. Write: Based on feedback, if I built Version 2, I would…
6. Celebrate the effort: reading briefs, designing before building, using sensors, and presenting.

### CERTIFICATE CEREMONY
7. Learners who engaged, completed artifacts, and presented demonstrate being a RoboCreator.
8. Receive your RoboCreator Certificate when you meet the pass threshold.
9. Look ahead: Stage 5–6 moves toward text-based programming. Skills from this term go with you – reading code, designing before building, sensors, and presenting.

## Assessment

- Summative project (35%): functionality, coding depth, English connection, maths connection (DDD 8.5)
- Summative oral presentation (25%): all 5 sections, vocabulary, engagement, maths figure stated
- Peer feedback rubric collected

## Key Vocabulary

presentation · demo · audience · certificate · evaluate · reflect · iteration · prototype · RoboCreator · showcase

**Safeguarding Note:** Low-anxiety presentations. Scores are not announced publicly. Certificates follow the pass threshold privately if needed, with a resubmission path.
