# Week 7 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About 30 minutes** (you will finish this week in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Write a complete 5-section design brief: Title, Problem Statement, Who It Is For, How It Works, Maths/English Connection
- Build a working Micro:bit programme that responds to at least one input and produces at least one output
- Test the programme and identify at least one thing to improve (document under 'What I Would Change')
- Iterate: make at least one improvement based on testing
- Complete a peer review form for a partner's project using the 3-question feedback format
- Explain the mathematical concept used in the project with a calculation or example

## Big idea
Real engineers do not jump straight into building. They write a plan, build it, test it, find what is wrong, and improve it. This process is called iteration – and today you will go through all of it.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits with USB cables
- Design brief template – Title / Problem Statement / Who Is It For? / How It Works / Maths or English Connection
- Peer review form – What does the project do? What works well? One suggestion to improve?
- Optional inspiration cards: Step counter, Temperature alarm, Quiz machine, Mood display, Countdown timer, Pattern generator

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Design brief structured writing: 5 sections with clear purpose. Peer review sentence frames: 'The project does…', 'One thing that works well is…', 'One suggestion I have is…'.
- **Maths:** Include at least one explicit maths concept: sensor stats (mean/range), sequence/pattern, counting loop, or measurement. Write a calculation or show an example in the design brief.

## Let's begin

### HOOK – What Problem Will You Solve?
1. Recall earlier projects: temperature alarm, loop counter, LED story. Which solves a real problem? Could you combine ideas?
2. Choose or invent a project idea that solves a problem you have actually noticed.
3. Say in one sentence: Our project is [name]. It solves [problem] by [solution].
4. Requirements: at least one INPUT, at least one OUTPUT, and at least one MATHS connection.

### DESIGN – Write Your Design Brief
5. Write the brief **before** coding.
6. Section 1 – Title: clear descriptive name.
7. Section 2 – Problem Statement: 2–3 sentences. Who experiences it? When? Where?
8. Section 3 – Who It Is For: user, age/situation, why they need this.
9. Section 4 – How It Works: numbered steps (inputs → processing → output).
10. Section 5 – Maths/English Connection: state the concept and write a calculation or example.

### DELIVER – Build (start)
11. **Step 1:** Open MakeCode and code from the 'How It Works' section. The brief is the specification – code must match it.
