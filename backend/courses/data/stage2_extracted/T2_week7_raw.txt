Playtest Observation Sheet (A4 per pair): 4 observation questions (written by the pair before testing) + a 3-column table: Tester 1 / Tester 2 / Tester 3 with row for each question
Feedback Frequency Tally (A5): list of potential issues with tally marks — "Couldn't find tap area", "Confused by level transition", "Win condition unclear", "Too easy", "Too hard", "Sound not matching action", "Other: ___"
Priority Improvement Card (A5): "Most common issue: ___. How many testers experienced it: ___. Our fix: ___. How we will test the fix: ___."
Post-fix retest record (A5): "Before fix — Tester experienced: ___. After fix — Same tester now experiences: ___. Is the fix successful? YES / PARTIALLY / NO."
Stopwatch per pair — for timing how long it takes a new player to understand the game rules without explanation
Facilitator demonstrates BAD playtesting: stands next to a learner playing the game, explains every step, says "No, tap THERE," corrects mistakes immediately.
Ask: "Was that useful? What did I find out about whether the game works?" Nothing — because the player was guided.
Demonstrate GOOD playtesting: hands game to learner, says nothing, watches and takes notes on what the player does. Notes specific actions: "Tapped background twice before finding character. Understood Level 2 immediately after transition."
Key rule: "In a playtest, the designer is a SCIENTIST — they observe. They do not help, explain, or react. All emotions happen AFTER the tester has finished."
Pairs write 4 observation questions on their Playtest Observation Sheet. Questions must be answerable by watching — not by asking the player.
Good examples: "Does the tester find the tap target within 5 seconds?", "Does the tester attempt to replay after winning?", "Does the Level 2 transition surprise the tester?"
Poor examples: "Did the tester enjoy it?", "Was it fun?", "Did they understand?" (These require asking — not observing.)
Facilitator reviews questions: "Can this be answered by watching? Yes/No." Rewrite any that require verbal input.
TESTER 1 (8 min): One member from the adjacent pair plays the game for 5 minutes. Designer pair observes SILENTLY, completing the tally sheet and observation sheet. No speaking unless the tester is completely stuck for 60 seconds. Record all observations factually.
After Tester 1 (2 min): Tester 1 verbally describes what they found easy and hard. Designer pair records responses. Tally sheet updated.
TESTER 2 (8 min): A learner from a DIFFERENT pair playtests. Same silent observation protocol.
After Tester 2 (2 min): Same debrief process.
FREQUENCY ANALYSIS (5 min): Review both sets of observations. Complete the Feedback Frequency Tally. Identify the top issue — the one that appears most often across both testers.
PRIORITY FIX (5 min): Complete the Priority Improvement Card. Decide: "What is the single most impactful fix we can make in 10 minutes?"
Implement the priority fix (8 min). One change only — the highest-priority item from the Improvement Card.
Retest (2 min): Ask one of the original testers to replay ONLY the part that was problematic. Observe. Complete the Post-Fix Retest Record.
Class debrief: "How many pairs fixed their priority issue? How many found the fix created a new problem?" (This is normal — real software iterations often introduce regressions.)
Reflection: "Playtesting taught me that ___. Before I thought my game was ___ but after watching real players I know ___."
Save the updated game as "PLAYTESTED GAME — [Pair Names]."
File all playtest materials: observation sheets, tally, improvement card, retest record.
Preview: "Demo Day next week. Your game will be played by adults and guests who have never seen it before. You will present it just like you observed today — but now YOU are explaining it to THEM."

Big Idea
Real software is never finished on the first build — it is improved through testing with real users. Playtesting is not about showing off a finished game. It is about discovering what does NOT work yet, so you can fix it.

⚠  Facilitator Preparation Note:
TESTER ASSIGNMENT: Assign testers from different pairs to avoid pairs always testing each other's games. If the class has 8 learners in 4 pairs: Pair 1 tests Pair 3's game, Pair 3 tests Pair 1. Pair 2 tests Pair 4, Pair 4 tests Pair 2. This ensures fresh eyes on every game and prevents friends being too lenient with feedback.
CROSS-CURRICULAR LINKS
English
Observation vs opinion in writing: "The tester tapped the background instead of the character" (observation — factual, specific) vs "The tester was confused" (opinion — vague, not actionable). Learners rewrite 3 opinion statements as observation statements. This is a core academic writing skill. Vocabulary: observation, evidence, frequency, priority, actionable, specific, tester, facilitate, silent.
Maths
Frequency analysis: after 3 testers, tally the most common issues. Which issue has the highest tally? That is the priority improvement. Calculate: if 2 of 3 testers experienced the same issue, what percentage is that? (67%.) If the fix reduces it to 1/3 testers, what is the new percentage? (33%.) Improvement = 67% − 33% = 34% reduction.
