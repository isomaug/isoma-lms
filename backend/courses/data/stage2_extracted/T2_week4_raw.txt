Decompose into 4 sub-problems. For each: name the sub-problem, list the blocks, write the test condition.
Tick the skills checklist — target minimum 5 of 10 skills.
Write 3 success criteria (specific and testable — same rules as Lesson 12 Stage 1).
Facilitator approval at the 12-minute mark. Any pair without an approved brief must simplify to 2 sub-problems before coding starts — no exceptions.
Build sub-problem by sub-problem. Test each before connecting (as practised in Lesson 11).
Facilitator checkpoint at 15 minutes: "How many sub-problems tested and ticked?" Any pair behind receives a 2-minute facilitator support session — after which they are on their own.
At 25 minutes: review progress. If 4 sub-problems complete — begin polishing (add backgrounds, sounds, tighten timing). If fewer than 3 complete — continue building only the most critical sub-problems.
Bug encountered: apply 3-step debug independently. If the bug takes more than 5 minutes: document it in the evaluation section ("Known issue: ___. Cause: ___. Attempted fix: ___. Result: still present / resolved.") and move on.

CROSS-CURRICULAR LINKS
English
The 2-minute presentation requires: clear spoken English, subject vocabulary, logical structure, and the ability to explain a complex process simply. Learners rehearse once before presenting. Presentation evaluation language: "The algorithm works by ___. The hardest bug was ___ because ___ . I fixed it by ___." Vocabulary: interactive, performance, present, algorithm, justify, reflect.
Maths
Skills count: learners count how many of the 10 listed skills appear in their programme. Calculate percentage: skills used ÷ 10 × 100 = ___%. Self-evaluation score: 3 criteria × 2 marks each = 6 maximum. Convert to percentage. Compare self-score percentage to skills percentage — do they correlate?

ASSESSMENT
Formative (During)

Facilitator checkpoint at 15 min: sub-problem progress noted per pair
At least 1 independent debug documented in evaluation section
Self-evaluation score completed and compared to peer evaluation
Term 2 Skills Passport: minimum 5 of 6 stamps for Term 2 readiness
Exit / Summative
Facilitator reviews self-evaluation grids. Ask each pair one follow-up question based on their self-score. Pairs who scored themselves 6/6 but have an incomplete programme: "What criterion do you think was not fully met? What is the evidence?" Target honest self-reflection over optimistic scoring.
Differentiation
Support: Reduce to 2 sub-problems, 3 skills, 2 success criteria. Facilitator assists with brief writing. Extension: Write a short "programmer's commentary" for the project — 5–8 sentences describing: the concept, the algorithm design, one technical challenge, the solution, and what you would build next if this were a real app.
