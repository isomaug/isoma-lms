Section 1 – Title: give the project a clear, descriptive name (not just 'My Project').
Section 2 – Problem Statement: in 2–3 sentences, describe the problem. Who experiences it? When? Where?
Section 3 – Who It Is For: describe the user. Age, situation, why they need this.
Section 4 – How It Works: numbered step-by-step description of what the programme does (inputs → processing → output).
Section 5 – Maths/English Connection: state which concept is used and write a calculation or example that proves it.
Step 1 – Build (15 min): Driver opens MakeCode and begins coding. Navigator reads from the design brief's 'How It Works' section to guide. The brief is the specification – code must match it.
Step 2 – Test (5 min): Download the programme. Run it 3 times. Does it do what the design brief says? If not, note what is different.
Step 3 – Iterate (5 min): Identify the biggest problem found during testing. Fix it. Download again. Confirm the fix works. Write 'What I Would Change' in the design brief (add this section at the bottom).

ASSESSMENT | ASSESSMENT
Formative (During) | Design brief review: are all 5 sections present with at least 1 paragraph each? Is the maths connection explicitly stated with a calculation? Programme observed: does it respond to at least one input and produce at least one output?
Exit Assessment | Completed design brief (all 5 sections) submitted as Week 7 assessed artifact (4-point rubric, DDD Part 8.3). Working prototype confirmed. Peer review form completed for partner. Maths connection must include a written calculation or example.
Differentiation | Support: provide a pre-filled design brief template with prompts; learner only writes the content, not the structure. Extension: Learner writes a second round of improvements based on peer feedback and documents both test rounds in the brief, comparing before and after.

Safeguarding Note: Project ideas must be school-appropriate and must not involve monitoring, tracking, or gathering personal data about other people. If a learner proposes a project that could be used to surveil others (e.g. 'a device to check if someone is in their room'), redirect to a positive use case. All peer review must be constructive – remind learners that criticism is about the project, not the person.
