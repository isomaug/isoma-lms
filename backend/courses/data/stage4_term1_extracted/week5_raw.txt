4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits with USB cables (temperature sensor is built-in)
Data table recording sheet (A4 per learner) – pre-printed with columns: Reading #, Time (min), Temperature (°C)
Blank graph paper (A4 per learner) – with x-axis and y-axis pre-drawn for line graph
Scientific report writing frame (A4 per learner) – sections: Aim / Method / Results / Conclusion
Rulers and pencils for graph drawing
Facilitator device + projected screen

Facilitator holds the Micro:bit and asks: 'Can this device feel the temperature in this room right now?' Pause. Run a pre-loaded temperature display programme on the projected screen. Show the reading.
Ask: 'Where do you see temperature sensors in real life?' Collect answers (fridge, weather app, fever thermometer, car dashboard, air conditioner). Bridge: 'Today we will turn your Micro:bit into a science instrument.'
Introduce scientific vocabulary: sensor, data, reading, mean, range, trend. Pairs read each word on a reference card and give one example from daily life.
Show a blank data table on screen. Ask: 'Why do scientists record data in a table? Why not just remember the numbers?' Establish: tables organise data; we can spot patterns.
Distribute data table sheets and scientific report frames. Each learner writes their Aim: 'Our aim is to measure the temperature in the room every 2 minutes for 12 minutes and look for a pattern.'
Pairs write the Method (2–3 sentences): what they will do, what they will record, and how they will display the results. Facilitator checks before devices are opened.
Graph preparation: on graph paper, draw x-axis (0–12 minutes) and y-axis (choose a scale that fits expected readings of ~20–35°C). Add title and axis labels.
Maths warm-up: 'If my 6 readings are 23, 24, 24, 25, 25, 26 – what is the mean? What is the range?' Pairs calculate and share.
Step 1 – Sensor Programme (8 min): Driver opens MakeCode. Using 'Input' category → 'temperature (°C)' block. Build: 'on button A pressed → show number temperature'. Alternatively, build a 'forever' loop: 'show number temperature → pause 10000ms (10 seconds) → clear screen'. Download to Micro:bit.
Step 2 – Data Collection (10 min): Every 2 minutes, Driver reads the temperature shown on the LED and calls it out. Navigator records it in the data table (Reading #, Time, Temperature). Collect 6 readings. Swap Driver/Navigator after reading 3.
Step 3 – Graph Plotting (5 min): Each learner plots their 6 readings on their line graph. Connect the points with a smooth line. Check: title written? Both axes labelled with units? Scale consistent?
Step 4 – Mean & Range (2 min): Calculate mean (sum ÷ 6) and range (max − min). Write both values clearly at the bottom of the data table.
Pairs compare their graphs. Ask: 'Does your temperature go up, go down, or stay roughly the same? What might cause it to change?'
Facilitator asks 3 learners: 'What is the mean temperature in this room according to your data? What is the range? What trend does your graph show?'
Learners write their Conclusion on the report frame (minimum 2 sentences): 'We found that… The data showed that… One real-world use for this sensor would be…'
Teaser: 'Next week, we will use the Micro:bit to tell a STORY on the LED – a 3-scene animation with a beginning, a problem, and a resolution. You will write the story on paper BEFORE you code it.'
Learners complete the tick sheet: ☑ Data table (6 readings) ☑ Line graph (labelled, title, scale) ☑ Mean calculated ☑ Range calculated ☑ Report (Aim + Method + Conclusion).
Collect all paper artifacts (data table, graph, report frame) – these are the Week 5 assessed artifacts.
Disconnect and store Micro:bit kits safely.

ASSESSMENT | ASSESSMENT
Formative (During) | Observe data collection: is the learner reading the sensor correctly and recording with correct units? Graph check: are both axes labelled with title? Is the scale appropriate and consistent?
Exit Assessment | Collected artifacts (data table + line graph + 5-sentence report) assessed against the 4-point rubric (see DDD Part 8.3). Mean and range must be calculated and written. Report must include all 4 sections with at least 5 sentences total.
Differentiation | Support: pre-draw the graph axes with scale and labels; learner only plots the points and writes Conclusion. Extension: Learner adds a second line to the graph (e.g. readings from near a window vs. centre of room) and compares the two trends in writing.
