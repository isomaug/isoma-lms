# Week 1 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Unbox and set up a BBC Micro:bit v2 and connect it to a laptop via USB
- Identify and name at least 4 hardware features of the Micro:bit (LED matrix, buttons A & B, USB port, edge connector)
- Open Microsoft MakeCode in a browser and navigate to the coding workspace
- Programme the Micro:bit to scroll your name on the LED display using the 'on start' block
- Programme Button A to show a happy face and Button B to show a sad face
- Define the words 'input' and 'output' in their own words and give one example of each from today's session

## Big idea
Every computer needs an input and produces an output. The Micro:bit's LED matrix is a tiny screen we can programme letter by letter.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits (Micro:bit + USB cable)
- 4 shared laptops with browser access to makecode.microbit.org
- Printed 'Micro:bit Parts Map' sheet (A4 for you) – labelled diagram
- Vocabulary card set: algorithm, input, output, variable, debug (one set for you)
- Blank 5×5 LED grid worksheets (A5, 2 for you)
- A computer or screen to follow along
- 'One Star, One Wish' feedback cards (pre-printed)
- Sticky label name badges

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Vocabulary in context: input, output, algorithm, debug. You read definitions on word cards and match each word to an example from today's session. Sentence frame: 'When I press Button ___, the Micro:bit ___.'
- **Maths:** Explore the 5×5 LED grid. Count the 25 pixels. Map grid positions using x–y coordinates (0–4). Challenge: 'How many pixels light up for the letter A?' You count and record.

## Let's begin

### HOOK – What Is This Device? (10 min)
1. Show a projected image of a Micro:bit. Ask: 'What do you think this is? What might it do?' Accept all answers.
2. Look closely at one Micro:bit (without USB connected). You feel the buttons, count the LEDs, and spot the USB port using their Parts Map sheet.
3. Vocabulary reveal: Read aloud the 4 key words (input, output, algorithm, debug) and gives one example. You repeat and hold up the matching word card.
4. Bridge: 'Today we are going to make this tiny computer say YOUR name. Let's go!'
### DESIGN – Plan My LED Display (10 min)
5. Give you two 5×5 LED grid worksheets. Task: 'Draw YOUR initial letter on the first grid by colouring in the squares.'
6. You share their designs. Ask: 'How many squares did you colour? Are there any squares that were the same for both of you?'
7. Look at the MakeCode blocks panel on the projected screen. Point to: 'on start', 'forever', 'show string', 'show icon'. You locate these on the interface map.
8. Write the session goal on the board: 'By the end, our Micro:bit will scroll our name and react to button presses.'
### DELIVER – Programme the Micro:bit (30 min)
9. Get ready to use laptops + Micro:bits. You open MakeCode. Work through the steps yourself – explain: Driver types/clicks, Navigator reads the interface map and checks.

## Save your progress
Keep your MakeCode project and worksheets ready for Lesson 2, where you will finish this week's activities and check your learning.
