# Week 8 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Deliver a structured 3-minute presentation using the format: Problem -> Solution -> Demo -> What I Learned -> What I Would Improve
- Demonstrate the working Micro:bit project live during the presentation with no you assistance
- Explain at least one coding concept used (loop, condition, input event, or sensor) in plain English to the audience
- Identify and explain one mathematical element of the project (threshold, calculation, data, or pattern)
- Complete a peer feedback rubric for at least one other presenter
- Receive and respond to at least one question from the audience about the project

## Big idea
Presenting your work is as important as building it. Engineers explain what problem they solved, how they solved it, and what they would do differently next time. Today you are the expert.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables (charged and tested before session)
- 4 shared laptops (for any last-minute fixes – first 15 min only)
- All design briefs and storyboards from Weeks 6 and 7 (returned to you)
- Presentation structure card (A5 for you): Problem / Solution / Demo / What I Learned / What I Would Improve
- Peer feedback rubric (A4 for you) – scored 1–4 per criterion: Clarity, Demo worked, Coding concept explained, Maths element explained
- You assessment rubric (see DDD Part 8.5 – Stage 3–4 Demo Day Rubric)
- RoboCreator Certificates (pre-printed, ready to award on pass)
- A computer or screen to follow along (for displaying each presenter's MakeCode project if desired)
- 'Celebration' sign or display for the room

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Structured oral presentation: minimum 5 full sentences per section of the presentation structure. You must use: an introduction sentence ('My project is called…'), a problem statement ('I noticed that…'), a technical explanation ('The Micro:bit uses a ___ block to…'), a reflection ('I found it difficult/easy to… because…'), and a conclusion ('If I had more time, I would…'). Audience members must listen actively and prepare one question to ask the presenter.
- **Maths:** Quantitative explanation: each presenter must state and explain one numerical or mathematical element of their project. This could be: a threshold value used in an if/else condition and why that number was chosen; a loop count and what multiplication it represents; a mean temperature from their Week 5 data and what it shows; or a duration calculation from their Week 6 story animation. You prompts if needed: 'Is there a number in your code? What does it mean?'

## Let's begin

1. PREP – Final Fixes and Rehearsal (15 min)
2. You have 15 minutes ONLY to make final fixes. You sets a visible timer. Remind: 'After 15 minutes, all laptops close. The code you have is the code you present.'
3. You silently reads through their presentation structure card and rehearses their 3-minute talk to themselves. Check your work carefully: 'What will you say for the Demo section? What maths element will you explain?'
4. You do a 60-second mock run of their presentation with each other. Partner checks: 'Did they mention the problem? Did they show the demo? Did they explain one coding concept?'
5. Close all laptops. Micro:bits are connected and tested – confirm project is downloaded and running before presentations begin.
### DELIVER – Presentations (30 min)
6. Presentation order: you draws names randomly. You presents individually for 3 minutes. Partner supports during the demo (holds the Micro:bit or reads from the design brief) but does NOT speak.
7. Presenter 1: Problem -> Solution -> Demo (Micro:bit runs LIVE) -> What I Learned -> What I Would Improve. You times with visible timer.
8. After each presentation: audience completes the peer feedback rubric (30 seconds). One audience member asks one prepared question. Presenter answers.
