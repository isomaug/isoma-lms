# Week 5 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Connect the Micro:bit temperature sensor input in MakeCode and display a live reading on the LED matrix
- Programme the Micro:bit to display a temperature reading every 10 seconds using a 'forever' loop with a pause block
- Record 6 temperature readings over time in a correctly labelled data table (paper worksheet)
- Plot the 6 readings on a hand-drawn line graph with time on the x-axis and temperature on the y-axis, with a title and labelled axes
- Calculate the mean temperature from the 6 readings and identify the range
- Write a 5-sentence experiment report using the structure: Aim / Method / Results (with graph) / Conclusion

## Continue

### DELIVER – Code, Collect, and Graph (30 min)
1. Step 1 – Build the Programme (10 min): Driver codes the temperature display programme in MakeCode. Navigator follows block-by-block on the interface map. Check your work carefully: 'Which block sets the repeat interval? What does the pause value mean in seconds?'
2. Step 2 – Download and Run (3 min): Download the programme to the Micro:bit. Confirm the temperature reading appears on the LED. Troubleshoot any issues (wrong board selected, USB not recognised).
3. Step 3 – Collect 6 Readings (10 min): Every time the Micro:bit displays a reading, the Navigator calls it out and the Driver records it in the data table. Record 6 readings at timed intervals. Both you must record in their own worksheet.
4. Step 4 – Plot the Graph (7 min): You unplug and use the data table to plot each reading as a point on the line graph. Connect points with a ruler. Check: does the line show a pattern? Continue with the next steps yourself for this task.
### DISCOVER – Analyse and Report (8 min)
5. You calculate the mean temperature: add all 6 readings and divide by 6. Record in the data table. Then calculate the range: highest minus lowest. Record both values.
6. You completes the 5-sentence report on the writing frame. You prompts: 'What did the data SHOW? Was your hypothesis correct? What might have caused any changes?'
7. Two you share their line graphs and reports. Class gives feedback using the 'One Star, One Wish' cards. Ask yourself: 'Did anyone see a TREND in their data? Did the temperature go up, down, or stay the same?'
8. Reflection: 'What is a sensor useful for in real life?' You give 2 examples (weather stations, thermometers in fridges, smoke alarms).
### WRAP-UP – Save and Collect (2 min)
9. Rename MakeCode project 'Week5_[Name]_Temp'. Disconnect Micro:bit and return to kit box.
10. Collect data tables, graphs, and report frames. You reviews for assessment record. Label with learner name and date.

## Check your understanding
- Formative (During)
- Observation: Does the Micro:bit display a live temperature reading? Is the pause block set correctly? (functionality check)
- Data table completed with 6 readings and correct column headings? (worksheet check)
- Line graph plotted with title, labelled x- and y-axes, and connected data points? (graph check)
- Mean and range calculated correctly? (maths check on worksheet)
- Exit Assessment
- You holds up a learner's graph and asks 3 questions:
- ① What was your mean temperature?
- ② What was your range?
- ③ Read the temperature at the 3rd reading from your graph. Learner answers from their own worksheet. 3 points for you – recorded on assessment sheet.
- Differentiation
- Support: Pre-label the graph axes and provide a partially completed data table. Give a sentence-completion report frame with blanks for the learner to fill in key values. Extension: Take a second set of 6 readings in a different location (e.g. near a window vs. centre of room) and compare the two datasets on one graph using two different coloured lines. Write a comparative conclusion.
