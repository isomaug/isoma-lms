# Week 5 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Connect the Micro:bit temperature sensor input in MakeCode and display a live reading on the LED matrix
- Programme the Micro:bit to display a temperature reading every 10 seconds using a 'forever' loop with a pause block
- Record 6 temperature readings over time in a correctly labelled data table (paper worksheet)
- Plot the 6 readings on a hand-drawn line graph with time on the x-axis and temperature on the y-axis, with a title and labelled axes
- Calculate the mean temperature from the 6 readings and identify the range
- Write a 5-sentence experiment report using the structure: Aim / Method / Results (with graph) / Conclusion

## Big idea
A sensor is a device that reads information from the real world. Scientists collect data, record it, and look for patterns. Today our Micro:bit becomes our science instrument.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables
- 4 shared laptops with MakeCode open
- Data table worksheet (A4 for you) – pre-drawn table: Time | Reading | Notes
- Line graph worksheet (A4 for you) – pre-drawn axes, numbered 0–40°C on y-axis, 0–60s on x-axis
- Experiment report writing frame (A5 for you): Aim / Method / Results / Conclusion
- Rulers and pencils for graph plotting
- A computer or screen to follow along
- Vocabulary cards: 'sensor', 'data', 'mean', 'range', 'trend', 'hypothesis', 'variable'
- 'One Star, One Wish' feedback cards

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Scientific report writing: you write a short experiment report using the structured format: Aim (what we wanted to find out), Method (how we collected data), Results (the graph), Conclusion (what we found). Minimum 5 sentences. Sentence starters provided: 'Our aim was to… We collected data by… Our results showed that… We found that the temperature… This tells us that…'
- **Maths:** Data handling: complete a 6-row data table (time vs. temperature). Plot readings on a line graph with correct scale, title, and labelled axes. Calculate mean = sum of readings ÷ 6. Identify the range = highest reading − lowest reading. Read data from the graph to answer 2 questions: 'At what time was the temperature highest?' and 'What was the difference between the first and last reading?'

## Let's begin

### HOOK – What Can Our Micro:bit Feel? (10 min)
1. Hold up the Micro:bit and ask: 'Can this device feel the temperature in this room right now?' Take 3 predictions. Then display the live temperature reading on the projected MakeCode simulator.
2. Ask: 'What is a sensor?' Let you suggest. Guide to: a sensor is a hardware component that reads something from the physical world – light, temperature, movement, sound.
3. Introduce the lesson challenge: 'We are going to turn our Micro:bit into a temperature logger. We will collect 6 readings, draw a graph, and write a science report – just like real scientists.'
4. Vocabulary warm-up: hold up the 7 vocabulary cards one by one. You repeat each word and the you gives a one-sentence definition. Ask: 'Which two words describe what we do with the data after collecting it?' (mean, range).
### DESIGN – Plan the Experiment (10 min)
5. Get ready to use data table worksheets. You fill in the heading row together: Time (seconds) | Temperature (°C) | Notes. You checks all worksheets have correct column headings before moving on.
6. You write a hypothesis (prediction) at the top of their worksheet: 'We think the temperature will be around ___ °C because ___.' Share two predictions with yourself.
7. You models the MakeCode programme structure on screen: 'on start' -> 'show number temperature (°C)'. 'forever' -> 'show number temperature' + 'pause 10,000ms' + 'clear screen'. You identify each block and explain its role.
8. You label their line graph worksheet before coding begins: write the graph title, x-axis label ('Time in seconds'), y-axis label ('Temperature in °C'). Check all graphs are labelled before distributing laptops.
### DELIVER – Code, Collect, and Graph (30 min)
9. Get ready to use Micro:bits and laptops. Work through the steps yourself.
