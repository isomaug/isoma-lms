# Week 7 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Identify a real problem they have noticed (at school, at home, or in the community) that a Micro:bit could help solve
- Complete a full 5-section design brief: Title, Problem Statement, Who It Is For, How It Works, What I Used
- Plan the programme using pseudocode or a block diagram before coding
- Build a working Micro:bit prototype that responds to at least one input (button, temperature, shake, or light sensor)
- Explicitly name and demonstrate one mathematical concept used in the project (e.g. a measured threshold, a counted loop, a calculated average)
- Peer-review a partner's project using a structured 3-question feedback form

## Big idea
Real engineers do not just code – they plan, build, test, and improve. A design brief is the written plan that guides the whole project. Today we are engineers.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables
- 4 shared laptops with MakeCode open
- Design brief template (A4 for you) – 5 sections: Title / Problem Statement / Who Is It For? / How It Works / What I Used
- Pseudocode / block diagram planning sheet (A4 for you)
- Peer review feedback form (A5 for you) – 3 questions: What does this project do? What works well? What would you improve?
- Sticky notes for iteration notes (bug log)
- A computer or screen to follow along
- Vocabulary cards: 'design brief', 'prototype', 'iterate', 'user', 'problem', 'solution', 'pseudocode', 'bug log'

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Design brief writing (structured writing): you complete all 5 sections of the design brief in full sentences. Minimum 1 paragraph per section. Section prompts: Title (name your project), Problem Statement (what problem did you notice?), Who Is It For? (who will use this?), How It Works (describe the code in plain English), What I Used (list the inputs and outputs your programme uses). The written brief is a formal assessed piece.
- **Maths:** Applied mathematics: learner must explicitly state and demonstrate one mathematical concept their project uses. Examples: a temperature threshold (if temp > 30 -> show sun), a loop counter that multiplies (if the loop runs 5 times and adds 4 each time, total = 20), or a data average from sensor readings. Learner writes a calculation or shows a graph that demonstrates the mathematical element of their project on the design brief.

## Let's begin

### HOOK – What Problem Can You Solve? (8 min)
1. Show 3 quick examples of real Micro:bit projects on the projected screen (e.g. a step counter, a temperature alarm, a reaction-time game). Ask: 'What PROBLEM does each one solve? Who is it for?'
2. Ask you: 'What is something you have noticed at school or at home that a small computer could help with?' Give 60 seconds of silent thinking, then collect 4–5 ideas on the board.
3. Introduce the design brief template. Explain: 'Engineers always write their plan before they build. The design brief is your contract – it tells you what you are making and why. Today, the brief is your guide.'
### DESIGN – Write the Brief and Plan the Code (20 min)
4. You independently completes Sections 1–3 of the design brief (Title, Problem Statement, Who It Is For). Check your work carefully: 'Is the problem specific? Who exactly will use it?'
5. You collaborate to complete Section 4 (How It Works) using pseudocode or a block diagram on the planning sheet. Each pair writes the logic in plain English before touching MakeCode: 'When ___ happens, the Micro:bit will ___.'
6. You identify and write the mathematical concept in Section 5. Ask yourself: 'Where is there a number, a comparison, a calculation, or a pattern in your programme?'
7. You does a quick design review: each pair explains their project idea in one sentence. You approves before coding begins. Redirect any ideas that are too complex or not achievable in the session.
### DELIVER – Build and Test the Prototype (25 min)
8. Get ready to use Micro:bits and laptops. You begin coding from their pseudocode plan. You role: coach only – ask 'What have you tried?' before offering any hint. Do not write code for you.
