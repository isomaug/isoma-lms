- Identify the ILO with the lowest self-assessment score and write a specific, actionable improvement goal for the Final Creative Sprint in Week 23

- Formative (During)
- Portfolio Audit Grid: all 4 ILO rows completed with a self-score (1–4), artifact reference, and one justification sentence? (audit completeness check)
- Weighted score estimation attempted and Demo Day target calculated? (maths application check)

- Peer feedback written for all 4 ILO sections using evidence-based language? (peer feedback quality check)
- Exit Assessment
- Read one DDD Part 8.5 rubric descriptor (score 3 for Coding criterion) aloud and asks you: ① 'Which of your artifacts best meets this descriptor?' ② 'Does your current self-score match — if not, why?' ③ 'What is your one improvement goal for the Final Sprint?' 3 points for you — you notes which you have realistic vs. unrealistic self-scores for calibration before Week 23.
- Differentiation
- Support: Reduce audit to 2 ILOs (ILO 2.1 and ILO 2.4 — the two with the most artifact evidence). Provide pre-selected artifact examples for each ILO and ask learner to choose between two options rather than searching the full folder. Skip the weighted score calculation and instead ask: 'Do you feel ready for Demo Day — yes, almost, or not yet?' Extension: Write a full 4-paragraph Portfolio Reflection (one paragraph per ILO, minimum 4 sentences each), citing specific evidence for each score. Calculate the minimum Demo Day score needed to achieve each grade band (Pass, Merit, Distinction) using the DDD Part 9 weighted formula. Present the calculation to the you as a worked example.

## Stay safe
Safeguarding Note: Self-assessment scores are private — yous must not display or discuss individual scores publicly. The portfolio audit is explicitly framed as a tool for self-improvement, not as a ranking exercise. You must not express surprise or disappointment at any self-assigned score, even if it diverges significantly from the you's own assessment. Any significant divergence (self-score of 4 where evidence supports 1 or 2) should be addressed in a private, supportive 1-to-1 conversation, not in front of the group. All artifact folders are handled with care — they represent weeks of learner effort and must be treated respectfully.
