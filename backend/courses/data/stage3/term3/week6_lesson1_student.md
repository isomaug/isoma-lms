# Week 6 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Retrieve and organise all physical and digital artifacts from Terms One, Two, and Three into four ILO categories: ILO 2.1 (programming), ILO 2.2 (conditions/logic), ILO 2.3 (sensor data), ILO 2.4 (narrative/creative)
- Score each ILO category 1–4 using the DDD Part 8.5 rubric criteria as a self-assessment tool — one score per ILO, supported by written justification citing specific evidence
- Identify the ONE artifact that best demonstrates each ILO and write one sentence explaining why it is the strongest piece of evidence for that ILO
- Identify the ILO with the lowest self-assessment score and write a specific, actionable improvement goal for the Final Creative Sprint in Week 23
- Complete the Stage 3 Portfolio Summary document: four ILO sections, each with a score (1–4), strongest artifact reference, and one sentence of justification
- Share the Portfolio Summary with a partner and give each other one specific, evidence-based piece of feedback using the frame: 'Your strongest evidence for ILO ___ is ___ because ___.'

## Big idea
A portfolio is not just a collection of work — it is a record of your thinking. Reviewing it honestly tells you two things: what you have already proved you can do, and what you still need to show. Today you become the assessor of your own learning — using the same criteria the you uses.

## What you need
- All returned physical artifacts from Terms 1, 2, and 3 (distributed by you before session — pre-organised by learner in labelled folders)
- Digital artifact access: you open their saved MakeCode projects on laptops to review code quality
- DDD Part 8.5 Rubric reference card (A5 for you — printed extract of the Stage 3–4 rubric from the DDD, 5 criteria, 1–4 descriptors per criterion)
- Portfolio Audit Grid (A4 for you) — 4 rows (one per ILO), 4 columns: ILO | Self-Score (1–4) | Strongest Artifact | One-Sentence Justification
- Stage 3 Portfolio Summary template (A4 for you) — 4 sections, each with a heading, score box, artifact reference line, and 2-sentence justification box
- Improvement goal card (A5 for you): 'My lowest ILO is ___. My specific improvement goal for the Final Sprint is ___. I will show this by ___.'
- A computer or screen to follow along (display the DDD Part 8.5 rubric on screen throughout)
- Vocabulary cards: 'portfolio', 'artifact', 'rubric', 'criterion', 'evidence', 'justify', 'evaluate', 'ILO', 'self-assessment', 'improvement goal'

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Evaluative writing at Bloom's L5 — the most sophisticated writing task in the Stage 3 programme. Each Portfolio Summary section requires: a specific artifact reference ('In T1 Week 5, I collected temperature data and drew a line graph showing…'), a score with justification ('I scored myself 3/4 because all sections were complete but my analysis paragraph only had 3 sentences instead of 5'), and one honest limitation ('My weakest evidence for ILO 2.3 is my data table because I only recorded 5 of the 6 required readings.'). Peer feedback writing: the partner feedback sentence requires evidence-based language — not 'good job' but 'your strongest evidence for ILO 2.4 is your T2 Week 4 storyboard because it shows all 5 scenes with LED plans, buttons, and durations correctly filled in.'
- **Maths:** Weighted score estimation: using the DDD Part 8.2 weighting (Participation 15%, Weekly Artifacts 30%, Demo Day 35%, Oral Presentation 10%, Peer Feedback 5%), you estimate their current weighted score based on their portfolio audit self-scores. Example: if a learner scores themselves 3/4 on 5 artifacts (Weekly Artifacts component) = (3/4) × 30% = 22.5% out of a maximum 30%. If their participation average is 2.5/3 = (2.5/3) × 15% = 12.5%. Estimated subtotal before Demo Day = 35%. You calculate what minimum Demo Day score they need to reach 55% overall. This directly engages the DDD Part 9.3 weighted score calculation and makes the certification criteria transparent — as the DDD Part 9 explicitly requires.

## Let's begin

### HOOK – What Does Your Portfolio Say About You? (8 min)
1. Get ready to use pre-organised artifact folders to you. Allow 90 seconds of silent browsing. Ask: 'What is the FIRST piece of work you looked at? Why that one?' Take 3 responses.
2. Project the DDD Part 8.5 rubric on screen. Read each criterion aloud: Functionality, Coding, English/Written, Maths. Ask: 'Which of these criteria do you feel most confident about? Be honest with yourself — there is no wrong answer.' Show of hands for each criterion. Record on the board.
3. Introduce the purpose: 'Today you are the assessor. You will use the same rubric the you uses on Demo Day to score your own work — not to judge yourself, but to KNOW yourself. If you know exactly what you need to improve, you still have two weeks to improve it.'
4. Get ready to use the DDD Part 8.5 rubric reference cards and portfolio audit grids. You write your name and the date at the top.
### Independent audit phase — 15 minutes of quiet individual work. You sort artifacts into the four ILO categories on the desk. For each ILO: select the strongest artifact, read the rubric descriptors for that ILO, assign a self-score 1–4, and write one justification sentence in the Portfolio Audit Grid. Check your work carefully silently — observe but do not prompt scores.
5. Maths calculation — 5 minutes: you complete the weighted score estimation on the back of the audit grid. Guide: 'Use the scores from your audit as your Weekly Artifact average. Use your best estimate for participation. Calculate what Demo Day score you need.' You supports any learner who finds the percentage calculation challenging — this is formative, not summative.
6. Complete the improvement goal card: 'My lowest ILO is ___. My specific improvement goal for the Final Sprint is ___. I will show this by ___.' You checks: is the improvement goal specific and achievable in one session? If not, help the learner reframe it. Example of poor goal: 'Do better at coding.' Example of good goal: 'Show ILO 2.2 by building a nested if/else condition with AND logic in my Final Sprint project.'
### DELIVER – Write the Portfolio Summary (22 min)
7. Get ready to use Stage 3 Portfolio Summary templates. You write all four ILO sections using the Portfolio Audit Grid as their source. Each section: ILO heading -> score (1–4) -> artifact reference -> 2-sentence justification. Check your work carefully: 'Is your justification SPECIFIC? Does it name the artifact, the week, and the evidence it contains?'
