# Lesson 24: Stage 3 Graduation Demo Day – RoboCreator Programme Completion Showcase

**Duration:** 60 minutes (split across Week 8 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 3 · Week 8
**Age Range:** 8–10 years
**Hardware:** BBC Micro:bit v2 + USB cables + shared laptops
**Software:** Microsoft MakeCode (makecode.microbit.org)
**Bloom's:** L3–L6 (full Bloom's arc demonstrated in a single session)
**DDD Phase:** DISCOVER – The Stage 3 Graduation Demo Day: DDD Part 7.2 Week 8 structure, Part 8.5 rubric applied in full, Part 9 certification criteria applied, and Part 9.4 Programme Completion Record issued to learners who have completed all three terms of Stage 3
**NCDC:** Uganda NCDC P3–P4: ICT Literacy Strand; Numeracy Strand – Patterns, Logic, Data & Problem Solving
**Cambridge:** Cambridge Primary Computing 0845 – Stage 3/4: Presenting and evaluating original digital work; Communicating technical reasoning clearly; Reflecting on a sustained learning journey; Receiving and applying structured feedback

**Big Idea:** Demo Day is not an exam. It is a celebration of everything you have built, learned, and become over three terms. You are not being judged — you are being witnessed. Present with confidence, because the work in your folder, the code on your Micro:bit, and the words you say today all belong to you.

**Source plan:** `Stage3_Term3_Week8_Microbit_LessonPlan.docx`

## Learning Objectives

- Deliver a structured 3-minute oral presentation using the 6-section format: Problem → Solution → Live Demo → Mathematical Element → What I Learned → What I Would Improve — without reading a script
- Demonstrate the graduation project live in front of the group with no facilitator assistance — the programme must respond to at least one input and demonstrate at least one Term Three skill visibly
- Explain at least one Term Three concept used in the project (function, systematic debugging, accelerometer, AI classifier, or Bluetooth) in plain English to the audience in 2–3 sentences
- Present the mathematical element from the design brief — show the calculation or graph, state what it represents, and explain its relevance to the project
- Complete the DDD Part 8.5 peer feedback rubric (all 5 criteria, scored 1–4) for at least one other presenter — with written justification for at least 2 of the 5 criteria scores
- Receive and respond to at least one audience question about the project design or code with a technically accurate answer

## Materials and Preparation

- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables (fully charged and tested before session — facilitator runs a pre-session device check)
- 4 shared laptops — available for final fixes during prep phase only (first 12 minutes); closed for all presentations
- All artifact folders from Terms 1, 2, and 3 returned to learners (including Portfolio Summary from Week 22)
- 6-section Final Design Briefs, test logs, and self-evaluation frames from Week 23 (returned to learners at session start)
- Demo Day presentation structure card (A5 per learner — returned from Week 23)
- DDD Part 8.5 Peer Feedback Rubric (A4 per learner) — all 5 criteria scored 1–4 with written justification space
- Facilitator Graduation Assessment Record (one A4 sheet per learner) — all 5 DDD Part 8.5 criteria, score boxes, weighted calculation columns, final grade band
- Cumulative Assessment Record Sheets for all learners (all prior term artifact and participation scores already recorded)
- RoboCreator Stage 3 Graduation Certificates — pre-printed for all learners meeting the pass threshold
- Programme Completion Records (pre-prepared for learners who have attended all three terms of Stage 3) — listing all terms, grades achieved, and portfolio summary
- Participation Acknowledgement cards (pre-prepared for all learners regardless of certificate outcome)
- Portfolio display space — wall, table, or board for exhibiting selected artifacts

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** Graduation-level structured oral presentation — the primary summative English evidence for Stage 3 (DDD Part 8.2, Component 4: Oral Presentation, 10% of total). Term Three requirement: the presentation must include all 6 sections, the learner must read at least one sentence from the design brief (Section 2: Problem Statement), and the explanation of the Term Three skill must use at least 3 technical vocabulary words used correctly. Peer feedback rubric: DDD Part 8.5 Criterion 5 (Presentation) requires written justification for the score given — learners must write at least one evidence-based sentence per score justification. Audience question response: the learner's answer must directly address the question asked — a deflected or off-topic answer does not satisfy this objective.

**Maths:** Mathematical element presentation — DDD Part 8.5 Criterion 4: 'Math: data, graph, or logical structure demonstrated.' This is the summative mathematics evidence for Stage 3. Term Three graduation requirement: the mathematical element must be visual (shown on paper or screen during the presentation) and must include a verbal explanation connecting the number or graph to a specific decision in the project code. Facilitator scores using DDD Part 8.5 descriptor: '4 = Clear, correctly applied math element with graph or data. 3 = Math element present but partially developed. 2 = Math element mentioned but not demonstrated. 1 = No math connection made.' Additionally, learners who completed the weighted score estimation in Week 22 may compare their predicted score to the actual outcome after results are issued — a real-world application of percentage calculation and prediction.

## Lesson Structure

1. PREP – Final Polish, Device Check, and Rehearsal (12 min)
2. Learners have exactly 12 minutes for final fixes. Facilitator sets a visible timer. Announce: 'At 12 minutes, all laptops close. The programme on the Micro:bit at that moment is your Demo Day programme.' This mirrors real-world presentation discipline and is non-negotiable.
3. While learners make final adjustments, facilitator conducts a rapid device check: confirm every Micro:bit has the graduation project downloaded and running. Check: does the LED display an initial state? Does at least one input trigger the expected response? Any device not functioning is flagged immediately.
4. Solo rehearsal — 3 minutes: each learner silently reads through the presentation structure card and rehearses mentally. Facilitator circulates: 'Can you explain your Term Three skill in 2–3 sentences right now without looking at notes? Try.'
5. At 12 minutes: all laptops close without exception. Facilitator collects any laptop that is not closed. Micro:bits remain connected to laptops for demo access.
### DELIVER – Stage 3 Graduation Presentations (32 min)
6. Presentation order drawn randomly. Each learner presents individually. Partner may hold the design brief during the presentation but may not speak. No notes beyond the design brief as a reference document.
7. Each presentation: Problem → Solution → Live Demo (Micro:bit runs live, Term Three skill must be visibly demonstrated) → Mathematical Element (shown on paper or screen) → What I Learned → What I Would Improve. Facilitator times with a visible countdown at 2:30 (gentle signal) and 3:00 (stop signal).
8. After each presentation: one audience member asks a prepared question — the presenter responds. Audience members score the presenter's rubric card for Criteria 3 (English/Written) and 5 (Presentation) only. Facilitator scores all 5 criteria on the Graduation Assessment Record.
9. Facilitator gives one verbal public strength after each presentation. All developmental feedback is written on the Assessment Record and shared privately. No scores, comparisons, or rankings are discussed in the group.
10. Repeat for all 8 learners. Facilitator completes the weighted calculation on each Graduation Assessment Record as presentations conclude — adding Demo Day scores to the cumulative prior-term scores already on record.
### DISCOVER – Portfolio Display, Reflection, and Celebration (12 min)
11. Portfolio display: selected artifacts from all three terms (chosen by each learner in Week 22) arranged on the display table or wall. Learners walk the display for 2 minutes, placing a coloured dot on one piece of work — not their own — that they found most impressive or surprising.
12. Whole-group learning journey reflection — one answer per learner, rotating around the group for each question: ① 'Name one thing you can do now that you could not do in Term One, Week 1.' ② 'What was the hardest challenge in Stage 3 and how did you work through it?' ③ 'What concept from Stage 3 will you carry into Stage 4 or beyond?' ④ 'What advice would you give to a learner starting Stage 3 tomorrow?'
13. Certificate ceremony: facilitator calls each learner by name. Every learner receives a Participation Acknowledgement card. Learners who meet the DDD Part 9 pass threshold (≥55% cumulative weighted total, ≥2/4 on all rubric criteria, ≥4 of 6 artifacts submitted, ≥6 of 8 sessions attended) receive the RoboCreator Stage 3 Graduation Certificate. Learners who have completed all three terms receive the Programme Completion Record. Certificate outcome is never announced publicly — only the certificate presentation itself is public. Learners below threshold are informed privately with a written resubmission plan.
14. Near-pass learners (45–54%): offered one resubmission of the Demo Day project within 2 weeks per DDD Part 9.2. Facilitator provides a written resubmission brief before leaving the session.
### WRAP-UP – Equipment Return and Record Keeping (4 min)
15. All Micro:bits returned to kit boxes. USB cables coiled and stored. Laptops returned to charging station and closed. Phones/tablets from T3 Week 5 confirmed returned and stored.
16. Facilitator collects all Graduation Assessment Records (all 8), peer feedback rubrics (all 8), and all remaining artifact documents. Every document must be signed and dated by the facilitator before the session ends.
17. Facilitator completes and signs the Cumulative Assessment Record Sheet for every learner before leaving the session. DDD Part 10.3 requirement: 'No certificate may be signed without a completed, signed Assessment Record Sheet on file for that learner.'
18. Programme Completion Records issued to eligible learners. Facilitator logs programme completion data with the Isoma Edutech Programme Director by end of day — per DDD Part 9.4. Any learner scoring below 2/4 on two or more criteria is reported to the Programme Director immediately per DDD Part 10.3, not deferred.

## Assessment

- Formative (During)
- Pre-session device check: graduation project downloaded and functioning on all 4 Micro:bits before presentations begin? (readiness check — facilitator conducts this)
- All 6 presentation sections delivered within 3 minutes? (structure and timing check — DDD Part 8.5 Criterion 5)
- Term Three skill demonstrably visible during the live demo — not just mentioned but shown? (T3 skill evidence check)
- Mathematical element shown visually (paper or screen) and explained verbally? (DDD Part 8.5 Criterion 4 check)
- Peer feedback rubric completed with scores for all 5 criteria and written justification for at least 2? (peer feedback quality check)
- Exit Assessment
- Official Stage 3 Graduation Assessment — DDD Part 8.5 rubric, 5 criteria scored 1–4: ① Functionality: programme runs without facilitator help, handles expected inputs (10% of total) ② Coding: loops, conditions, sensor input used correctly and intentionally (10% of total) ③ English/Written Brief: design brief complete, all 6 sections, clearly written (10% of total) ④ Maths: data, graph, or logical structure demonstrated visually and explained verbally (10% of total) ⑤ Presentation: structured 6-section delivery, confident, correct vocabulary (5% of total). Weighted with all prior cumulative scores per DDD Part 8.2: Participation 15% + Weekly Artifacts 30% + Demo Day 35% + Oral Presentation 10% + Peer Feedback 5% = 100%. RoboCreator Stage 3 Graduation Certificate awarded at ≥55% cumulative weighted total. Programme Completion Record issued to learners completing all three Stage 3 terms.
- Differentiation
- Support: Learner may read directly from the design brief for all 6 sections — no penalty. Partner may hold the Micro:bit during the demo and advance through the programme states on cue. If the live demo fails, partial functionality credit is available per DDD Part 8.5 descriptor (score of 2: 'Runs partially; core feature works'). Facilitator may prompt 'show me your function' or 'show me the sensor block' to help a learner locate and demonstrate a feature they are struggling to describe verbally — this is support, not coaching, and is noted on the assessment record. Extension: Learner presents without the design brief as a reference for any section, answers 2 audience questions (not 1), and offers a forward-looking evaluation of how this project could be extended in Stage 4 using the Arduino platform (DDD Stage 5–6) — demonstrating L5 Evaluating and Stage 4 readiness in a single reflection.

## Safeguarding

Safeguarding Note: Certificate outcomes and rubric scores are never announced publicly — only the certificate presentation is public, and it is celebratory for every learner. All developmental feedback is delivered in writing, privately, after the group session. Learners below the pass threshold must be spoken to privately by the facilitator on the same day — not in front of peers, not via a parent/guardian without the learner present first. Group photographs require written parental consent on file per DDD Part 10.4 — this was collected at enrolment. Any safeguarding concern observed during the session must be reported to the Programme Director on the same day — facilitators are mandatory reporters per DDD Part 10.4. Programme Completion Records are official Isoma Edutech documents — they must not be altered, photocopied, or distributed without Programme Director approval.
