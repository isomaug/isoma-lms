# Week 8 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Deliver a structured 3-minute oral presentation using the 6-section format: Problem -> Solution -> Live Demo -> Mathematical Element -> What I Learned -> What I Would Improve — without reading a script
- Demonstrate the graduation project live in front of the group with no you assistance — the programme must respond to at least one input and demonstrate at least one Term Three skill visibly
- Explain at least one Term Three concept used in the project (function, systematic debugging, accelerometer, AI classifier, or Bluetooth) in plain English to the audience in 2–3 sentences
- Present the mathematical element from the design brief — show the calculation or graph, state what it represents, and explain its relevance to the project
- Complete the DDD Part 8.5 peer feedback rubric (all 5 criteria, scored 1–4) for at least one other presenter — with written justification for at least 2 of the 5 criteria scores
- Receive and respond to at least one audience question about the project design or code with a technically accurate answer

## Big idea
Demo Day is not an exam. It is a celebration of everything you have built, learned, and become over three terms. You are not being judged — you are being witnessed. Present with confidence, because the work in your folder, the code on your Micro:bit, and the words you say today all belong to you.

- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables (fully charged and tested before session — you runs a pre-session device check)
- 4 shared laptops — available for final fixes during prep phase only (first 12 minutes); closed for all presentations
- All artifact folders from Terms 1, 2, and 3 returned to you (including Portfolio Summary from Week 22)
- 6-section Final Design Briefs, test logs, and self-evaluation frames from Week 23 (returned to you at session start)
- Demo Day presentation structure card (A5 for you — returned from Week 23)
- DDD Part 8.5 Peer Feedback Rubric (A4 for you) — all 5 criteria scored 1–4 with written justification space
- You Graduation Assessment Record (one A4 sheet for you) — all 5 DDD Part 8.5 criteria, score boxes, weighted calculation columns, final grade band
- Cumulative Assessment Record Sheets for all you (all prior term artifact and participation scores already recorded)
- RoboCreator Stage 3 Graduation Certificates — pre-printed for all you meeting the pass threshold
- Programme Completion Records (pre-prepared for you who have attended all three terms of Stage 3) — listing all terms, grades achieved, and portfolio summary
- Participation Acknowledgement cards (pre-prepared for all you regardless of certificate outcome)
- Portfolio display space — wall, table, or board for exhibiting selected artifacts

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Graduation-level structured oral presentation — the primary summative English evidence for Stage 3 (DDD Part 8.2, Component 4: Oral Presentation, 10% of total). Term Three requirement: the presentation must include all 6 sections, the learner must read at least one sentence from the design brief (Section 2: Problem Statement), and the explanation of the Term Three skill must use at least 3 technical vocabulary words used correctly. Peer feedback rubric: DDD Part 8.5 Criterion 5 (Presentation) requires written justification for the score given — you must write at least one evidence-based sentence per score justification. Audience question response: the learner's answer must directly address the question asked — a deflected or off-topic answer does not satisfy this objective.
- **Maths:** Mathematical element presentation — DDD Part 8.5 Criterion 4: 'Math: data, graph, or logical structure demonstrated.' This is the summative mathematics evidence for Stage 3. Term Three graduation requirement: the mathematical element must be visual (shown on paper or screen during the presentation) and must include a verbal explanation connecting the number or graph to a specific decision in the project code. You scores using DDD Part 8.5 descriptor: '4 = Clear, correctly applied math element with graph or data. 3 = Math element present but partially developed. 2 = Math element mentioned but not demonstrated. 1 = No math connection made.' Additionally, you who completed the weighted score estimation in Week 22 may compare their predicted score to the actual outcome after results are issued — a real-world application of percentage calculation and prediction.

## Let's begin

1. PREP – Final Polish, Device Check, and Rehearsal (12 min)
2. You have exactly 12 minutes for final fixes. You sets a visible timer. Announce: 'At 12 minutes, all laptops close. The programme on the Micro:bit at that moment is your Demo Day programme.' This mirrors real-world presentation discipline and is non-negotiable.
3. While you make final adjustments, you conducts a rapid device check: confirm every Micro:bit has the graduation project downloaded and running. Check: does the LED display an initial state? Does at least one input trigger the expected response? Any device not functioning is flagged immediately.
4. Solo rehearsal — 3 minutes: you silently reads through the presentation structure card and rehearses mentally. Check your work carefully: 'Can you explain your Term Three skill in 2–3 sentences right now without looking at notes? Try.'
5. At 12 minutes: all laptops close without exception. You collects any laptop that is not closed. Micro:bits remain connected to laptops for demo access.
### DELIVER – Stage 3 Graduation Presentations (32 min)
6. Presentation order drawn randomly. You presents individually. Partner may hold the design brief during the presentation but may not speak. No notes beyond the design brief as a reference document.
7. Each presentation: Problem -> Solution -> Live Demo (Micro:bit runs live, Term Three skill must be visibly demonstrated) -> Mathematical Element (shown on paper or screen) -> What I Learned -> What I Would Improve. You times with a visible countdown at 2:30 (gentle signal) and 3:00 (stop signal).
8. After each presentation: one audience member asks a prepared question — the presenter responds. Audience members score the presenter's rubric card for Criteria 3 (English/Written) and 5 (Presentation) only. You scores all 5 criteria on the Graduation Assessment Record.
9. You gives one verbal public strength after each presentation. All developmental feedback is written on the Assessment Record and shared privately. No scores, comparisons, or rankings are discussed in the group.
