# Week 7 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Identify a specific, real problem or opportunity that is more ambitious than the T1 Week 7 project — explain in writing why this problem matters and who it is for
- Complete a full 5-section design brief: Title, Problem Statement, Who It Is For, How It Works (including pseudocode), What I Used (inputs, outputs, variables, conditions)
- Build a working Micro:bit prototype that uses at least 3 of the 4 ILOs and at least one skill introduced in Term Two (variable, Boolean AND/OR, dual sensor, extended narrative, or JavaScript annotation)
- Include and demonstrate one explicit mathematical element with a written calculation or graph in Section 5 of the design brief
- Test the prototype systematically — record 3 test cases in a test log (input -> expected output -> actual output -> pass/fail)
- Write a structured self-evaluation: 2 strengths, 1 limitation, and 1 specific improvement for the Demo Day version

## Big idea
This is your biggest project yet. You now know variables, conditions, sensors, narratives, loops, radio, Boolean logic, and JavaScript. A great project does not use everything at once — it uses the right things, chosen deliberately, to solve a real problem well. Plan carefully. Build bravely. Evaluate honestly.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables
- 4 shared laptops with MakeCode open
- Term Two Design Brief template (A4 for you) – 5 sections: Title / Problem Statement / Who It Is For / How It Works (with pseudocode box) / What I Used (inputs, outputs, variable names, conditions, mathematical element)
- Test log template (A5 for you) – 3 rows: Input | Expected Output | Actual Output | Pass/Fail
- Self-evaluation frame (A5 for you): 2 strengths / 1 limitation / 1 Demo Day improvement
- Squared paper (for calculations or graphs in design brief Section 5)
- Sticky notes for build-phase bug log
- A computer or screen to follow along
- Vocabulary cards: 'design brief', 'pseudocode', 'test case', 'test log', 'pass/fail', 'prototype', 'iterate', 'self-evaluate', 'limitation', 'improvement'

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Design brief writing (formally assessed piece): you complete all 5 sections in full sentences with a minimum of 1 paragraph per section. The 'How It Works' section must include pseudocode written in plain English — each line of pseudocode becomes one line of MakeCode. Section 4 sentence frame: 'When ___ happens, the Micro:bit will ___. If ___ is greater than ___, then ___. Otherwise, ___.' Self-evaluation writing: 2 strengths must be specific and evidence-based ('My project responds correctly to ___ because ___'), not vague ('It was good'). The limitation must be honest and the improvement actionable.
- **Maths:** Mandatory mathematical element: you choose and document ONE of the following in the design brief — (a) a threshold value derived from a calculation (e.g. if the mean light level from T2 Week 3 was 180, set the alarm threshold at 150 and explain why), (b) a loop counter that demonstrates multiplication (loop runs N times adding X -> total = N×X), (c) a timing sequence where scene durations are planned, summed, and a fraction of total is calculated, or (d) a comparative data element where two sensor readings are compared and the difference is shown. All calculations must be written out in full on squared paper and attached to the design brief.

## Let's begin

### HOOK – Your Best Project Yet (8 min)
1. Open with the T1 Week 7 design briefs returned to you. Ask: 'Look at what you wrote in Term One. What do you know NOW that you did not know then?' Collect 4–5 answers. Write them on the board under 'New Skills'.
2. Challenge: 'Term Two Demo Day is next week. This is your last chance to build before you present. Your project today should be MORE ambitious than Term One — because YOU are more capable.' Show the Term Two skills list on screen (variables, Boolean logic, dual sensor, 5-scene narrative, JavaScript, radio, integration).
3. Introduce the test log: 'Real developers test their code systematically. Today you will run 3 planned test cases — planned BEFORE you run them. If the actual output does not match the expected output, that is a bug. You write it down. You fix it. That is engineering.'
### DESIGN – Complete the Full Design Brief (18 min)
4. Get ready to use design brief templates and test log sheets. You completes Sections 1–3 (Title, Problem Statement, Who It Is For) INDEPENDENTLY — 6 minutes. Check your work carefully: 'Is the problem specific? Can you name one person who would use this?'
5. You collaborate on Section 4 (How It Works): write pseudocode together. Each line of pseudocode must directly correspond to a MakeCode block you can name. You checks: 'Does your pseudocode include a variable? A condition? A sensor?'
6. You complete Section 5 (What I Used) — list all inputs, outputs, variable names, and conditions. Write the mathematical element with the calculation or graph on squared paper. Attach to design brief.
7. You approval gate: sign off design brief before laptops open. Redirect any project missing the mathematical element or using fewer than 3 ILOs. Every learner must also complete the 3-row test log header: write 3 planned test cases BEFORE building.
### DELIVER – Build, Test, and Iterate (27 min)
8. Get ready to use Micro:bits and laptops. Assign Driver and Navigator. Driver codes from the pseudocode in the design brief — not from memory, not from improvisation.
