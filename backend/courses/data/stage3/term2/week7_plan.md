# Lesson 15: Term Two Creative Sprint – Design, Build & Refine Your Best Project

**Duration:** 60 minutes (split across Week 7 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 2 · Week 7
**Age Range:** 8–10 years
**Hardware:** BBC Micro:bit v2 + USB cables + shared laptops
**Software:** Microsoft MakeCode (makecode.microbit.org)
**Bloom's:** L5–L6 Evaluating / Creating
**DDD Phase:** DISCOVER – Learners design and build their most ambitious original Micro:bit project, supported by a full 5-section design brief, with Demo Day in mind
**NCDC:** Uganda NCDC P3–P4: ICT Literacy Strand; Numeracy Strand – Patterns, Logic & Data
**Cambridge:** Cambridge Primary Computing 0845 – Stage 3: Designing original solutions; Evaluating solutions against criteria; Documenting and communicating design decisions; Preparing work for formal presentation

**Big Idea:** This is your biggest project yet. You now know variables, conditions, sensors, narratives, loops, radio, Boolean logic, and JavaScript. A great project does not use everything at once — it uses the right things, chosen deliberately, to solve a real problem well. Plan carefully. Build bravely. Evaluate honestly.

**Source plan:** `Stage3_Term2_Week7_Microbit_LessonPlan.docx`

## Learning Objectives

- Identify a specific, real problem or opportunity that is more ambitious than the T1 Week 7 project — explain in writing why this problem matters and who it is for
- Complete a full 5-section design brief: Title, Problem Statement, Who It Is For, How It Works (including pseudocode), What I Used (inputs, outputs, variables, conditions)
- Build a working Micro:bit prototype that uses at least 3 of the 4 ILOs and at least one skill introduced in Term Two (variable, Boolean AND/OR, dual sensor, extended narrative, or JavaScript annotation)
- Include and demonstrate one explicit mathematical element with a written calculation or graph in Section 5 of the design brief
- Test the prototype systematically — record 3 test cases in a test log (input → expected output → actual output → pass/fail)
- Write a structured self-evaluation: 2 strengths, 1 limitation, and 1 specific improvement for the Demo Day version

## Materials and Preparation

- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables
- 4 shared laptops with MakeCode open
- Term Two Design Brief template (A4 per learner) – 5 sections: Title / Problem Statement / Who It Is For / How It Works (with pseudocode box) / What I Used (inputs, outputs, variable names, conditions, mathematical element)
- Test log template (A5 per learner) – 3 rows: Input | Expected Output | Actual Output | Pass/Fail
- Self-evaluation frame (A5 per learner): 2 strengths / 1 limitation / 1 Demo Day improvement
- Squared paper (for calculations or graphs in design brief Section 5)
- Sticky notes for build-phase bug log
- Facilitator device + projected screen
- Vocabulary cards: 'design brief', 'pseudocode', 'test case', 'test log', 'pass/fail', 'prototype', 'iterate', 'self-evaluate', 'limitation', 'improvement'

## Cross-Curricular Links

**English:** Design brief writing (formally assessed piece): learners complete all 5 sections in full sentences with a minimum of 1 paragraph per section. The 'How It Works' section must include pseudocode written in plain English — each line of pseudocode becomes one line of MakeCode. Section 4 sentence frame: 'When ___ happens, the Micro:bit will ___. If ___ is greater than ___, then ___. Otherwise, ___.' Self-evaluation writing: 2 strengths must be specific and evidence-based ('My project responds correctly to ___ because ___'), not vague ('It was good'). The limitation must be honest and the improvement actionable.

**Maths:** Mandatory mathematical element: learners choose and document ONE of the following in the design brief — (a) a threshold value derived from a calculation (e.g. if the mean light level from T2 Week 3 was 180, set the alarm threshold at 150 and explain why), (b) a loop counter that demonstrates multiplication (loop runs N times adding X → total = N×X), (c) a timing sequence where scene durations are planned, summed, and a fraction of total is calculated, or (d) a comparative data element where two sensor readings are compared and the difference is shown. All calculations must be written out in full on squared paper and attached to the design brief.

## Lesson Structure

### HOOK – Your Best Project Yet (8 min)
1. Open with the T1 Week 7 design briefs returned to learners. Ask: 'Look at what you wrote in Term One. What do you know NOW that you did not know then?' Collect 4–5 answers. Write them on the board under 'New Skills'.
2. Challenge: 'Term Two Demo Day is next week. This is your last chance to build before you present. Your project today should be MORE ambitious than Term One — because YOU are more capable.' Show the Term Two skills list on screen (variables, Boolean logic, dual sensor, 5-scene narrative, JavaScript, radio, integration).
3. Introduce the test log: 'Real developers test their code systematically. Today you will run 3 planned test cases — planned BEFORE you run them. If the actual output does not match the expected output, that is a bug. You write it down. You fix it. That is engineering.'
### DESIGN – Complete the Full Design Brief (18 min)
4. Distribute design brief templates and test log sheets. Each learner completes Sections 1–3 (Title, Problem Statement, Who It Is For) INDEPENDENTLY — 6 minutes. Facilitator circulates: 'Is the problem specific? Can you name one person who would use this?'
5. Pairs collaborate on Section 4 (How It Works): write pseudocode together. Each line of pseudocode must directly correspond to a MakeCode block learners can name. Facilitator checks: 'Does your pseudocode include a variable? A condition? A sensor?'
6. Learners complete Section 5 (What I Used) — list all inputs, outputs, variable names, and conditions. Write the mathematical element with the calculation or graph on squared paper. Attach to design brief.
7. Facilitator approval gate: sign off design brief before laptops open. Redirect any project missing the mathematical element or using fewer than 3 ILOs. Every learner must also complete the 3-row test log header: write 3 planned test cases BEFORE building.
### DELIVER – Build, Test, and Iterate (27 min)
8. Distribute Micro:bits and laptops. Assign Driver and Navigator. Driver codes from the pseudocode in the design brief — not from memory, not from improvisation.
9. Build Phase 1 (12 min): establish the core programme — variable, sensor input, condition, and first output. Download and run Test Case 1 from the test log. Record Actual Output. Pass or Fail? If Fail — add to bug log sticky note and fix before Phase 2.
10. Build Phase 2 (10 min): add the Term Two skill element (the feature that goes beyond T1 Week 7). Run Test Case 2. Record. SWAP Driver and Navigator. New Driver adds at least one refinement identified by the Navigator.
11. Test Case 3 and refinement (5 min): run Test Case 3. Record Actual Output. Address any remaining failures. Review design brief Section 4 — is the pseudocode still accurate? Update it if the code changed.
### DISCOVER – Self-Evaluate and Prepare for Demo Day (5 min)
12. Each learner independently completes the self-evaluation frame: 2 specific strengths, 1 honest limitation, 1 actionable improvement for Demo Day. Facilitator prompt: 'Your strength must be provable from the test log. Your limitation must be real.'
13. Pairs share self-evaluations verbally — 30 seconds each. Partner checks: 'Is the improvement genuinely achievable by next week?' If not, help them reframe it as something achievable.
14. Facilitator previews Demo Day structure for Week 16: 'Problem → Solution → Live Demo → Mathematical Element → What I Learned → What I Would Improve. You will have 3 minutes. Practise this structure at home using your design brief as your script.'
### WRAP-UP – Save and Collect (2 min)
15. Rename MakeCode project 'T2W7_[Name]_DemoProject'. Disconnect Micro:bit. Learners may photograph the test log with facilitator permission to guide home refinement.
16. Collect design briefs, test logs, and self-evaluation frames. These are formally assessed artifacts and are returned at Demo Day as reference documents. Facilitator checks all 5 design brief sections are complete and the test log has 3 recorded test cases.

## Assessment

- Formative (During)
- Facilitator approval gate: design brief signed off with all 5 sections and 3 test cases planned before coding? (design gate check)
- Prototype uses at least 3 ILOs and at least one Term Two skill demonstrably? (ILO coverage check)
- All 3 test cases run and recorded with input, expected output, actual output, and pass/fail? (test log check)
- Self-evaluation includes 2 specific strengths, 1 honest limitation, and 1 actionable Demo Day improvement? (self-evaluation check)
- Exit Assessment
- Facilitator asks each learner: ① 'What Term Two skill does your project use that your T1 Week 7 project did not?' ② 'What was the result of Test Case 2?' ③ 'What is your one improvement for Demo Day?' Learner answers from their design brief and test log. 3 points per learner — recorded on assessment sheet. Flag any learner scoring below 2/4 on this artifact immediately per DDD Part 10.3.
- Differentiation
- Support: Reduce to 2 ILOs in the project; allow learner to rebuild and improve the T1 Week 7 project with one addition (e.g. add a variable or change the condition to use AND). Reduce the test log to 2 test cases. Provide a sentence-completion design brief where each section has a 50% pre-written frame. Extension: Complete all 5 sections of the design brief in full paragraphs (minimum 3 sentences each). Add a fourth test case that tests an edge case — what happens when the sensor value is exactly at the threshold? What happens when the variable reaches its maximum? Document and fix any edge-case failures.

## Safeguarding

Safeguarding Note: Design brief project ideas are reviewed at the facilitator approval gate. Any project that could be used to monitor, track, or gather data about individuals without consent (including other learners) must be redirected. Test logs and self-evaluation frames are private assessment documents — facilitator does not share individual results with the group. Remind learners that projects may be photographed for the Isoma Edutech portfolio only with prior written parental consent on file.
