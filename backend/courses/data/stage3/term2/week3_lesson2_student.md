# Week 3 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Programme the Micro:bit to display the live light level reading (0–255) on the LED matrix using the 'light level' sensor block in MakeCode
- Collect Dataset A: 6 light level readings with the Micro:bit in normal room light, recorded in a labelled data table
- Collect Dataset B: 6 light level readings with the Micro:bit LED covered (low light simulation), recorded in a second labelled data table
- Plot both datasets on ONE line graph using two different colours, with a labelled title, axes, and a key/legend identifying each line
- Calculate the mean for each dataset and identify the range for each dataset
- Write a 5-sentence comparative analysis: what did each dataset show, how do they differ, and what might explain the difference

## Continue

### DELIVER – Code, Collect, and Plot (30 min)
1. Step 1 – Build the programme (5 min): code 'forever': show number light level; pause 2000ms; clear screen. Download and run. Confirm the Micro:bit is displaying live light readings.
2. Step 2 – Collect Dataset A (8 min): Micro:bit in normal position on the desk. Each time a reading flashes, Navigator calls it out, Driver records it in Dataset A table. 6 readings. Both you record in their own worksheets.
3. Step 3 – Collect Dataset B (8 min): Driver places palm gently over the LED matrix to reduce light. Collect 6 readings in the same way. Record in Dataset B table. Do NOT touch the programme — same code, different condition.
4. Step 4 – Plot the dual graph (9 min): Continue with the next steps yourself. Using the two coloured pencils, plot Dataset A points with Colour 1 and connect with a line. Plot Dataset B points with Colour 2 and connect with a line. Label the legend. Check: does the graph have a title, labelled axes, and a key?
### DISCOVER – Calculate and Write (8 min)
5. You calculate mean and range for both datasets. Record on the worksheet. Ask yourself: 'Which dataset has the greater range? What does a large range tell us about consistency?'
6. You write their 5-sentence comparative analysis independently using the writing frame. Check your work carefully: 'Have you used a comparative word? Have you given a reason for the difference?'
7. Two you share their graphs and read one sentence from their analysis. Class asks: 'Did your hypothesis match your results?' You respond with evidence from their data.
### WRAP-UP – Save and Collect (2 min)
8. Rename MakeCode project 'T2W3_[Name]_Light'. Disconnect Micro:bit.
9. Collect dual data tables, dual line graphs, and comparative analysis writing frames. All three are formally assessed artifacts this week. You checks each graph has a title, two labelled lines, and a legend.

## Check your understanding
- Formative (During)
- Observation: Does the Micro:bit display live light level readings correctly? (functionality check)
- Both data tables completed with 6 readings each and correct headings? (worksheet check)
- Dual line graph plotted with both lines in different colours, title, labelled axes, and legend? (graph check)
- Mean and range calculated correctly for both datasets? (maths check)
- Exit Assessment
- Ask yourself each pair: ① 'Which dataset had the higher mean — A or B? By how much?' ② 'What was the range for Dataset B?' ③ 'Read me your strongest comparative sentence from the analysis.' 3 points for you. You records on assessment sheet alongside the artifact grade for the graph and writing frame.
- Differentiation
- Support: Pre-collect Dataset B readings as a class demonstration so learner only records and plots. Provide a sentence-completion analysis frame with blanks rather than open starters. Allow learner to use a ruler to draw graph lines and a calculator for mean. Extension: Collect a third dataset (Dataset C — very bright light from torch or near a window) and add a third line to the graph using a third colour. Write a 3-dataset comparison paragraph. Discuss: what type of graph (bar vs. line) is more appropriate for this data and why?
