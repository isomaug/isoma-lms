# Week 8 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Deliver a structured 3-minute oral presentation following the format: Problem -> Solution -> Live Demo -> Mathematical Element -> What I Learned -> What I Would Improve
- Demonstrate the Term Two project live with no you assistance — the programme must run and respond to at least one input during the presentation
- Explain at least one Term Two skill used in the project (variable, Boolean AND/OR, dual sensor, 5-scene narrative, or JavaScript annotation) in plain English
- Present the mathematical element from the design brief — state the calculation or graph and explain what it demonstrates about the project
- Complete a structured peer feedback rubric (DDD Part 8.5 adapted) for at least one other presenter — scored on all 5 criteria
- Receive and respond to at least one clarifying question from the audience about the project or design decisions

## Big idea
This is not just a presentation — it is a portfolio review. Every piece of work from Term Two tells a story of growth. Today you present your best project, explain your thinking, and reflect on how far you have come since Week 1 of Term One.

## What you need
- 4 BBC Micro:bit v2 kits + USB cables (all charged and tested before session begins)
- 4 shared laptops (available for final fixes — first 12 minutes only, then closed)
- Term Two design briefs and test logs returned to you (collected at T2 Week 7)
- Presentation structure card (A5 for you): Problem / Solution / Live Demo / Mathematical Element / What I Learned / What I Would Improve
- Peer feedback rubric (A4 for you) – DDD Part 8.5 Stage 3–4 rubric: 5 criteria (Functionality, Coding, English/Written Brief, Maths, Presentation), scored 1–4 with descriptor per level
- You assessment rubric (DDD Part 8.5 official — one for you, pre-printed)
- You cumulative assessment record sheet (one for you — all Term One and Term Two artifact and participation scores to date)
- RoboCreator Term Two Certificates of Achievement (pre-printed for all passing you; participation acknowledgement for all others)
- Display board or wall space for portfolio display of selected artifacts

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Structured oral presentation — the DDD's primary English summative evidence for Stage 3–4 (Part 8.2: '3-minute structured presentation: intro -> demo -> conclusion; scored on structure, vocabulary accuracy, ability to explain a coding concept in plain language, and confidence'). Term Two requirement: the presentation must include a reference to the written design brief — learner reads one sentence from Section 2 (Problem Statement) and one from Section 4 (How It Works). Audience members must prepare and ask at least one genuine question — practising active listening and analytical questioning as explicit English skills (DDD Part 2.4 cross-curricular framework).
- **Maths:** Mathematical element presentation — required per DDD Part 8.5 criterion 4: 'Data, graph, or logical structure demonstrated in project.' Term Two requirement: the mathematical element must be from the design brief Section 5 and must be shown visually (calculation written on paper held up, or graph on screen). Learner states: what the number or graph represents, how it was calculated, and what it means for the project. Audience may ask a mathematical question — learner is expected to answer. Note whether the mathematical element is correct, clearly explained, and genuinely connected to the project (all three required for a score of 3 or 4 on the rubric).

## Let's begin

1. PREP – Final Polish and Rehearsal (12 min)
2. You have exactly 12 minutes to make final fixes — you sets a visible timer. Announce clearly: 'At 12 minutes, all laptops close. Whatever you have is what you present.' This is non-negotiable and mirrors real-world presentation discipline.
3. You review the presentation structure card and rehearse silently. Check your work carefully: 'Have you prepared what to say for the Mathematical Element section? Do you know which Term Two skill you will explain?'
4. You do a 90-second mock presentation to each other — presenter runs through all 6 sections, partner times and checks the structure card. Partner gives one specific improvement: 'You skipped the Mathematical Element — you need to add that.'
5. All laptops closed at the 12-minute mark. Micro:bits checked: programme downloaded, LED displaying initial state. You confirms all 8 you are ready before beginning.
### DELIVER – Term Two Demo Day Presentations (32 min)
6. Presentation order drawn randomly. You presents individually — 3 minutes maximum. Partner may hold the design brief or operate a second Micro:bit if the project uses radio, but may NOT speak.
7. Each presentation follows the 6-section structure card. You times with a visible countdown. You completes the DDD Part 8.5 rubric for each presenter during the presentation — do not interrupt to ask questions until after the presentation ends.
8. After each presentation: (a) one audience member asks a prepared question — presenter answers. (b) Audience members complete their peer feedback rubric for that presenter (45 seconds). (c) You gives one verbal strength in public and holds any developmental feedback for private written comments.
9. Repeat for all 8 presenters. You records rubric scores on the cumulative assessment record sheet — these scores combine with all prior artifact and participation scores per the DDD Part 8.2 weighting (Demo Day Project 35%, Oral Presentation 10%, Peer Feedback 5%).
