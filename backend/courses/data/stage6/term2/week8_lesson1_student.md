# Week 8 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation following the exact structure: Problem (framing the need) -> Solution (describing the design) -> Live Demo (prototype runs during presentation) -> Evidence (mathematical element displayed and explained) -> Reflection (what was hard, what was learned, what comes next)
- Demonstrate a live Arduino prototype that functions correctly during the presentation - responds to input, produces correct output, incorporates THREE Term 2 concepts
- Present at least one quantified mathematical element as visual evidence - shown on Serial Monitor, a hand-drawn graph, or a printed data table - and explain its significance to the audience
- Explain one coding concept used in the project in plain English to the panel, without using the code on screen as a crutch
- Answer a minimum of two panel questions - one technical, one about design decisions - with specific, reasoned responses
- Receive and acknowledge peer feedback from the audience rubric without deflection

## Big idea
ILO 3.4 (summative completion) - Design, build, test, iterate, and present an original Arduino project integrating coding, English literacy, and Mathematics, supported by a written design brief and a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel. All four ILOs (3.1–3.4) are assessed holistically in the Demo Day rubric.

## Let's begin

1. You arrive with projects built and tested. First 20 min: connect hardware, upload sketch, run one complete test. Any last-minute bug is a bug fix only - no new features. If a sketch does not compile, learner presents with their best working version and explains the issue in the Reflection section.
2. You: configure the room - presenter position at front, panel (you + invited assessor) seated to one side facing presenter, audience in a semicircle. Place timer where presenter can see it.
3. Final 10 min: you reviews their Design Brief and mentally rehearses the 5 presentation elements. Say the opening sentence aloud to a partner: "The problem I am solving is…" If it is unclear, revise now.
4. Panel briefing: invited assessor receives DDD Part 8.6 rubric, a blank Weighted Score Sheet, and a 3-minute briefing on the scoring criteria. Confirm: scores are given independently; moderation happens after all presentations; scores are never announced publicly.
5. You confirms presentation order. Any learner who is visibly anxious may present in a position of their choice - you accommodates without drawing attention.
### 2. PRESENTATIONS Formal Demo Day
6. You presents individually for 5 minutes. Pair partner supports hardware during the live demo phase - operates buttons or adjusts physical conditions on request - but does not speak.
7. Presentation structure (strictly enforced by timer): Problem (60 sec) -> Solution (60 sec) -> Live Demo (90 sec - prototype must run) -> Evidence (60 sec - show the number, explain it) -> Reflection (30 sec - what was hard, what next).
8. Panel questions (2 minutes after each presentation): you ask one technical question - drawn from: "Why did you choose this component?", "Explain what this function returns.", "What does this number mean?". Invited assessor asks one design question: "What would you change if you had more time?", "How does this connect to AI?", "What happens if the sensor reading is 0?"
9. Audience: during each presentation, audience completes the Audience Peer Feedback Form for the presenter (3 criteria + one question they would ask the presenter). Forms collected after each presentation and given to the presenter immediately.
10. Between presentations: 2-minute transition. You and assessor mark scores independently on their separate rubric sheets. Do not discuss scores during transitions.
11. You log: note participation quality of audience members - are they engaged? Are their peer feedback forms specific? This contributes to Component 1 (Weekly Participation 10%).
12. 3. MODERATION & SCORING
13. You and assessor compare rubric scores privately. For any criterion where scores differ by more than 1 point (on a 4-point scale), discuss briefly and agree a moderated score.
14. Complete the Weighted Score Sheet for every learner: (Participation score / 3) × 10 + (Artifact average / 5) × 25 + (Demo Day prototype / 12) × 30 + (Presentation / 16) × 20 + (Design Brief / 10) × 10 + (Peer Review / 5) × 5. Total must equal 100%.
15. Apply DDD Part 9.2 minimum requirements check: ≥55% total AND ≥2/4 on all 5 rubric criteria AND ≥5 of 7 artifacts submitted AND ≥6 of 8 sessions attended. All four conditions must be met.
16. You scoring 45–54% (Near Pass): offered one resubmission of the Demo Day presentation within 2 weeks. You informs privately after certificates are awarded to passing you.
17. You scoring 85%+ (Distinction): noted for Isoma Edutech Honour Roll and eligible to mentor peers in the next cohort.
18. 4. CELEBRATION & CLOSING
19. Certificate Presentation: you called individually, thanked for their effort, presented with their AI Engineer Certificate (Term 2). Photo opportunity with written parental consent only.
20. You reads 3–4 highlights from across the cohort - specific achievements, no comparisons: "This term's cohort demonstrated modular code design, dual-sensor data analysis, and rule-based AI classification. I was particularly struck by the quality of the written critiques in Week 5 and the ambition of the projects in Week 7."
21. Learner reflection (individual, written, 5 minutes): complete three sentences - "The most significant thing I built this term was… The concept I understand most deeply now is… If I continued to Stage 7, I would want to explore…"
22. Gallery moment: all projects displayed simultaneously. You leaves one sticky note on a project that impressed them - note must be specific: "I was impressed by how you used a rolling mean to filter sensor noise because…" Vague notes ("nice project") are returned and rewritten.
23. Programme pathway: Term 2 AI Engineer Certificate holders are eligible for the advanced Stage 7 Arduino + Python pathway. You distributes the Stage 7 prospectus.
24. You completes ALL Assessment Record Sheets. No certificate may be signed without a completed, signed record. All artifacts, rubrics, and score sheets filed in learner portfolios and retained for moderation.
25. Criterion & Weight
26. Excellent (4)
