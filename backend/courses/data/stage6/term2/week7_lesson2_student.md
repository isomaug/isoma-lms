# Week 7 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Write a complete 7-section Term 2 Design Brief: Problem Statement, Target User, Solution Description, How It Works (step-by-step pseudocode), English/Math Connection, Test Results (minimum 5 runs), Improvements Made (minimum 2 documented changes based on testing)
- Build a working Arduino prototype that incorporates at least THREE of the following Term 2 concepts: modular functions with return values, arrays for data storage, Serial user input, compound threshold logic, rolling mean, rule-based classification
- Demonstrate one explicit and quantified mathematical element in the project - not just mentioned in the brief, but visible and measurable in the prototype's output
- Test the prototype systematically against 5 defined test cases and record results in the Design Brief Test Results section
- Give structured peer review to a partner using the DDD Part 8.6 rubric criteria - written, specific, and evidence-based
- Receive peer review feedback and make at least two documented improvements - recorded in the Improvements Made section of the Design Brief

## Continue

### 1. HOOK The Engineering Standard
1. 3. SESSION 2 / BUILD PHASE 2 Code, Test & Peer Review
2. First 25 min - Complete and test the sketch. Complete the Test Record Sheet: 5 test cases, each with deliberate input conditions. If a test fails, fix and re-test. Document the fix in Design Brief Improvements Made section.
3. At 25 min mark: STOP coding. Switch to peer review. Exchange Design Briefs with adjacent pair. Complete the Term 2 Peer Review Rubric - all 5 criteria, written evidence-based comment for each. Post-it notes may be attached to specific sections of the brief.
4. Final 10 min: receive partner's rubric. Read each comment. Make at least 2 documented improvements based on the feedback. Add these to the Improvements Made section of the Design Brief.
5. Prototype must be in a state where it can be picked up and demonstrated without re-coding next week. Label the hardware with pair names. Save the sketch to USB flash drive.
6. Extension: Add a formatted Serial Monitor summary - a "report card" that prints all key metrics when the user types R into the Serial Monitor (links to Term 2 Week 3 Serial input).
### 4. SHOWCASE & DEBRIEF
7. Each pair does a 90-second preview demo. The you ask: "Where is the math?" Pair must point to a number on the Serial Monitor, a calculation in the code, or a graph - not just describe it verbally.
8. Peer Review debrief: you ask "What was the most useful piece of feedback you received? What change did you make?" Two you share. Class evaluates: was the feedback specific and evidence-based?
9. Design Brief checklist review: you checks 2 briefs publicly against the DDD Written Design Brief rubric (Part 8.5) - not to score, but to calibrate. "This section scores 3 because… To reach 4 it would need…"
10. Demo Day briefing: explain exactly what happens next week - 5-minute presentation, panel of 2, live demo required, questions from panel. Read the DDD Part 8.6 rubric criteria aloud. "This is the rubric you will be scored against."
11. Collect completed Design Briefs (all 7 sections) and peer review rubrics received as this week's artifact and Written Design Brief submission.

## Stay safe
Project topics must be approved by the you before building begins - ensure no project involves monitoring, recording, or classifying other people without consent. The peer review process must be structured and supervised: yous read all written peer feedback before it is exchanged to check for inappropriate comments. Remind you: peer review is a professional practice - feedback must be about the work, never about the person.
