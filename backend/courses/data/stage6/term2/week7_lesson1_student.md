# Week 7 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (you will finish in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- Write a complete 7-section Term 2 Design Brief: Problem Statement, Target User, Solution Description, How It Works (step-by-step pseudocode), English/Math Connection, Test Results (minimum 5 runs), Improvements Made (minimum 2 documented changes based on testing)
- Build a working Arduino prototype that incorporates at least THREE of the following Term 2 concepts: modular functions with return values, arrays for data storage, Serial user input, compound threshold logic, rolling mean, rule-based classification
- Demonstrate one explicit and quantified mathematical element in the project - not just mentioned in the brief, but visible and measurable in the prototype's output
- Test the prototype systematically against 5 defined test cases and record results in the Design Brief Test Results section
- Give structured peer review to a partner using the DDD Part 8.6 rubric criteria - written, specific, and evidence-based
- Receive peer review feedback and make at least two documented improvements - recorded in the Improvements Made section of the Design Brief

## Big idea
ILO 3.4 (summative) - Design, build, test, iterate, and present an original Arduino project that integrates at least one coding concept, one explicit English literacy connection, and one explicit Mathematics connection, supported by a written design brief (DDD Part 8.5).

## Let's begin

### 1. HOOK The Engineering Standard
1. You reads aloud the ILO 3.4 assessment criteria from the DDD (printed on the board): "Functionality (10%): project runs, responds to input, produces correct output. Code quality (10%): variables, loops, functions, comments. Integration (10%): explicit, demonstrable English AND Math connection."
2. Ask: "What is different about Term 2 Week 7 vs Term 1 Week 7?" Collect answers. Key differences: THREE Term 2 concepts required; mathematical element must be quantified and visible; peer review uses the actual DDD rubric criteria.
3. Get ready to use the Term 2 Design Brief Template. You read the mandatory field checklist: "At the top of your brief, tick which THREE Term 2 concepts your project includes. These ticks are your commitment - they become assessment criteria."
4. You have 10 minutes to draft their Problem Statement and complete the mandatory concept checklist. You approves the Problem Statement before any hardware is touched - if it is too vague, return for revision. Criteria: specific, verifiable, serves an identified user.
5. Get ready to use and read the Term 2 Peer Review Rubric. You know from the start what their partner will be evaluating - this shapes design decisions.
6. 2. SESSION 1 / BUILD PHASE 1 Design Brief + Circuit
7. First 30 min - Design Brief Sections 1–5: Complete Problem Statement, Target User, Solution Description, How It Works (pseudocode - written in English numbered steps BEFORE typing code), English/Math Connection. You approves How It Works pseudocode: "Can I understand what this does without seeing the code? If not, rewrite."
8. Second 30 min - Circuit Build: select components from tray. Justify each selection on the Design Brief component list: "I need the TMP36 because my project classifies temperature environments." Wire the circuit. Photograph or sketch the wiring diagram in the Design Brief.
9. You coaching: circulate using ONLY the Socratic coaching card questions. Do not give code. Do not suggest a solution. Ask: "What does this component do? How will you know it works? Where will the math connection appear in your output?"
10. First sketch: write on paper first (pseudocode), then type. No uploading until the pseudocode has been signed off by the you.
