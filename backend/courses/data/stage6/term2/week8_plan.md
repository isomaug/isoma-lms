# Term 2 · Lesson 8: Demo Day - AI Engineer Showcase & Certificate Award

**Duration:** 3 hours (Term-time Saturday) (split across Week 8 Lesson 1 start + Lesson 2 finish)
**Term / Week:** Term 2 · Week 8
**Age Range:** 10–12 years
**Hardware:** Each learner's complete Term 2 project (built and tested in Week 7). Arduino Uno, USB cable, full project circuit, sketch loaded and verified before session
**Software:** Arduino IDE 2.x - project sketch. Serial Monitor if project outputs data during presentation
**Bloom's:** L3–L6 Full Summative Range
**DDD Phase:** EVALUATE & SHOWCASE - Learners deliver a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel, demonstrate a live working prototype, answer panel questions, and receive summative scores across all 6 DDD assessment components
**NCDC:** Uganda NCDC Curriculum P5/P6 - Literacy Strand: Formal oral presentation with structured argument - problem framing, solution explanation, evidence presentation, and critical reflection. Numeracy Strand: Quantifying project outcomes - presenting measurements, calculations, or statistical analysis as evidence during the presentation

**Big Idea:** ILO 3.4 (summative completion) - Design, build, test, iterate, and present an original Arduino project integrating coding, English literacy, and Mathematics, supported by a written design brief and a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel. All four ILOs (3.1–3.4) are assessed holistically in the Demo Day rubric.

**Source plan:** `Stage6_T2_Week8_Demo_Day.docx`

## Learning Objectives

- Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation following the exact structure: Problem (framing the need) → Solution (describing the design) → Live Demo (prototype runs during presentation) → Evidence (mathematical element displayed and explained) → Reflection (what was hard, what was learned, what comes next)
- Demonstrate a live Arduino prototype that functions correctly during the presentation - responds to input, produces correct output, incorporates THREE Term 2 concepts
- Present at least one quantified mathematical element as visual evidence - shown on Serial Monitor, a hand-drawn graph, or a printed data table - and explain its significance to the audience
- Explain one coding concept used in the project in plain English to the panel, without using the code on screen as a crutch
- Answer a minimum of two panel questions - one technical, one about design decisions - with specific, reasoned responses
- Receive and acknowledge peer feedback from the audience rubric without deflection

## Lesson Structure

1. Learners arrive with projects built and tested. First 20 min: connect hardware, upload sketch, run one complete test. Any last-minute bug is a bug fix only - no new features. If a sketch does not compile, learner presents with their best working version and explains the issue in the Reflection section.
2. Facilitator: configure the room - presenter position at front, panel (facilitator + invited assessor) seated to one side facing presenter, audience in a semicircle. Place timer where presenter can see it.
3. Final 10 min: each learner reviews their Design Brief and mentally rehearses the 5 presentation elements. Say the opening sentence aloud to a partner: "The problem I am solving is…" If it is unclear, revise now.
4. Panel briefing: invited assessor receives DDD Part 8.6 rubric, a blank Weighted Score Sheet, and a 3-minute briefing on the scoring criteria. Confirm: scores are given independently; moderation happens after all presentations; scores are never announced publicly.
5. Facilitator confirms presentation order. Any learner who is visibly anxious may present in a position of their choice - facilitator accommodates without drawing attention.
### 2. PRESENTATIONS Formal Demo Day
6. Each learner presents individually for 5 minutes. Pair partner supports hardware during the live demo phase - operates buttons or adjusts physical conditions on request - but does not speak.
7. Presentation structure (strictly enforced by timer): Problem (60 sec) → Solution (60 sec) → Live Demo (90 sec - prototype must run) → Evidence (60 sec - show the number, explain it) → Reflection (30 sec - what was hard, what next).
8. Panel questions (2 minutes after each presentation): facilitator asks one technical question - drawn from: "Why did you choose this component?", "Explain what this function returns.", "What does this number mean?". Invited assessor asks one design question: "What would you change if you had more time?", "How does this connect to AI?", "What happens if the sensor reading is 0?"
9. Audience: during each presentation, audience completes the Audience Peer Feedback Form for the presenter (3 criteria + one question they would ask the presenter). Forms collected after each presentation and given to the presenter immediately.
10. Between presentations: 2-minute transition. Facilitator and assessor mark scores independently on their separate rubric sheets. Do not discuss scores during transitions.
11. Facilitator log: note participation quality of audience members - are they engaged? Are their peer feedback forms specific? This contributes to Component 1 (Weekly Participation 10%).
12. 3. MODERATION & SCORING
13. Facilitator and assessor compare rubric scores privately. For any criterion where scores differ by more than 1 point (on a 4-point scale), discuss briefly and agree a moderated score.
14. Complete the Weighted Score Sheet for every learner: (Participation score / 3) × 10 + (Artifact average / 5) × 25 + (Demo Day prototype / 12) × 30 + (Presentation / 16) × 20 + (Design Brief / 10) × 10 + (Peer Review / 5) × 5. Total must equal 100%.
15. Apply DDD Part 9.2 minimum requirements check: ≥55% total AND ≥2/4 on all 5 rubric criteria AND ≥5 of 7 artifacts submitted AND ≥6 of 8 sessions attended. All four conditions must be met.
16. Learners scoring 45–54% (Near Pass): offered one resubmission of the Demo Day presentation within 2 weeks. Facilitator informs privately after certificates are awarded to passing learners.
17. Learners scoring 85%+ (Distinction): noted for Isoma Edutech Honour Roll and eligible to mentor peers in the next cohort.
18. 4. CELEBRATION & CLOSING
19. Certificate Presentation: each learner called individually, thanked for their effort, presented with their AI Engineer Certificate (Term 2). Photo opportunity with written parental consent only.
20. Facilitator reads 3–4 highlights from across the cohort - specific achievements, no comparisons: "This term's cohort demonstrated modular code design, dual-sensor data analysis, and rule-based AI classification. I was particularly struck by the quality of the written critiques in Week 5 and the ambition of the projects in Week 7."
21. Learner reflection (individual, written, 5 minutes): complete three sentences - "The most significant thing I built this term was… The concept I understand most deeply now is… If I continued to Stage 7, I would want to explore…"
22. Gallery moment: all projects displayed simultaneously. Each learner leaves one sticky note on a project that impressed them - note must be specific: "I was impressed by how you used a rolling mean to filter sensor noise because…" Vague notes ("nice project") are returned and rewritten.
23. Programme pathway: Term 2 AI Engineer Certificate holders are eligible for the advanced Stage 7 Arduino + Python pathway. Facilitator distributes the Stage 7 prospectus.
24. Facilitator completes ALL Assessment Record Sheets. No certificate may be signed without a completed, signed record. All artifacts, rubrics, and score sheets filed in learner portfolios and retained for moderation.

Mathematics Thread
Formal oral presentation with structured argument - the most demanding communication task of the entire programme. Learners prepare a written introduction (3–4 sentences) and written conclusion (3–4 sentences) to read from notes. The body of the presentation is spoken from memory - not read. The Evidence section requires learners to point to a number and explain what it means to a non-specialist: "This graph shows that the mean temperature across 20 readings was 28.3°C. This is significant because it is above the threshold I set at 27°C, which means the alert condition was triggered in 12 of the 20 readings - that is 60% of the time." This is precise technical oral communication - a skill that transfers directly to secondary school, university, and professional life.
Quantifying project outcomes with at least one of: (1) mean and range from a sensor dataset with percentage of readings above/below threshold; (2) arithmetic sequence produced by a for loop - first term, common difference, nth term stated; (3) classifier accuracy as a percentage with worked calculation shown. The mathematical evidence MUST be displayed visually during the presentation - Serial Monitor values, a hand-drawn graph, or a data table held up for the panel. "Show the number. Say what it means. Explain why it matters." Weighted score calculation: facilitators complete the DDD Part 9.3 formula table for every learner during the moderation phase.
