# Week 6 - Lesson 2

## Session length
**About half of this week's plan** (finish what you started in Lesson 1)

## Finish the week
- Define the terms: artificial intelligence, machine learning, training data, pattern recognition, classification, prediction
- Sort 20 picture cards into categories using a self-chosen rule, explaining the rule in plain English — mirroring how a classification AI works
- Read a provided article about a real-world AI application and write a summary of minimum 150 words in own words
- Identify one benefit and one concern about the AI application described in the article
- Connect at least one AI concept (pattern recognition, classification, or rule-based decision) to a coding project from Weeks 1–5
- Calculate the probability that a randomly chosen card belongs to a given category, expressed as a fraction and percentage

## Continue

### 2. GUIDED ACTIVITY Read, Discuss & Classify
1. Step 5 (10 min): Pair share — swap frames with partner. Partner reads and gives one piece of feedback: "I think you should add more detail about…"
### 3. INDEPENDENT / PAIR WORK Write, Connect & Debate
2. Task 1 (25 min): Complete the Summary Writing Frame — all 5 sections. Minimum total 150 words. You circulates: check that you are not copying from the article. Prompt: "Say it in your own words."
3. Task 2 (15 min): Complete the AI Concept Connection Card. Three prompts: (1) Which AI concept connects to your Week 5 LDR sensor work? (e.g. the sensor collecting training data) (2) Which connects to Week 4 sorting algorithm? (e.g. classification) (3) If you were going to train an AI to do one thing in Uganda, what would it be and why?
4. Task 3 — Class Debate (10 min): You poses question: "Should AI be allowed to make decisions about people's lives?" You choose a side and give one reason. Record contributions on the board — this forms the participation log entry.
5. Extension: Write a simple rule-based AI in Arduino pseudocode — 5 if/else rules that classify a sensor reading as one of 3 categories. No coding needed — pseudocode only.
### 4. SHOWCASE & DEBRIEF
6. 2 you read their "What the AI does" paragraph aloud. Class checks: Is it in their own words? Does it explain clearly?
7. Probability check: one pair shares their fraction and percentage. Class verifies calculation.
8. Ask yourself: "What is the difference between a computer following an if/else rule and an AI learning from data?" Allow 3 responses — this is a key analytical question from ILO 3.3.
9. AI Connection review: you reads out 2 AI Concept Connection Card answers. Discuss: "Is that a fair connection? Why?"
10. English close: "The best summaries showed the writer understood the article — they explained it differently from the original. Copying is not understanding."
11. Collect Summary Writing Frames and AI Concept Connection Cards as this week's artifact (the written summary).

## Stay safe
AI discussion must remain respectful and age-appropriate. If you raise facial recognition or surveillance examples, acknowledge them thoughtfully. Do not use real names of classmates in classification examples. This lesson connects to digital citizenship — frame AI as a tool made by people, with human responsibilities.
