Stage 5–6 Summative Assessment & Certificate Presentation
LEARNING OBJECTIVES
• Deliver a 5-minute formal presentation to a panel (facilitator + invited assessor) following the structure: Problem → Solution → Live Demo → Evidence → Reflection
• Demonstrate a live, working Arduino prototype that responds to input and produces correct output during the presentation
• Explain at least one coding concept used (loop, condition, variable, function) in plain English to a non-technical audience
• Present at least one quantified mathematical outcome — a measurement, calculation, or data point from the project — displayed on a graph or in a data table
• Read a written introduction and conclusion from prepared notes
• Answer a minimum of 2 questions from the panel about the project design, coding decisions, or mathematical elements
MATERIALS & PREPARATION
• Each learner's completed project: Arduino Uno + circuit + fully tested sketch loaded
• Each learner's completed Design Brief (Week 7 artifact — must be submitted before presenting)
• Printed "Demo Day Assessment Rubric" × 2 per learner — one for facilitator, one for invited assessor (from DDD Part 8.6)
• Printed "Peer Feedback Rubric" × 8 — each audience member completes one per presenter (from DDD Part 8.6)
• Printed "AI Engineer Certificate" × 8 — to be signed and awarded at the end of the session
• Presentation order list (prepared by facilitator beforehand — randomised)
• Timer visible to presenter and panel
• Optional: small refreshments to mark the celebration — this is a milestone achievement

Intended Learning Outcome (ILO)
Big Idea
ILO 3.4 (summative) — Design, build, test, iterate, and present an original Arduino project integrating coding, English literacy, and Mathematics, supported by a written design brief and a 5-minute formal presentation.
Engineers do not just build — they communicate. A project that works but cannot be explained is not yet complete. Today you present your full journey: the problem, the solution, the evidence, and what you would do next.
English Literacy Thread
Mathematics Thread
Formal oral presentation with a structured argument: Problem → Solution → Live Demo → Evidence → Reflection. Learners prepare a written introduction (3–4 sentences) and written conclusion (3–4 sentences) to read from notes — this is not a script for the whole presentation, but an anchor for the opening and closing. Vocabulary expectation: learners must use at least 4 computing vocabulary terms correctly during the presentation. Panel questions assess communication clarity — can the learner explain a technical concept in plain English to someone who is not a coder?
Quantify project outcomes with at least one of: (1) a sensor measurement with mean and range calculated (from Week 5 data skills); (2) an arithmetic sequence produced by the project's loop, with first term and common difference identified; (3) a geometry or measurement calculation used in the physical build. The mathematical evidence must be displayed visually during the presentation — on a hand-drawn graph, a data table, or written on a card held up during the demo. "Show the number. Say what it means."

Activities & Facilitator Actions
1. PREPARATION Final Polish & Set-Up
30 min
• Learners arrive with projects already built and tested at home or in a pre-session setup window.
• First 20 min: each pair connects hardware, uploads sketch, and runs a final test. Facilitator circulates — any last-minute bugs must be resolved before presentations begin. No new features — bug fixes only.
• Final 10 min: learners review their Design Brief, check they can answer: "What problem does it solve? What coding concept did you use? What is the math connection?" Practise the opening sentence aloud to partner.
• Facilitator confirms presentation order. Remind panel of rubric criteria. Invited assessor receives a copy of the assessment rubric and a 2-minute briefing on the marking criteria.
• Set room: presenter position at the front, panel seated facing the presenter, audience (other learners) in a semicircle. One laptop visible for Serial Monitor output if relevant.
2. PRESENTATIONS Formal Demo Day
70 min
• Each learner presents individually for 5 minutes. Partner supports hardware during the live demo portion but does not speak.
• Presentation structure (enforced): (1) Introduction — read from notes: name, project title, problem addressed. (2) Solution — explain how the project works in plain English. (3) Live Demo — run the project. Narrate what is happening. (4) Evidence — show the mathematical element: graph, data table, or calculation. (5) Reflection — what was hard? What would you improve?
• Panel questions (2 minutes after each presentation): facilitator asks 1 question; invited assessor asks 1 question. Questions are drawn from: "Why did you choose this component?", "Explain what this line of code does.", "What does your data tell you?", "How did you decide on this design?"
• Audience: each learner in the audience completes one Peer Feedback Rubric for the presenter. Forms collected after each presentation.
• Facilitator and assessor score independently on the Demo Day Assessment Rubric during the presentation. Moderate if scores differ by more than 10%.
• Between presentations: 2-minute transition time. Next presenter sets up hardware.
3. SCORING & CERTIFICATE AWARD
30 min
• Facilitator and assessor moderate scores privately. Calculate weighted total score using DDD Part 9 formula.
• All learners who meet the pass threshold (≥55% weighted total across all 6 assessment components, Weeks 1–8) receive the AI Engineer Certificate.
• Learners who score 85%+ are noted for the Isoma Edutech Honour Roll and encouraged to mentor peers in the next cohort.
• Near Pass (45–54%): learner is offered one resubmission of the Demo Day project within 2 weeks. Facilitator explains this privately — not in front of peers.
• Certificate Presentation: each learner is called individually, thanked for their effort, and presented with their certificate. Photo opportunity (with consent).
• Close: facilitator reads out 2–3 highlights from across the presentations — specific achievements, not comparisons between learners. "This cohort demonstrated… I was particularly impressed by the way [concept] was applied across multiple projects."
4. CELEBRATION & CLOSING REFLECTION
30 min
• Learner reflection (individual, written): "Complete these 3 sentences: The most important thing I learned in this programme was… The skill I am most proud of is… One thing I want to learn next is…"
• Gallery moment: all projects displayed simultaneously. Learners walk around and leave one sticky note on a project they admired — must be specific: "I liked how you used a for loop to…" not just "good job".
• Facilitator shares next stage pathway: Stage 5–6 completers who earn the AI Engineer Certificate are eligible to enrol in the next cohort as Peer Mentors.
• Programme Director (if present) addresses the cohort.
• Facilitator completes Assessment Record Sheets for all learners. No certificate may be signed without a completed, signed record on file.
• All Design Briefs, peer review rubrics, and Score Sheets filed in learner portfolios. Retained for moderation.

Quality and constructiveness of written peer feedback provided to a classmate. Scored on specificity, evidence-based comments, and constructive tone. Not graded on what score was given.
Peer Feedback Rubric forms collected for all 8 learners.
