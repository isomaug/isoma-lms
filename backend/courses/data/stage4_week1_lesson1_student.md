# Week 1 - Lesson 1

## Session length
**About 30 minutes** (you will finish this week in Lesson 2)

## Today you will learn
- How to unbox and connect a BBC Micro:bit v2 to a laptop with a USB cable
- How to open Microsoft MakeCode in a browser and find the block editor
- How to write and download a programme that scrolls your name on the LED display
- How to programme Button A for a happy face and Button B for a sad face
- What the words **input** and **output** mean, using examples from this lesson

## Big idea
Every digital device has inputs and outputs. A Micro:bit is a tiny computer. It reads inputs (buttons, sensors) and makes outputs (LED lights, sound).

## What you need
- 1 BBC Micro:bit v2 and a USB cable
- A laptop or computer with internet
- MakeCode open at [makecode.microbit.org](https://makecode.microbit.org)
- Your Micro:bit interface map
- Vocabulary cards: algorithm, input, output, variable, debug
- Your 5×5 grid worksheet

## Links to English and Maths
- **English:** Learn the words input, output, and algorithm. Match each word to an example you see today. Try to use each word in a sentence.
- **Maths:** Explore the 5×5 LED grid. Count all 25 lights. Shade your first initial. Think about coordinates (x, y).

## Let's begin

### 1. Meet the Micro:bit
Look at a Micro:bit that is already scrolling a name (or watch a demo video if you have one).

Ask yourself:
- What is this device?
- What do you think it can do?

### 2. Input or output?
For each action, decide: **INPUT** or **OUTPUT**?
- Pressing a button
- Lighting an LED
- Detecting temperature

### 3. Vocabulary cards
Match each word to an example:
- algorithm
- input
- output
- variable
- debug

### 4. Find MakeCode on your map
Open MakeCode. On your interface map, find:
- block editor
- simulator
- download button
- device name

### 5. Plan before you code
On your 5×5 grid worksheet, shade the squares for the letter of your first initial.

Count the shaded squares.

Remember: your shaded grid is your **algorithm** — a plan before you code.

Write one sentence:
> When I press Button A, the Micro:bit will ______. When I press Button B, it will ______.

Do not open the coding screen until your plan is written.

### 6. Connect and open MakeCode
1. Connect the Micro:bit with the USB cable.
2. Open [makecode.microbit.org](https://makecode.microbit.org) (or the offline MakeCode app).
3. Use your interface map to find each part of the screen.

### 7. Step 1 — Scroll your name (about 5 minutes)
1. Start a new project.
2. In the simulator, drag **on start** → **show string**.
3. Type your first name (or a fictional name — not your surname, phone number, or home address).
4. Download to the Micro:bit.
5. Check: does your name scroll on the LED?

### 8. Step 2 — Button faces (about 8 minutes)
1. Add **on button A pressed** → **show icon** (happy face).
2. Add **on button B pressed** → **show icon** (sad face).
3. Download and test both buttons.

## Save your progress
Keep your Micro:bit connected if you are continuing soon. In Lesson 2 you will finish, explain your blocks, and check your learning.
